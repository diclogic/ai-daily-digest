# AI Daily Digest — November 25, 2026

**Top story: in two days, two frontier labs each posted a claimed closure of a decades-old complexity-theory problem — and neither has been checked by a human.** On **November 23** Anthropic published a 70-page paper credited to **Claude as sole author** claiming **VP ≠ VNP over every field**, with a Lean 4 formalization; on **November 24** OpenAI posted a preprint claiming the **matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4**, upper *and* matching lower bound. Both ship machine-checked artifacts, and both repositories say in their own words that no human mathematician has verified that the formal statements say what the papers claim. The counterpoint landed the same day: **Axios**, citing more than two dozen insiders, reported that employees at **both** OpenAI and Anthropic increasingly doubt their *unreleased* models can be safely released at all — and Anthropic's stock closed **below its $83 IPO price** for the first time.

## Notable Papers & Research

### OpenAI: the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

**"The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths"** — OpenAI, posted **November 24, 2026** in the public [openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math) repository ([preprint directory](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026)). Read firsthand: the paper claims **ω_F = 9/4 over every field**, pairing the upper bound with a lower bound stating that at every side length *n ≥ 2*, at least **⌈n^(9/4)/2⌉** nonscalar multiplication-or-division gates are required, plus a geometric border-rank bound of at least n^(9/4). An earlier OpenAI preprint on **October 2** had given only the upper bound (2.25, over the complex numbers).

**Why it matters:** fifty-seven years after Strassen, ω has been bounded from above and never pinned. A *matching lower bound* is a different kind of claim from a faster algorithm — it says the problem has a floor, which no amount of cleverness gets under. **Two caveats are load-bearing and both come from OpenAI's own repository.** First, the scope gap: the Lean development proves the 9/4 exponent only for **division-free circuits computing formal polynomial identities**, while the abstract's lower bound explicitly *counts division gates* — the formalized result is narrower than the stated one. Second, the repo-level README states that only about **42%** of top-line results in the collection are formalized in Lean, that results sit at "different stages of verification," and that unformalized results "could have issues." There is **no peer review and no independent confirmation**, and no near-term consequence for GPU matmul. Independent expositions of the ≤ 9/4 *upper* bound have appeared ([ECCC 2026/281](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/281/download/), [arXiv:2611.09716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09716)), but those address the upper bound, not the new lower bound. ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/culture/openai-exact-2-25-matrix-multiplication-exponent), [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-claims-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-exactly-94))

### Anthropic: a paper credited to Claude claims VP ≠ VNP

**"VP ≠ VNP over every field"** — author listed as **Claude (Anthropic)**, committed **November 23, 2026** to [anthropics/formal-math](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math) ([`vp-vnp/`](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/vp-vnp), single commit `918c8e2`, "vp-vnp: VP != VNP, paper and Lean proof"). Read firsthand: the README claims that over every field, circuits for the **Hamiltonian cycle polynomial** — and for the **permanent outside characteristic 2** — need more than **m^(c(log m)^(1/3))** gates for all large *m*, with the same holding for approximating circuits; therefore VP ⊊ VNP, and VNP is not contained in the closure of VP. Secondary coverage puts the Lean formalization at roughly **73,000 lines across 324 files**; that figure is from reporting, not counted here.

**What the repository itself says, verbatim in substance:** the result was discovered by an **internal research Claude model**; the paper and the Lean formalization were **both written by AI models**; and **no human mathematician has checked the proof**. The repo is explicitly "a research artifact," unmaintained and not accepting contributions. The README is also careful about the limit of machine checking: `lake build` plus a second kernel (nanoda) confirms the *proofs* match the *Lean statements*, but **nothing machine-checks that `Statement.lean` says what the paper claims** — Appendix Q maps Lean counterparts to numbered statements and notes where they differ. The README states the result **does not settle P vs NP**. Early expert reaction is thin and single-source: one report has Lance Fortnow reading it as sidestepping Geometric Complexity Theory. ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/claude-proves-vp-not-vnp-valiant-conjecture), [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/claude-claims-proof-of-valiants-conjecture-vp-vs-vnp), [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-claude-written-paper-claims-vp-vnp-with-a-lean-proof/)) **Treat the "73,000 lines / 324 files" and the Fortnow reading as unverified here.** Related and worth noting from the same repo: an `eth/` project, also credited to Claude, whose README claims a result **refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis** — an extraordinary claim carrying the same verification caveat.

### The counterweight: LLMs have not yet changed how science is done

**"Large Language Models Have Not Yet Transformed Science"** — Ron Bekkerman, Nichole Li, Bahareh Kargar, Mark Somers, [arXiv:2611.22975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22975) (cs.CL/cs.CY, submitted **November 17**). A bibliometric study of roughly **2.5 million full-text papers, 2015–2025**, across Computer Science, Mathematics, Physics, Health Sciences and Life Sciences. The finding is the absence of a finding: most pre-2023 trends continue without a visible break. Two exceptions are named — **computer-science coauthor teams in Asia grew significantly faster**, and an apparent decline in biomedical topic diversity is attributed mostly to **standardized article formats** rather than a real narrowing of research topics. The authors frame the work as a baseline against which later adoption effects can be measured.

**Why it belongs next to the two items above:** on the same week two labs claim machine-generated closure of named open problems, the best available population-scale measurement says the aggregate practice of science has not yet moved. Both can be true — spectacular tails, flat mean — and anyone citing one should know the other exists. *Caveat:* read here via abstract and aggregator pages only; the full methodology was not checked.

## Model & Product Launches

### Claude Ship: Anthropic hands pull-request approval to agents

**Announced November 23**, widely covered through **November 25**. Three GitHub agents: **Code Review** (generally available), **PR Steward** (beta) and **Claude Approvals** (beta). Claude Approvals reads a **natural-language policy file committed to the repo** and can combine signals — Code Review findings, Anthropic's own classifiers — so routine changes merge automatically while sensitive paths require a human. **PR Steward** works failing CI checks, merge conflicts and review feedback, and is reportedly designed **never to approve or merge anything**. Teams pick review depth (Quick / Standard / Deep); Anthropic suggests starting in **observe-only** mode. A `/ship` command is rolling out in Claude Code and the desktop app. Available to **Claude Team and Enterprise** orgs on GitHub.

**Why it matters, and where the coverage is sloppier than the product:** the novel piece is not AI review — it is **AI holding the approval bit**, with the policy that governs it written in prose and version-controlled next to the code. Several outlets describe agents that "merge code with no human involved"; Anthropic's framing is policy-bounded approval with humans as named escalation points, and the gap between those two readings is the whole story. **Billing has a trap worth knowing:** Claude Approvals has no base charge, but its classifiers are token-billed, and a policy that leans on Claude's review **triggers a billed Code Review even in observe-only mode** — the free trial mode is not free. One report says PR Steward has **no spend cap until an Owner sets one**. Eligible Enterprise orgs can claim **$10,000+ in Claude Ship credits**, usable within 30 days. **Anthropic's evidence is its own:** the Claude Code team's merged PRs "more than doubled" June→September while reverted changes fell "more than 40%" — internal figures, one team, no external replication. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-launches-claude-ship-letting-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-code-changes/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-claude-ship-lets-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-pull-requests-on-their-own/), [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-claude-ship-pull-request-agents/)) *Anthropic's own announcement page was not reachable from this session (see method note); all detail above is from secondary coverage.*

### Reserved slot: Epochal gives agents a dated internet *(a company not covered elsewhere in this digest)*

**Launched November 24** by three **xAI founding members** — **Guodong Zhang, Zihang Dai, Manuel Kroiss** (Alex Bocharoff and Frank Luan also listed) — with **$100 million** that Bloomberg reports is **Zhang's own money**, no outside investors disclosed. The first product, the **Epochal Time Portal**, exposes **search, fetch and sandbox APIs that return the web as it stood at any past instant**, drawing on a reported **1 trillion-plus indexed pages across 30+ years**. The sandbox sets its **clock, installed software and downloads to the same date**. A limited free tier is open to developers.

**Why it earns the slot:** every forecasting evaluation of an agent with web access is contaminated — the model can read what happened. Epochal sells the *absence* of hindsight as infrastructure, which is a genuinely different product from a search API, and the sandbox detail (pinning the toolchain, not just the index) shows they understand where leakage actually comes from. **Two serious caveats.** The company **has not said where the archive came from or whether the pages are licensed**, which for a trillion-page corpus is the entire legal question. And the headline evaluation — 347 live Kalshi and Polymarket questions replayed weeks later, reported as **0.98 correlation with outcomes for Time Portal agents vs. 0.16 live-web and 0.49 no-web** — appears in **exactly one outlet**, and a 0.98 is not a plausible forecasting correlation on real prediction-market questions. **Do not repeat those numbers as findings.** ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/epochal-xai-cofounders-time-portal-forecasting), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/24/epochal-launches-with-100m-to-help-developers-train-predictive-ai-models), [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/three-xai-co-founders-launch-epochal-with-100m-from-zhang), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/xai-founders-unveil-new-ai-startup-to-predict-future-events))

### UK NCSC: a "marked rise" in AI-agent attacks since September, exploiting old flaws in minutes

**November 24**, in an NCSC blog post with three officials on record. Attackers are using **agentic offensive frameworks** — red-teaming software that lets a model plan and run attacks with ordinary security tools — with **ARTEX** named as the framework most associated with the activity, alongside **Cairn, PentAGI and RedAmon**. Victim impact ranged from **outages to full system compromise**. The NCSC is explicit that **the vulnerabilities are not new**: they are "unmitigated weaknesses that are often the result of years of technical debt." **What changed is speed** — an agent goes from discovery to exploitation in minutes, tries every endpoint, parameter and credential it finds, and learns from every error message. The guidance is deliberately unglamorous: AI **"does not make established cyber security controls obsolete,"** and "building strong foundations has never mattered more," because AI-driven *defence* will take time to mature and organisations cannot wait for it. Context: an **FBI flash alert on ARTEX on Nov 13**, and a **CrowdStrike** case in which an agent found and exploited a SQL injection at an Argentine healthcare organisation **within 90 minutes**. **What's left has a sharper theme than yesterday's: the agent-tooling boom is now trying to make frontier behaviour reproducible without a frontier model** — by distilling it, by routing around it, or by hand-writing it.

**1. [huggingface/pybot](https://github.com/huggingface/pybot)** — *a chatbot with no model weights, written in pure Python by a swarm of over 1,000 agents.* **Apache-2.0**, **~1.2k stars** (created after Nov 10), **134 forks**, **21 commits** on main, **7 open issues**, **7 open PRs**, no releases. Behaviour comes entirely from **if-else branches, regex patterns and lookup tables**. The architecture is legible: each turn, every module in `bot/modules/` proposes a reply and an **arbiter** picks the one the user sees, over four shared components — an **understanding engine** (~25,000 lines, handling typos, chat-speak, topic and pronoun reference), a **memory store** (~109,000 lines), a **knowledge base** (~317,000 lines of world facts as data) and a **response composer** (~104,000 lines). The `bot/` directory is **528 Python files, ~640,000 lines**. Runs on **Python 3.8+ with no packages and no model download**; `python -m pybot` for interactive chat, `-v` to see which module produced a reply.

**Why it's trending, and why it's more than a joke:** the README starts from **ELIZA** and asks the honest version of the question — ELIZA's ceiling was that expanding it meant writing rules by hand, so what happens when agents do that work at a scale no human team would attempt? The answer is a number: on **SmolTalkBench** (LLM judge comparing each reply to a reference), the agents' **5,000+ versions** moved the win rate from **0% to 26%** — which the README places **on par with SmolLM-360M-Instruct**. Six hundred forty thousand lines of hand-rolled rules to match a 360M-parameter model is simultaneously an impressive engineering result and the most precise statement of what weights buy you that anyone published this week. **Caveats, including one the README leaves dangling:** 26% means it still **loses most comparisons**; startup takes seconds because 640k lines must load; replies take about a second; and the prose claims the project is "backed by 550k lines of tests the agents wrote themselves" while the repository table describes `tests/` as covering **only the command line** — the README does not reconcile those, and the smaller number is the one the repo structure supports.

**2. [sybil-solutions/skill-distill](https://github.com/sybil-solutions/skill-distill)** — *take a session where a frontier model did a task well, and grind out a skill that lets an open-weight model reproduce it.* **MIT**, **743 stars** (created after Nov 10), **60 forks**, **2 commits** on main. The frontier agent is the **teacher**; the **student** is a harness plus an open model (e.g. omp with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash). The loop: read the session and write a skill, run the student **in tmux on a fresh copy of the starting state** with the original prompt plus the skill, diff against the reference, **find the first point where the student went wrong and fix the skill there**, rerun **three trials per version** until it matches. A human makes the final call — the teacher shows reference and best student output side by side, and the skill is done only when the user accepts it. Ships `scripts/distill.py` (run bookkeeping: init, trial, status, evidence, verdict, ledger, report, accept), `extract_session.py` (Claude Code / Codex / omp logs → readable references), `capture_web.py` (headless Chromium screenshots and errors), and adapters for omp, pi, Codex, Claude Code and opencode.

**Why it's here:** the anti-cheating rules are the actual contribution. **The skill may not contain the reference's finished output, or a script that writes it** — short snippets only where needed; **checks must pass on the reference first**, and a check that fails there is treated as broken rather than as a student failure; the student gets the reference prompt **verbatim**, a clean state, and the skill, with **harness global context and other skills disabled**. That is a tighter protocol than most published distillation work, written for a repo with two commits. **Caveats, and they are large:** the README reports **no benchmark numbers, success rates or comparative measurements at all** — its only evidence is an unquantified `examples/` folder. **Only the omp adapter is marked tested**; Codex is tested as teacher but needs a provider as student; **pi and opencode are written but untested**, and opencode's logs cannot be parsed yet. Open models in Claude Code need `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` pointed at an Anthropic-compatible server. Raw session logs stay in a git-ignored `runs/` folder because they carry local paths.

**3. [INK-Q/agy-fast](https://github.com/INK-Q/agy-fast)** — *batch the agent's small operations so it burns fewer turns, fewer tokens and less upload.* **MIT**, **1.3k stars** (created after Nov 10), **75 forks**, **119 commits**, **8 open issues**, **25 open PRs**. A zero-dependency local **MCP plugin for Google Antigravity** (CLI, Desktop 2.0, IDE) exposing five composite tools: **`batch_search`** (up to 20 mixed text/regex/glob/fuzzy/read-only-git queries), **`apply_edits`** (up to 50 files per call, atomic or best-effort, optional inline verification), **`quick_outline`** (symbol outlines for up to 40 files, ~15 language formats), **`read_slices`**, and **`run_checks`** (up to 12 build/lint/test commands concurrently, output folded to an 8.8 KB cap). A **`SessionStart` hook** injects a ≤1.5 KB workspace snapshot once per session; native UI, diff viewer and checkpoints are untouched.

**Why it's here:** the README opens with a measurement rather than a pitch — **55 sessions, 8,446 LLM turns, 17,302 tool calls** — and names four bottlenecks: median **~6.1s time-to-first-token** per turn, serial fragmented operations, **49.2s** average for the first three turns, and **`read_file` accounting for 81.9% of all tool-returned bytes**. That last figure is the whole design rationale, and it is the kind of number almost nobody in this category publishes. Reported effects: **−33.7%** LLM turns per 10 actions in main sessions (**−38.2%** at ≥150k context, **−58.8%** in subagents), **−31.5%** estimated billable quota, **−39.7%** tool-return upload per action, prompt-cache hit rate **89.6% → 94.0%**, turns stalling over 60s **31.2% → 7.4%**, throughput **35.1 → 61.5 tok/s**. **Caveats:** all self-reported from the author's own sanitized transcripts, with a **subagent sample of four sessions**; quota figures are weighted estimates and the README honestly reports the weaker variants too (**−25.8%** under a conservative cache discount, **−29.4%** under high output weighting); fixed overhead is ~**1.7k tokens per session**; `apply_edits` format depends on the Antigravity CLI version; the README suggests **`--dangerously-skip-permissions`** for auto-approval, which is a real footgun even hedged with "trusted repositories only"; and a background updater pings GitHub release tags every 4 hours unless disabled.

**4. [mankidev/antialgorithm](https://github.com/mankidev/antialgorithm)** — *an agent skill whose entire job is to stop the model giving you its most average answer.* **MIT**, **839 stars** (created after Nov 10), **53 forks**, **19 commits**. For names, stories, game ideas, posters and UI: the model **names its own default out loud**, then moves to a different idea **for a reason taken from the project**. Four scripts: **`seed.py`** rolls a starting point when a request is vague (visual seed: method, colors, layout, type, scale; text seed: voice, angle, register; idea seed: verb, constraint, scale, pressure) using **system randomness**, logging history so recent values aren't repeated, with `--keep "axis=value"` to pin one axis and re-roll the rest; **`moves.py`** logs the main move behind each non-default choice **so the model doesn't reuse a move just because it worked last time**; **`rough.py`** adds irregularity to SVG lines, shapes and spacing for hand-made styles; **`check.py`** renders an SVG at full and icon size and flags text running off an edge, sitting under a shape, low-contrast or too small.

**Why it's here:** "make the output less generic" is the most common complaint about generative tools and the least operationalized. This decomposes it into a **named default + a logged reason for departing + a non-repetition ledger**, which is a mechanism rather than a prompt. **Caveats, one of which the README states against its own marketing:** the showcase's bottom row of six posters was **hand-picked from about a dozen development runs** while the top row is one unfiltered batch — the author says so, and it means the comparison is **not a representative sample**. There are **no quantitative metrics of any kind**, and the "choices cluster around the average" chart is explicitly **an illustration, not measured data**. `rough.py` is only for hand-made methods; `check.py`'s text-size defaults are tuned for posters and need `--min-text 0.6` for app screens; `check.py` needs numpy, Pillow and rsvg-convert or cairosvg.

**Also worth a look:** **[Immortal-Protocols/lastbook](https://github.com/Immortal-Protocols/lastbook)** (**MIT** for original code, **3.1k stars**, 377 forks, 810 commits, 65 open issues / 120 open PRs) — an offline survival library for Mac, Windows and Linux combining manuals, videos, encyclopedias and offline maps, where the **AI is optional and entirely local**: **Gemma 4 26B A4B** (~17 GB), offered only on machines with enough memory, answering from the library **with citations to its own pages**, and falling back to plain page search when absent. **Opens no network port and sends no telemetry.** It is the clearest example this month of local models being chosen for *guaranteed availability* rather than cost or privacy. **Caveats:** the library content is **hundreds of gigabytes distributed separately** and not in the repo; Linux was tested only on **Ubuntu 24.04.5 under WSL2 with CPU-only AI**; the iPad app is **source-only**; meaning-based search (bge-small-en-v1.5) is **Windows-only** while Mac uses keyword search; the library server is adapted from a household setup and still shows it; and distributed bundles include **GPL components** (python-libzim, libzim) with their own obligations despite the MIT headline.
