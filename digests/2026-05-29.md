## Notable Papers & Research

**"Divergent Creativity in Humans and Large Language Models"** — Jerbi et al. (Université de Montréal, Concordia, U of T, Mila, Google DeepMind). A study of 100,000+ people found generative AI now outperforms the average human on divergent thinking tests, though the top 10% of humans still outperform all models tested. Published in *Scientific Reports*. ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/01/260125083356.htm))

**"A Matter of TASTE: Improving Coverage and Difficulty of Agent Benchmarks"** — Keren et al. (arXiv, May 29). Proposes a new framework for systematically evaluating agentic AI benchmarks, addressing gaps in coverage and difficulty calibration. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/current))

**"Refusal Before Decoding: Detecting and Exploiting Refusal Signals in Intermediate LLM Activations"** — Collu et al. (arXiv, May 29). Shows that LLM refusal behavior can be detected and manipulated in intermediate activations before output generation begins, raising important safety implications. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/current))

**JEPA World Models** — Klindt, LeCun & Balestriero. Formally proves conditions under which Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (LeJEPA) recover true latent variables, providing theoretical grounding for Yann LeCun's world-model approach. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/current))

## Model & Product Launches

**Claude Mythos nearing public release.** Anthropic confirmed its restricted Mythos model — which far exceeds Opus 4.7 in code reasoning and autonomous vulnerability discovery — is approaching general availability. Users briefly spotted `claude-mythos-1-preview` in Claude Code's UI. In controlled deployment, Mythos has uncovered 10,000+ high/critical-severity vulnerabilities across major open-source projects, with 1,596 disclosed and 97 patched as of May 22. No firm release date yet; Anthropic says it is finalizing safeguards. Leads GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.7 on multiple agentic benchmarks (MCP Atlas, CharXiv Reasoning). ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/google-ai-ultra-gemini-spark-omni.html) · [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/19/google-io-2026-news/))

Treat claims with caution — no independent benchmarks have been published yet. ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/miami-startup-subquadratic-claims-1-000x-ai-efficiency-gain-with-subq-model-researchers-demand-independent-proof))

## Key Opinions & Statements

**Sam Altman** acknowledged at a conference on May 26 that the "jobs apocalypse" he once predicted hasn't materialized: *"I'm delighted to be wrong about this — I thought there would have been more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs being eliminated by now than has actually happened."* This marks a notable reversal from his earlier warnings. ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/26/sam-altman-ai-job-losses-openAI-/) · [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/sam-altman-dario-amodei-walking-back-ai-jobs-apocalypse-prophecies-ipo/))

**Dario Amodei** similarly walked back job-loss predictions at a May 5 briefing with JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon, invoking the Jevons Paradox — automating 90% of a job could create more demand for the remaining 10%. He cautioned, however, that *"AI is moving faster than all these previous technologies"* and disruption remains possible. Both leaders' rhetorical shifts come as their companies eye IPOs. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/dario-amodei-jevons-paradox-will-ai-wipe-out-white-collar-jobs/))

**Demis Hassabis** declared at Google I/O that humanity stands in the *"foothills of the singularity"* and moved his AGI timeline from "five to ten years" to *"a real possibility by 2029."* He said the language was intentionally provocative to spur governments and economists to prepare. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/26/deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis) · [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/20/2026/google-exec-demis-hassabis-predicts-were-at-the-foothills-of-the-singularity))

**Yann LeCun** called CEO warnings about AI job loss *"extremely destructive,"* arguing the real danger is *"making life-altering decisions based on exaggerated claims."* Now heading AMI Labs after leaving Meta with a $1B raise, LeCun maintains AI will amplify human intelligence rather than replace it. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/05/ai-job-apocalypse-warnings-destructive-yann-lecun/) · [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/04/ai-godfather-survival-guide-hype-doom))

**Andrej Karpathy** joined Anthropic's pre-training team (announced May 19), where he'll lead a new effort using Claude itself to accelerate pretraining research. He said he remains *"deeply passionate about education"* and plans to return to that work *"in time."* Separately, his tweet highlighting that ChatGPT's voice mode runs on a much weaker GPT-4o-era model sparked developer backlash against OpenAI. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/19/openai-co-founder-andrej-karpathy-joins-anthropics-pre-training-team/) · [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/apr/10/voice-mode-is-weaker/))

**Ilya Sutskever** continues to lead Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), now valued at $32B after a $2B raise. SSI maintains its singular focus — no products until safe superintelligence is achieved. Sutskever received the 2026 National Academy of Sciences Award for Industrial Application of Science. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/12/openai-co-founder-ilya-sutskevers-safe-superintelligence-reportedly-valued-at-32b/))
