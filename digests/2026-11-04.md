# AI Daily Digest — November 4, 2026

**Top story: the hardware decoupling got explicit on both ends in the same 24 hours.** The **Financial Times** reported that Nvidia has begun telling cloud and data-centre customers, verbally and globally, that **new contracts require at least 51% of their service sales to come from outside China** — a rule Nvidia has not confirmed on the record. Hours later **Digitimes** reported from the other side of the same wall: **DeepSeek has ordered 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT chips** for a 1GW site in Inner Mongolia and intends to **pre-train V5 on them from scratch**, with deliveries ramping in Q4 2026. And **China's NDRC** said on November 3 that it will **accelerate comprehensive AI legislation** and set full-lifecycle safety standards, naming disclosure duties that would reach DeepSeek, Moonshot and Alibaba. Three separate stories, one structure: the compute supply chain is being partitioned by contract, substituted by necessity, and regulated on both sides at once.

## Notable Papers & Research

### From the November 3 arXiv listing
**Caveat first:** arXiv was unreachable this session. The five items below come from a third-party daily digest of the cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.LG listings for 2026-11-03 ([news-radar #968](https://github.com/datnguyenquy94/news-radar/issues/968)), read directly on GitHub. **Titles, IDs and the reported figures have not been checked against the papers themselves** — treat the quantitative claims as the digest's, not as verified results.

- **Controlling Sharpening in LLM Post-Training** ([arXiv:2611.03146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03146)) — a repeat-sampling regularizer intended to preserve output diversity while keeping RLHF's gains, with the largest benefit reported on hard tasks. The most practically interesting of the three top picks: mode collapse under post-training is the failure every team hits and few publish about.
- **From Local Skills to Long-Horizon Web Tasks: Progressive Skill Exploration for LLM Web Agents** ([arXiv:2611.03116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03116)) — a curriculum that trains primitive web actions first and then chains them, aimed at sparse-reward multi-step workflows.
- **Exact Fusion of Softmax Attention Heads Can Require Exponentially Many Atoms** ([arXiv:2611.02943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02943)) — a theoretical result that collapsing multi-head attention into a single head can require exponentially many key-value pairs to preserve outputs. A lower-bound argument for why multi-head designs persist, which is rarer and more durable than another benchmark.
- **CUA-Master: Scaling Computer-use Agents through Open-World Recursive Improvements** ([arXiv:2611.03126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03126)) — agents generate, critique and refine their own interaction data; the digest reports **>30%** gains on web-automation benchmarks.
- **Action Verification through Conjecture and Refutation for Language Model Agents** ([arXiv:2611.02950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02950)) — agents test a proposed action against counterexamples before executing it; the digest reports a **42%** reduction in unsafe tool use.

### OpenAI publishes LifeVal — and grades itself on it
**November 2.** OpenAI released **LifeVal**, an evaluation for the everyday tasks people most want help with: finding childcare, budgeting, government paperwork, grocery ordering, recipe modification, workout planning. Tasks were drawn from the **American Time Use Survey** and the **Multinational Time Use Study**, plus a survey of **3,995 respondents** on where they wanted assistant help most. OpenAI says it has used LifeVal internally **for about a year** to steer ChatGPT toward real agent work. **Why it matters:** it is a deliberate move away from coding and exam benchmarks toward domestic logistics, which is where consumer assistants actually fail. **Why to discount the numbers:** coverage conflicts on whether the task set and scoring were actually opened to third parties, and the more careful accounts say they were not — meaning the published scores are **self-graded on a set OpenAI controls**. Circulating figures of "80.6% for GPT-6 Astra" appear in some outlets and are contradicted by others; OpenAI's own page gives no such number. Do not cite the scores. [OpenAI — LifeVal](https://openai.com/index/lifeval/) · [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/openai-s-lifeval-judges-ai-on-real-life-errands-not-just-coding)

## Other Notable AI News

### Nvidia's 51% rule: China exposure capped by contract
The **Financial Times**, citing **five people familiar with the matter**, reported that Nvidia now requires cloud and data-centre customers to derive **at least 51% of their service sales from outside China**. The rule was communicated **verbally**, applies to **new contracts only**, and is **global** despite headlines framing it as Asian. **What is genuinely unclear:** how the 51% is measured — revenue, capacity or customer count — which providers are covered, and whether the US government asked for it. **Nvidia has not confirmed any of it on the record.** Context: Nvidia reportedly more than halved the number of Asian customers cleared to buy its AI chips earlier this year, and one analysis using its 10-Q puts **~$39.5B of commitments and guarantees** tied to AI cloud partners against **$12.4B of first-half revenue** from China-headquartered customers. Providers outside the US are already reported to be struggling to find enough non-Chinese demand to comply. **Why it matters:** export controls restrict what chips cross the border; this restricts who may rent them once they are abroad — a materially broader instrument, imposed privately by a vendor rather than publicly by a state. [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/nvidia-tells-data-center-operators-to-limit-business-from-chinese-customers-with-new-global-restrictions-report-claims-allegedly-restricted-to-49-percent-of-the-total-ensuring-51-percent-are-non-chinese-outfits) · [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/updates/nvidia-clients-to-scale-back-chinese-relations)

### China says it will accelerate AI legislation and set full-lifecycle safety standards
At a Beijing briefing on **November 3**, **NDRC** official **Wang Ruomeng** said China will speed up **comprehensive AI legislation** and establish safety standards and ethical guidelines across **the full AI lifecycle**: *"We will accelerate legislation to support core AI enablers — data, computing power and algorithms — and regulate key application scenarios."* Reported elements include **mandatory disclosure of safety risks and reporting of incidents and vulnerabilities** by developers and operators — reaching **DeepSeek, Moonshot and Alibaba** — and **sector-specific regulatory sandboxes** allowing more room for trial and error. Officials also committed to supporting open AI development through the **15th five-year plan** (to 2030) and to safety-net protections for workers displaced by AI, and called for international cooperation on AI safety governance. **Status check:** this is a **legislative plan, not an enacted law**, with **no effective date**. It follows the **September 2026** third edition of TC260's national AI Safety Governance Framework and lands amid US–China talks on AI risk. **Why it matters:** incident-reporting and vulnerability-disclosure duties are the same instrument Western regimes are reaching for, which makes the near-term question one of enforcement asymmetry rather than philosophy. ## Trending Open-Source Projects

All four below were **created within the last three weeks** (confirmed via GitHub repository search filtered on creation date) and none has appeared in a previous digest — so the star counts shown are also, effectively, the velocity. Figures were read from the repository pages directly today. **One follow-up on yesterday's lead item:** `vercel-labs/agent-desktop` **accelerated** rather than faded — **13.2k stars, +3,791 today** against +2,275 yesterday, still #1 on the daily board.

**[storytold/craft-anything](https://github.com/storytold/craft-anything)** — *"an open-source, clean-room engine in pure Rust"* that inspects a website, app or game and has a coding agent rebuild it. Dual MIT/Apache-2.0, **100% Rust**, **~9.0k stars**, 1.5k forks, 338 commits, 116 open issues. Four stages: **Study** (pages, code, files, runtime behaviour), **Build** (agent creates a new project in your chosen stack), **Check** (diffs the build against the original and lists mismatches), **Refine**. Ships a Simple mode, an Advanced workbench, a CLI and an **MCP server exposing ~320 tools**; **Claude Code is the primary supported builder**. **Why it's trending:** "point it at a thing and get a working clone" is the most legible agent demo since computer use, and the check-and-diff loop is what distinguishes it from a prompt. **What the README itself says to worry about:** it is **early alpha**, and releases are alpha. **"Clean room is not a sandbox"** — it does **not** isolate your machine, and agents run with your permissions; dependency installs can execute code, including npm install scripts, and "always allow" suppresses future prompts. Building via API keys and non-Claude agents is **written but untested live**; the **Codex adapter is unverified on a real install**; of the listed ACP agents **only Maki has been run**; behaviour checks for apps and games are **only partly verified**. On legality the README is explicit: use it only on software you own or are permitted to examine, it does **not** bypass DRM, logins or anti-cheat, and the agent is instructed not to copy logos, photos or text without permission. Requires Rust 1.97+; a managed tier is the next planned item.

**[heydamir/unslop](https://github.com/heydamir/unslop)** — *a skill that turns vibecoded, AI-looking UI into a designed interface.* **~7.7k stars**, 384 forks, and **12 commits** — the most extreme stars-per-commit ratio on this board, and the clearest signal of the week's dominant repo category. Works across Next.js, React, Vue, Svelte, Tailwind and plain HTML/CSS, on dashboards, apps, landing pages, or a live site given a URL. It **audits** the screen for slop markers (purple gradients, glows, emoji labels, uneven spacing), **proposes a direction and waits for your input** before editing, then fixes in passes: spacing, color, icons, copy, type, layout, details, visuals, motion. Installs by copying `skills/unslop` into `~/.claude/skills/` or `~/.agents/skills/`. **Why it's trending:** it is the third repo in a week — after `Nutlope/hallmark` and `pbakaus/impeccable` — monetizing the aesthetic signature of agent-written frontends, and the one that reduces to a single copyable directory. **What to know:** licensed **CC BY 4.0** (commercial use allowed with credit and a link back) — a content license, not a software one, which is an odd fit for installable code. Image generation is **paid**, routed through fal, Replicate, OpenAI images or Higgsfield; it shows the prompt and asks before **every** paid generation, and offers to set up an MCP server if none is connected. Live-site snapshots need **Playwright and Chromium**; the sub-10-second "story video" of the unslopping also needs **ffmpeg** on PATH. The README also advertises the author's paid service to unslop one screen in three days — the repo is partly a funnel.

**[ccch1mneyyy/dsh-Go](https://github.com/ccch1mneyyy/dsh-Go)** — an **unofficial, community-built Android client for DeepSeek Harness**, either running the DSH engine **on the phone** or connecting to DSH on a PC/server over SSH or DSHarness Remote. **AGPL-3.0** (with `sdk/` and `examples/` MIT), **~8.1k stars**, 383 forks, 266 commits, 201 open issues; README is primarily Simplified Chinese. Installs an Ubuntu environment, Node.js and DSH **in-app, without root or Termux**; adds model switching, image and voice input, tool-approval handling, native tool cards/forms/panels with a plugin SDK, conversation share/export/replay, themes and desktop pets. **Why it's trending:** DeepSeek's own `dsh-superagent-prototype` drew 17.4k stars this week, and this is the mobile client that ecosystem lacked — the on-device-engine path, not a thin remote shell. **The README is unusually honest about risk, and it should be read before installing.** It has **no affiliation with DeepSeek**. **Phone control** — reading the screen, tapping, typing — works only with the local engine in normal mode and requires accessibility permissions; **screen text may be sent to the model and screenshots to vision-capable models**, and relaxing app rules **reduces built-in safeguards**. It warns that AI **can be misled by malicious instructions in web pages, messages or files**, and that commands, file changes and phone control can cause data loss, privacy exposure or financial loss; it recommends backups, vetted hosts and plugins only, and **never running irreversible tasks unattended**. Practical limits: **Android 8.0+**, **arm64 only** for real phones, ~1GB free space for local DSH, **APK sideloading** (not Play Store), battery savers can kill it in the background, and installing over a differently signed test build **requires uninstalling first, erasing conversations and settings**. Remote mode supports **images only** and no DS Bot or log export. Anonymous usage statistics are **on by default**; share links are end-to-end encrypted but **anyone with the full link can read them**. iOS and HarmonyOS are planned, not available. The author asks to be contacted before derivative or commercial work.

**[spotify/portal-ai-plugins](https://github.com/spotify/portal-ai-plugins)** — Spotify-maintained coding-agent plugins connecting **[Spotify Portal](https://portal.spotify.com)** to **Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and pi**. Apache-2.0, **~3.1k stars**, 239 forks, **9 commits**, **+91 today** on the daily board. Wraps the upstream `@spotify/portal-cli` to give agents the developer-portal layer: catalog and technical-docs search by natural-language query, service briefings covering **ownership, health, incidents and docs**, read-only diagnostics across plugin/CLI/auth/action readiness, and discovery and invocation of Portal **actions** with help, dry-run and confirmation safeguards. It can create, run, check and troubleshoot **Fleetshift** shifts and open PRs from completed runs. Also ships **shunt**, which routes I/O-heavy work to cheaper worker models through AiKA. **Why it's here:** it is the first major vendor to publish agent plugins for its *internal developer platform* rather than its product API — service ownership and incident context are exactly what agents lack when touching a large estate, and Spotify is shipping that as the integration surface. **What to know:** **shunt is Claude Code-only "for now"**; everything runs through `npx` against the upstream CLI, so the plugins inherit its auth and version; setup gates on five specific CLI commands (`auth`, `actions`, `owner`, `search`, `service`) being present; and the repo carries **no description, website or topics** and restricts PR creation to collaborators — published, but not yet positioned as a community project.
