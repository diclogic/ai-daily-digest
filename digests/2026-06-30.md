### Andrej Karpathy — Viral CLAUDE.md Expands to Ten Rules

The biggest Karpathy story this weekend isn't a public statement but a document attributed to him: a 10-rule CLAUDE.md file that reportedly emerged from his pre-training team at Anthropic. The six new rules (added to the widely-adopted 4-rule community template by Forrest Chang, now at 200K+ combined stars) cover verification before fixing, debugging discipline, dependency hygiene, and named failure modes for agentic loops. AI Builder Club reports the expanded ruleset dropped Claude Code error rates from 41% to 11% in their testing. Karpathy has not publicly confirmed or denied authorship; readers should evaluate on merits rather than attribution.

## Other Notable AI News

### Trump AI Executive Order — July 2 Deadline in 2 Days

The June 2 Executive Order's first 30-day deadline arrives Wednesday. Federal agencies must: prioritize AI-enabled cyber defense, release binding operational directives for civilian IT, and form an "AI cybersecurity clearinghouse" with industry. The 60-day deadline (August 1) requires a voluntary framework for pre-release federal access to frontier models — the mechanism now governing both GPT-5.6 and Mythos releases.

([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) · [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/02/nx-s1-5844347/ai-safety-trump-executive-order) · [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/president-trump-signs-executive-order-establishing-ai-cybersecurity-and-frontier-model-framework))

### Colorado AI Act — Original Effective Date Passes Today Without Effect

The original Colorado AI Act was set to take effect today (June 30) but Governor Polis signed SB 189 on May 14, replacing it with a substantially narrower law focused on disclosure and transparency. The replacement drops the duty of care for algorithmic discrimination, deployer impact assessments, and AG reporting obligations. New effective date: January 1, 2027. The pivot away from an EU-style risk-based framework is seen as a bellwether for other states considering AI regulation.

([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/colorado-ai-act-amended-and-effective-date-delayed) · [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/colorado-amends-its-artificial-intelligence-law-substantially-reducing) · [Carpe Datum](https://www.carpedatumlaw.com/2026/05/colorados-ai-reset-two-weeks-a-white-house-callout-and-a-pivot-away-from-the-eu-model/))
