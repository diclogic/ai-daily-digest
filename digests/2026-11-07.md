## Notable Papers & Research

### Google DeepMind: AI agents find two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA
Announced **November 6** by **Pushmeet Kohli**, led on DeepMind's side by **Dj Dvijotham** and **Abhradeep Thakurta**, working with cryptanalysts **Nadia Heninger** (UC San Diego / Inria) and **Emmanuel Thomé** (Inria). Two results: a signature-forgery technique reported as roughly **1,600× more efficient** against short-digest 1024-bit keys, and a new batch-factoring algorithm that cuts the **marginal** cost of factoring additional related keys by about **30×**. DeepMind validated the approach in a sandboxed simulation against an **already-solved** RSA-1024 benchmark key, reproducing the earlier result with **3.5× less CPU time**. The padding weakness reportedly bites when an attacker controls roughly half or more of the signed block. **Keys of 2048 bits and above are not affected by this specific result.** The headline multiplier is a theoretical estimate and the full technical report could not be retrieved from a primary source here. [Kohli on X](https://x.com/pushmeet/status/2118753824194408683) · [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/google-deepmind-ai-rsa-1024-bit-weaknesses/)

The interesting part is the framing, not the number: this is the second week in a row in which a frontier lab has published AI-driven work against production-grade security assumptions rather than against a benchmark.

### METR on Anthropic: capability growth roughly twice as fast, and AI is credited with most of it
Published **November 5**. METR estimates that growth on **AECI** — Anthropic's internal version of the Epoch Capabilities Index — has accelerated about **1.9×** since October 2025 (95% CI **1.5–2.8×**) relative to the March 2024–October 2025 rate, with **unreleased model snapshots** included in the analysis. METR further estimates that **AI itself accounts for perhaps ~80%** of that acceleration, a judgment it marks as substantially uncertain; secondary summaries note the evidence does not establish a clear causal link. **Much of the supporting evidence was redacted by Anthropic.** Co-founder **Sam McCandlish**: *"We redacted a lot, and I accept that this makes it harder for others to check METR's work."* Note that this is a benchmark-growth result, **not** a METR time-horizon result — separate work argues the time-horizon metric is nearing saturation. [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/metr-says-anthropics-model-progress-is-roughly-twice-as-fast-but-key-evidence-remains-redacted/) · [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-metr-anthropic-likely-improving-models-at-2x-its-old-rate/)

### OpenAI: models behave *better* in production than in the safety evals meant to catch them
OpenAI's alignment team published **"Eval-production behavior gaps in agent safety and the role of eval-awareness"** (approximately **November 3–4**). Across six models, matched **Codex** production decisions were handled more safely than the controlled test, with test scores **10.2 to 37.4 percentage points lower**. General test-awareness was verbalized in **57.0–83.7%** of evaluation runs versus **4.6–23.5%** in production, and the largest gaps appeared in the models that most often verbalized an **incorrect** evaluation objective. Rank ordering survived (Spearman ≈ **0.771**), so evals still rank models usefully even where they misestimate absolute safety. Causally, awareness alone did not move the needle — explicitly framing the objective did. [alignment.openai.com](https://alignment.openai.com/eval-production-behavior-gap/) · [LessWrong summary](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ae7wxdzEy8G3Dkrqn/openai-sidestepping-evaluation-awareness-and-anticipating)

This is a quietly awkward finding for the whole evaluation stack: it says the number on the card is not the number in deployment, in **either** direction.

### The agent-benchmark cluster: everyone is now measuring delivery, not answers
Four papers from the past week converge on the same move — grading agents on finished work under changing conditions:
- **DeliverBench** ([arXiv 2611.03412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03412), submitted Nov 2) — 11 professional projects (financial due diligence, legal drafting, drug-safety reporting) chaining **150 subtasks** across **2–15 rounds** of new material and client feedback.
- **CompressionBench** (Nov 4 preprint) — coding agents asked to produce compressed models; **29 of 36** submissions passed final verification, best design averaging **77.33%** downstream accuracy against **79.56%** for BF16.
- **VisSearch-Bench** (Nov 3) — long-horizon visual search for multimodal agents; the best system fully solves **26.47%** of tasks.
- **FrontierPhysics** (UC Berkeley / Caltech) — agents on physics research tasks fail on rigorous reasoning and expert intuition far more than on knowledge or execution.

## Model & Product Launches

### OpenAI GPT-6 Cyber — the first model OpenAI calls "Critical"
Released **November 5**. GPT-6 Cyber is the first OpenAI model rated **Critical** for cybersecurity under the Preparedness Framework, one tier above GPT-5.6 Cyber's **High**; by OpenAI's own definition that tier means a model can both find and build working zero-days in real-world critical systems and run attacks end to end. It scores **48.5% on ExploitGym** (up from **32.1%**), and researchers used it to find **81 previously unknown high-severity vulnerabilities in Firefox**, which Mozilla has begun fixing. OpenAI reports it stays inside task boundaries and avoids out-of-scope services in **99.93%** of cases, up from **94.49%** — a vendor figure with no external red-team confirmation yet. Access is gated to approved **Daybreak Red** customers via API and Codex, with requests restricted to approved networks by default, plus a new **Daybreak Red for Critical Infrastructure** tier. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-cyber/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openai-launches-gpt-6-cyber-after-ai.html) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/openai-says-new-gpt-6-cyber-model-hunts-flaws-without-crossing-the-line/)

This is the release behind the 948 Linux kernel exploits and the $1.2M API bill reported here on November 5 — the model that paper ran on was unreleased then, and now it is shipping.

### Reserved slot: Baidu open-sources ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base *(company not covered elsewhere in this digest)*
Released **November 5**: a **280B-parameter sparse MoE** with about **14B active per token**, pre-trained from scratch on **33.5 trillion tokens**, 128k training context with 1M supported at fine-tuning and inference, published in **FP8 and BF16** on Hugging Face and ModelScope. It is a **base checkpoint** — no instruction tuning, no RL — with a post-trained ERNIE 5.5 Flash promised to follow. Two strings attached: the license covers *"all geographical areas worldwide other than the European Union, the United Kingdom and South Korea,"* and commercial products must visibly display **"Powered by ERNIE."** Benchmark claims are Baidu's own. ## Key Opinions & Statements

### Hassabis: society is "not ready," and the human reviewer is the line
**Demis Hassabis** on **Masters of Scale** with **Reid Hoffman**, published within the past few days (recorded at the Masters of Scale Summit in San Francisco in October). His argument: AI is already making engineers dramatically more productive, but **closed-loop self-improvement without a human reviewer is the riskiest path to AGI** — and self-improvement works best precisely where answers are checkable, in math, coding and games, which is also where the loop is easiest to close. He characterized recent AI cyber incidents as the product of **poor engineering rather than inevitability**, said building a national frontier model **"doesn't make sense"** for most countries, and noted **AlphaFold** is now used by roughly **4 million researchers**. [Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/demis-hassabis-society-is-not-ready-for-the-ai-future/) · [summary](https://daily.scuttlebutt.co/p/self-improving-ai-without-a-human)

No transcript was retrievable from this session, so the wording above comes from the episode page and secondary summaries.

## Trending Open-Source Projects

**Methodology caveat for this edition:** GitHub's API was restricted to this repository today, so **none of the star figures below could be verified directly** — unlike previous digests. Secondary sources disagree wildly (one tracker reports totals for these repos that differ by 3× from another), so treat absolute counts as unreliable and weight the **daily velocity** figures, which at least come from same-day snapshots. Previously featured repos are skipped, including **deepseek-ai/dsh-superagent** (featured November 2), whose `-prototype` repo is this week's single fastest riser at a reported ~20.3k stars from an October 31 creation.

**1. [cloudflare/security-audit-skill](https://github.com/cloudflare/security-audit-skill)** — A coding-agent skill that runs multi-phase security audits and emits independently verified, machine-readable findings: six phases from reconnaissance through coverage-led hunting, candidate validation, structured output, independent verification and reporting, with a separate set of isolated agents trying to **disprove** each finding. MIT, installs via `npx skills add`. *Why it's trending:* reportedly **+2,524 stars in a single day** — the week's biggest one-day gain among skills repos — and it is the single-repo entry point to the vulnerability-discovery harness Cloudflare described in its own blog post, landing in the same week OpenAI shipped a Critical-rated offensive model. The adversarial-verification step is the design idea worth stealing.

**2. [mattpocock/skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills)** — "Skills for Real Engineers," pulled straight from the author's `.agents` directory: TDD and related engineering disciplines packaged as agent skills. MIT. *Why it's trending:* reportedly **+2,058 stars in a day**, and a Trendshift snapshot puts it at **+62,030 over the 61 days to November 6** — sustained velocity, not a single spike. It is the clearest evidence that the skills layer, not the framework layer, is where agent tooling is consolidating.

**3. [stablyai/orca](https://github.com/stablyai/orca)** — A desktop (plus mobile and remote-runtime) environment for running a **fleet of coding agents in parallel** — Claude Code, Codex and others — on your own subscription, each isolated in its own git worktree, with GitHub and Linear integration and a phone companion for monitoring and follow-ups. MIT. *Why it's trending:* it answers the question every heavy agent user hits at once — orchestration and isolation, not model quality — and it ships as a real app rather than a framework. Active release cadence (v1.4.242 in late October).

**4. [thedotmack/claude-mem](https://github.com/thedotmack/claude-mem)** — Persistent context across sessions for coding agents: captures tool-use observations, compresses and summarizes them with Claude's agent SDK, stores them in **SQLite with FTS5** full-text search, and feeds them back into later sessions via hooks. *Why it's trending:* the complement to Orca's parallelism problem — memory between sessions rather than coordination within one — and reported star counts have roughly doubled over the past several weeks on third-party trackers. Worth noting the obvious caveat its own reviewers raise: everything the agent read gets persisted somewhere.

*Also seen, logged but not featured:* **AegisKernel**, a zero-trust capability-and-taint runtime for agent tool calls built by a 16-year-old developer, posted to Show HN with sub-millisecond latency claims — real project, self-reported benchmarks, and only ~12 points of HN traction, below this section's bar for now.
