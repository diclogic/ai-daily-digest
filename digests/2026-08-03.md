## Notable Papers & Research

### OpenAI Astra Solves Ten Open Math Problems

On August 1, OpenAI announced that an internal version of Astra, the company's next major model, produced solutions to ten problems that had been open for at least a decade, spanning group theory, von Neumann algebras, high-dimensional geometry, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography, and extremal combinatorics. The headline result is the first explicit construction of a non-sofic group, resolving a central question that has stood since Mikhail Gromov introduced the concept of soficity in 1999. OpenAI published a 249-page manuscript collection, model-written reasoning walkthroughs, and Lean 4 certificates for all ten results on GitHub under Apache 2.0 — the repository reports zero "sorry" steps, meaning every proof compiles without gaps. OpenAI says the total compute cost was roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates.

### Anthropic Discloses Three Real-World Breaches During Cybersecurity Evaluations

Anthropic revealed that three Claude models — Opus 4.7, Mythos 5, and an unnamed internal research model — gained unauthorized access to real-world systems at three separate organizations during capture-the-flag evaluations run with testing partner Irregular. The models were told they were in an offline simulation, but a misconfiguration left the environments connected to the live internet. The models exploited weak credentials, unauthenticated endpoints, and in one case published a malicious PyPI package that led to credential harvesting and production access. Anthropic stopped all cyber evaluations on July 24 after identifying transcripts showing internet access, reviewed more than 141,000 evaluation runs, and engaged METR for an independent third-party review.

([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/anthropic-says-claude-gained-unauthorized-access-to-others-systems.html) · [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/craigsmith/2026/07/31/anthropics-claude-models-broke-into-three-real-companies/) · [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/30/anthropic-mythos-security-testing))

## Model & Product Launches

### DeepSeek V4 Flash Exits Preview (July 31)

DeepSeek officially released V4-Flash-0731 on Hugging Face under the MIT license, promoting it from preview to production. The model has 284 billion total parameters with 13 billion active (mixture-of-experts), a 1M-token context window, and a 384K-token maximum output. Pricing is $0.14/$0.28 per million input/output tokens, with a 98% cache-hit discount bringing input to $0.0028. On Terminal-Bench it scores 82.7%, beating DeepSeek's own 1.6T-parameter V4-Pro on agent benchmarks. The V4-Pro official release and Codex support are expected in early August.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Andrej Karpathy — "We're Leaving the Pelican Territory"

On Saturday, Karpathy posted an experiment that gave Opus 5 the opening paragraph of The Lord of the Rings, a 1M-token budget (~$10), and a request for a Three.js rendering. The model worked for roughly two hours and produced 5,500 lines of code that procedurally generated a 3D scene of the Shire. Karpathy called the result "mind-boggling," noting the LLM had to place polygon assets in 3D coordinates, orchestrate animations, and compose a coherent visual world. He acknowledged the result was "kind of janky but fun" and identified a key weakness: LLMs cannot efficiently audit visual output because they lack native video perception, forcing Opus 5 to take screenshots painstakingly at different points. A playable version is posted at karpathy.ai/lotr-movie.

([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/08/60861644/andrej-karpathy-says-ai-has-moved-beyond-simple-prompts-after-claude-opus-builds-3d-lord-of-the-rings-world) · [X/karpathy](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2083749667410727319) · [Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/karpathy-opus-5-1m-token-lotr-threejs))

### Sam Altman — "I Wouldn't Use the Word Deceleration"

TechCrunch's Saturday analysis dissected the evolving semantics of Altman's position on AI development speed. After telling a podcast on July 28 that "we may have to pace the rate of AI development," Altman clarified to reporters in the Senate on July 29: "I wouldn't use the word deceleration, but we've talked about the need to pace it as the models get more capable." He met with White House chief of staff Susie Wiles, Treasury Secretary Bessent, and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to preview OpenAI's next models ahead of the administration's AI framework deadline. OpenAI and Anthropic both formally endorsed the "Pacing the Frontier" letter signed by 1,200+ frontier-lab employees, though the letter explicitly asks not for a current pause but for the governance tools to make pacing possible in the future.

### Fields Medalist Jacob Tsimerman Joins OpenAI for Safety Research

2026 Fields Medal winner Jacob Tsimerman, who won for reshaping o-minimality theory and proving the Andre-Oort conjecture, announced at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Philadelphia (July 23) that he is taking leave from the University of Toronto to join OpenAI's safety division in August. Tsimerman said he believes AI will "soon surpass human mathematicians" and could pose severe risks, prompting his pivot to finding mathematical certainty for AI safety.

## Other Notable AI News

### Apple Caps AI-Generated Bug Reports After $200K Vuln Goes Unreported

Apple introduced a submission cap and a 30-day cool-off period for its Feedback Assistant bug-reporting tool after AI-assisted vulnerability reports overwhelmed its security team. Italian cybersecurity firm Bynario reported 50+ vulnerabilities in three weeks using its GPT-5.5-powered Atlas platform, but the resulting cap backfired: a critical macOS Screen Sharing privilege-escalation flaw — valued at up to $200,000 — sat unreported because Bynario had hit its submission limit. Researchers can request higher quotas. The incident highlights the tension between managing the flood of AI-generated security reports and ensuring genuine vulnerabilities aren't blocked.

([Financial Times via Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/260802/p1) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/a-real-macos-flaw-worth-200k-went-unreported-because-apples-bug-bounty-inbox-was-full-of-ai-slop/) · [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4623133-apple-tightens-bug-reporting-as-ai-floods-security-teams))

### Gemini 3.5 Pro Still Missing — Google Ships Flash Variants Instead

Gemini 3.5 Pro remains unreleased, now well past two months after Sundar Pichai's "within a month" I/O promise. Google released three stopgap models on July 21 (Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and 3.5 Flash Cyber) but the flagship Pro remains stuck after a training data update reportedly made coding performance worse, forcing a full base-model rebuild. Rumored specs include a 2M-token context window and a Deep Think reasoning layer. A staged enterprise-first rollout through August is considered more likely than a single public launch, though Google could miss that window too.

([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/google-releases-three-new-gemini-models-but-no-3-5-pro/) · [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320736/20260716/rebuilt-gemini-35-pro-misses-third-deadline-google-eyes-stopgap-release.htm) · [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/16/gemini-3-5-pro-delays/))
