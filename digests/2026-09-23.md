# AI Daily Digest — September 23, 2026

**Top story: both frontier labs shipped on the same Tuesday, and both shipped *down* the cost curve rather than up the capability curve.** Anthropic released **Claude Opus 5.5** — explicitly labelled "our first release since we called for pacing the frontier" — matching or beating Fable 5.1 across nine benchmarks at 20% lower token prices and 40% lower typical task cost. Hours later OpenAI released **GPT-6 Sol and Luna**, cutting API prices 50% or more against their GPT-5.6 predecessors and, per independent measurement, landing at roughly GPT-5.6-level capability for a fraction of the spend. OpenAI also published a framework committing to let outside evaluators assess models *during training* rather than at release. Whether "pacing" means a slower capability frontier or a cheaper one is now the live question — and it will be asked in person today, when Altman, Amodei, Yoshua Bengio and Clément Delangue brief the UN Security Council at 11:00 New York time.

## Notable Papers & Research

The September 22–23 arXiv window produced an unusually coherent cluster: **three separate papers on recursive self-improvement of agent harnesses**, submitted within hours of OpenAI publicly naming RSI as the capability that most needs governing.

### RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses
Peng Xia, Rujun Han, Zifeng Wang, Yanfei Chen, Yufan Zhang, Yoonho Lee, Chengsong Huang, Han Yu, Zhongying CuiZhu, Yifei Ming, Huaxiu Yao, Burak Gokturk, Tomas Pfister, Chen-Yu Lee — Google Cloud AI Research, UNC-Chapel Hill, Stanford, WashU (arXiv 2609.24972)
An agent's capability is mostly its **harness** — prompts, control flow, tooling, memory, context management — wrapped around a frozen backbone. Letting the agent evolve its own harness works, and then stops working: the paper's starting observation is that unregularized self-improvement **memorizes the training tasks**, showing large in-distribution gains that shrink or vanish out of distribution. RRSI constrains both halves of the loop: a proposer on a **temporally annealed edit budget** that rewards unexplored trajectories, and a selector with a **critic** that rejects benchmark-specific edits plus a **pruner** that drops changes too small, too costly, or no longer useful. Across eight coding, agentic-workspace and engineering-design benchmarks it gains **up to 14.1 points in-distribution and up to 4.7 points on five OOD benchmarks**, while yielding a harness that runs on **30% fewer policy tokens** than unregularized evolution. The efficiency result is the interesting one: regularizing self-improvement makes the resulting agent cheaper, not just more general. Code at [google-research/rrsi](https://github.com/google-research/rrsi). ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24972))

### Harness-Zero: Harness Distillation via Agent-as-Harness
arXiv 2609.24974 (submitted September 21)
The companion problem to RRSI: if the best harness varies by domain, instance and model, the gains are **stranded at deployment** behind whichever harness you actually ship. Harness-Zero uses a domain- or instance-optimized harness as *training-time guidance* and transfers the behaviors it induces into model weights, so the gains survive under one fixed target harness. The difficulty is that source and target harnesses differ in action space and available information, so the optimized harness's traces cannot serve directly as supervision — the "agent-as-harness" construction is the bridge. Reported: a **macro-average lift from 23.3% to 44.3%**, agent-as-harness at **81.1% vs 78.1% for code-as-harness**, and **82.3% recovery of 28 harness-induced behavior patterns**. Read alongside RRSI, the pair describes a full loop — evolve a harness safely, then compile it into the model — which is a more concrete picture of near-term RSI than most of the policy discussion assumes. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24974))

### Rare Event Estimation via Iterative Unalignment
Hanming Yang, Daksh Mittal, Jing Dong, Hongseok Namkoong — Columbia Business School (arXiv 2609.24969, submitted September 21)
How often will an autonomous agent do the catastrophic thing, when the catastrophe arises from **stochastic variation in its own sampled actions**? Naive Monte Carlo is hopeless at these probabilities, and building importance-sampling proposals requires coordinated changes across a context-dependent chain of conditional distributions. The authors' method **perturbs the original model's weights** to construct the proposal, so the proposal is itself a differentiably parameterized language model and the search becomes gradient-based over weight space. The framing is worth noting on the day of a UN Security Council session on loss of control: it treats "how rare is the disaster" as a measurable quantity rather than a rhetorical one. Code at [namkoong-lab/iterative-unalignment](https://github.com/namkoong-lab/iterative-unalignment). ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24969))

### OSWorld-Pro: Process-based Evaluation for Computer Use Agents
arXiv 2609.24890 (submitted September 21)
Computer-use agents are graded on the final deliverable after hundreds of steps, which tells you that a run failed but not where or why. OSWorld-Pro supplies **300+ tasks decomposed into 2,800+ subgoals grounded in 67,000+ human annotations**, scored by human-aligned LLM judges over sequentially dependent subgoals. That buys **partial credit for early progress without a final deliverable** and pinpoints the step at which a trajectory diverges. A direct complement to OSWorld, and to the agentic-coding benchmark numbers both labs published yesterday — which are still end-state scores. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24890))

### Also in the window
- **DolphinBench: Mapping the Pareto Frontier of Agent Memory** (2609.24971) — memory benchmarks built from realistic long-horizon interaction rather than prompt-answer pairs, framing memory as a trade-off surface. The third memory-is-a-trade-off paper in three days, after MemCalib on Monday.
- **Critical-State RL: Diagnosing Trainable States for Multi-Turn Tool Use** (2609.24985) — identifies which states in a failed multi-turn tool-use trajectory are actually *trainable*, separating genuine action effects from downstream randomness.
- **The Answer-Basin Representation Hypothesis: We Are Not Probing or Steering Concepts** (2609.24821) — argues linear representations reflect the organization of *answer probability*, not concepts; if it holds, a large body of probing and steering interpretability work is measuring something other than what it claims.
- **DexTacWAM: A Visuo-Tactile World-Action Model for Dexterous Manipulation** (2609.24976) and **Uranus: Next-Generation Simulation Infrastructure for Embodied AI** (2609.24815) — the week's physical-AI entries, both landing the same day as Intrinsic Core below.

## Model & Product Launches

### Anthropic — Claude Opus 5.5
Released **September 22**, the first model in the 5.5 family and the first Anthropic release since Amodei's September 12 pacing essay. Performance sits at or above **Claude Fable 5.1** on most work at **40% lower typical task cost** than Opus 5. Headline numbers: **66.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0** (vs GPT-6 Astra's 57.9%), **54.4% on FrontierCode v1.1** (Astra's best: 53.3%), and **1846 Elo on GDPval-AA v2.1** across 44 occupations, roughly **300 Elo clear of Astra**. Token prices drop 20% to **$4/M input and $20/M output**, cache reads drop 60% to **$0.20/M**, output is **~30% faster**, and per-task token use falls 25–40%. Anthropic also claims a plainer writing style, cutting the verbosity users complained about in long sessions.

On safety, Opus 5.5 is the strongest performer on Anthropic's automated behavioral audit across nearly 2,000 scenarios, with an **85% reduction in containment-boundary circumvention attempts versus Opus 5** and improved prompt-injection resistance; external pre-release testing was done by **METR and Frontier Design**. Available on the Claude Platform as `claude-opus-5-5` and on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure. **Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 follow "in the coming weeks."**

Customer quotes are unusually specific about the efficiency claim rather than the capability one — GitHub CPO Mario Rodriguez: *"Claude Opus 5.5 used among the fewest tokens and steps we measured… solving more tasks than Opus 5 in less than half the steps"*; Optiver's Noyan Tokgozoglu: *"matched Opus 5's quality in about half the turns, time and output tokens, cutting costs by 40 to 50%."* ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/anthropic-releases-opus-5-5-with-lower-prices-and-fable-level-performance/), [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/claude-opus-5-5-matches-fable-5-1-at-40-percent-lower-cost-as-anthropic-promises-to-fix-claudish-writing/), [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/378947/anthropic-claude-opus-5-5-fable-price-comparison))

### OpenAI — GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna
Released **September 22**, nineteen days after GPT-6 Astra. Both carry a **1.05M-token context (922K input / 128K max output)**. Pricing is the story, and OpenAI confirmed to VentureBeat that these are **permanent, not promotional, rates**: **Sol at $2/M input and $10/M output** (from $4/$20) and **Luna at $0.10/M and $0.50/M** (from $0.20/$1.20). Cached input reads get a 90% discount — **$0.20/M on Sol, $0.01/M on Luna** — and developers can now change reasoning effort or available tools *without invalidating cached context*, plus explicitly mark where a cacheable prefix ends. OpenAI claims **Sol makes ~50% fewer factual mistakes than GPT-5.6 Sol**, approaching Astra's reliability at a fraction of the compute.

Independent numbers are more equivocal than the pricing: on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index Sol scores **80** at max reasoning and Luna **74.6**, but Artificial Analysis measured **GPT-6 Luna (max) at 41 on the Intelligence Index, two points *below* GPT-5.6 Luna**, and **Sol (max) at 68.8% on DeepSWE v1.1 against GPT-5.6 Sol's 72.7%**. Sol posts **64.6% on SWE-Bench Pro**. The fair summary: at the top of each tier's effort range these are roughly as capable as the 5.6 models and occasionally slightly less — but at matched cost per task they are clearly better. Live in ChatGPT Work and Codex for most paid accounts and in the API; Luna also reaches the desktop app and Free/Go users. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/openai-launches-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/), [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/openai-gpt-6-sol-luna-release/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-releases-gpt-6-sol-and-luna-models-slashing-api-costs-50-or-more), [OpenAI dev community](https://community.openai.com/t/announcing-gpt-6-sol-and-gpt-6-luna-in-the-api-codex-and-chatgpt/1399925), [Vellum](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/gpt-6-sol-and-luna-benchmarks-explained))

### Intrinsic — Intrinsic Core *(reserved slot: company not covered elsewhere in this digest)*
Alphabet's industrial robotics unit **Intrinsic** open-sourced the core of its platform at **ROSCon 2026 in Toronto on September 22**, under **Apache 2.0**, as a pre-configured ROS-compatible environment that runs on local hardware. ## Key Opinions & Statements

### Fei-Fei Li — safety assessment "shouldn't be left solely to the companies developing" the systems
Speaking on **Bloomberg Tech, September 22**, the World Labs CEO made the sharpest version of the independent-evaluation argument anyone has made this month: safety assessment of increasingly capable AI **should not be left to the labs building it**, and needs **independent benchmarks** drawing on academia, government and industry rather than any single group grading its own homework. She also insisted humans must remain in control, and framed the whole question in the terms that have defined her public work — Bloomberg titled the segment *AI's Future Is "About Humans."* Why it matters: Li is the one major figure in this debate who runs a frontier lab **and** holds an academic post, and her position is notably harder than the labs' own. Anthropic and OpenAI are both offering evaluators *deeper access to lab-run processes*; Li is arguing for **benchmarks the labs do not control at all**. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/world-labs-founder-fei-fei-li-urges-independent-oversight-of-ai), [Bloomberg video](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-22/fei-fei-li-ai-s-future-is-about-humans-video), [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/fei-fei-li-independent-ai-oversight/))

### OpenAI — third-party assessors get access *during* training
Published **September 22**: OpenAI committed to letting external groups run technical safety assessments **across training, evaluation and deployment**, rather than only near release, naming four priority areas — safety cases spanning training and deployment, evaluation of critical safeguards, review of capability evaluations tied to the Preparedness Framework, and **independent investigation of misalignment incidents**. The stated principles are *"strong independence mechanisms, scientific rigor, robust security practices and clear responsibilities."* OpenAI says it is in talks with **METR and Redwood Research**.

Worth reading against what Altman promised ten days earlier, when he described evaluators getting **"desks, badges and laptops, with the right to publish what they found."** The published framework says assessors *"may be brought into the offices for the most sensitive work,"* names no confirmed partner, and sets no access terms or publication rights. It is a real move — pre-release access during training is more than anyone offered a month ago — and it is also materially softer than the pitch. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/priorities-principles-third-party-assessments/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/openai-to-let-outside-groups-evaluate-ai-models-at-earlier-phase), [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-evaluators-training-phase))

### Z.ai open-sources ZCode after its coding assistant silently uploaded users' repositories
The most damaging developer-trust story of the month closed a chapter on **September 22**. Chinese blogger **Ferstar** found that ZCode — from Z.ai, the lab behind the GLM models — had staged a **313MB encrypted archive of a commercial project including its full Git history** for upload to Alibaba Cloud, retrying **564 times** after failures, alongside a 15KB file that did transmit. The decryption key was held **only** by Z.ai's server, so users could neither read nor delete what had been taken. The cause was the **Codebase Indexing** feature — used for session checkpoints, rollbacks and wiki generation — **enabled by default with no clear toggle**. Z.ai has now apologized, disabled the features, switched on **zero data retention** for developers and businesses, and **open-sourced ZCode** outright. Open-sourcing as damage control is a notable precedent: the remedy for "we can't see what your client is doing" is being set at "then read the client." ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/22/zai_says_sorry_for_slurping_up_your_code_open_sources_zcode/5298300), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/devs-say-chinese-ai-company-silently-uploaded-hundreds-of-megabytes-of-local-workspace-data-z-ai-the-firm-behind-the-glm-models-didnt-ask-for-user-consent-and-made-564-attempts-to-exfiltrate-313mb-archive), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3368159/chinese-ai-firm-zai-faces-reputation-hit-after-users-spot-unauthorised-uploads), [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-09-22/zai-open-sources-coding-tool-after-backlash-over-silent-data-uploads-102487378.html))

### Meta hot-fixes a Muse zero-day in 16 hours
Researcher **Patrick Wardle** disclosed on **September 21** that an **unprivileged local process** could rewrite an undocumented setting in Muse's Mac app to redirect dictation traffic to an attacker-controlled endpoint — silently hijacking the agent when a user pressed the microphone button. ## Trending Open-Source Projects

**1. [dream-num/univer](https://github.com/dream-num/univer)** — ~15.7k stars, **+255 today**
Apache-2.0 SDK for embedding spreadsheets, documents, slides and relational tables into your own app, now positioning itself as *"the office harness for AI agents."* Isomorphic by design — the same canvas-rendered engine and formula layer run in the browser and in Node — with a plugin architecture and a single Facade API across document types and runtimes. *Why it's trending:* the agent-office category is consolidating fast, and Univer is the mature open core in it; server-side workbook processing with the same architecture as the browser is exactly what an agent manipulating structured office content needs, and nobody else ships it under a permissive license.

**2. [superdesigndev/treg](https://github.com/superdesigndev/treg)** — ~2.3k stars, **+230 today**
*"OpenRouter for agent tools."* A unified registry putting **3,000+ endpoints across 60+ providers** — SEO, enrichment, social trends, image and video generation — behind one token and base URL, billed at fractions of a cent per call, so teams can hit Semrush, Crunchbase or Apollo **without holding accounts with any of them**. Ships a CLI, OAuth credential management with auto-refresh, team/org RBAC, a shareable skill-and-recipe system, MCP integration, and self-hosting on Postgres or SQLite. Apache 2.0, with redistribution restrictions for competing hosted services. *Why it's trending:* it attacks the least glamorous blocker in agent deployment — that the useful data sits behind signup walls and per-seat subscriptions an agent cannot sign up for.

**3. [Vincentwei1021/anything2explainer](https://github.com/Vincentwei1021/anything2explainer)** — ~2k stars, created within the last month
A Claude Code / Codex skill that turns a topic or document into a **narrated 1280×720 motion-graphics explainer** with synced voiceover, subtitles and chapter progress bars, in Chinese or English with language-specific pacing and typography. The notable design choice: **every frame is rendered in code via Remotion (React + TypeScript)** — no stock footage and no generative video model. The repo ships the entire method, not just a prompt: research with sources, scriptwriting, TTS with word-boundary timing, storyboarding, parallel shot building, QC validation, style guides and a motion vocabulary, plus a full paper trail from the reference cut. **4. [kharmanskyi/open-steps](https://github.com/kharmanskyi/open-steps)** — ~871 stars, created within the last month
MIT-licensed plugin suite that translates coding-agent output into plain language for non-engineers: eight skills including `os-done-or-not` (a completion verdict), `os-step-by-step` and `os-big-picture`, plus two hooks that inject context and request end-of-session reports. Works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Gemini CLI. Its distinguishing claim is that it doesn't summarize — it **separates verified facts from assumptions** and presents decisions with context. *Why it's trending:* the same instinct as yesterday's `reverify` and this morning's OSWorld-Pro, aimed at a different audience — an agent's own account of finishing is not evidence that it finished, and the person who most needs that distinction is the one who can't read the diff.
