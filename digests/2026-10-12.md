## Notable Papers & Research

### OpenWAM: An Open Framework for Composable World-Action Models
**Heng Yu, David D. Yuan, Juze Zhang, Changan Chen, Yao Feng, Michelle Baldonado, Steve Cousins, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu, Ehsan Adeli** (Stanford) ([arXiv:2610.07922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07922), submitted Oct 6; [project page](https://openwam.stanford.edu/))

The most interesting paper in the window, and it answers a question this digest has been asking for two weeks: **Fei-Fei Li is a co-author** (not lead) on a new world-action-model framework, which is the first Li-attached research output since the AMD acquisition was announced. The paper's complaint is methodological: existing world-action-model systems "vary the video backbone, interaction structure, supervision, and inference procedure simultaneously," so nobody can tell which choice did the work. OpenWAM fixes the substrate — causal robot-video pretraining on **10,000+ hours** starting from Wan2.2-5B — and then varies one thing at a time across joint, video-then-action, action-then-video and decoupled generation via a shared Mixture-of-Transformers.

The headline ablation is large: causal adaptation lifts video-then-action success on **LIBERO-Long from 68.4% to 97.8%**, and giving video and actions separate experts beats a shared DiT by **5.0 points (VTA)** and **3.0 points (Joint)**. A frozen local-context inverse dynamics model reaches **84.0% mean success** on four held-out LIBERO-90 tasks with only the video predictor adapted. Two caveats: a secondary summary reports a 5B video expert / 2B action expert trained on 334,000 clips plus a released LIBERO-Long-CF failure dataset, and we could not confirm those figures from the paper; and **there is a name collision** — [arXiv:2609.07398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07398) ("OpenWAM: An Open, Modular Exploration Towards Systematic World-Action Model Pretraining," Yuran Wang et al.) is a different, unrelated paper. Do not conflate them.

### Beyond Imitation: A Framework and Benchmark for LLM-Assisted Peer Review
**Rachel S.Y. Teo, Yutaro Yamada, Shashank Kotyan, Yuki Imajuku, Tarin Clanuwat** (Sakana AI; Teo also NUS) ([arXiv:2610.11087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11087), posted ~Oct 8; published in TMLR 07/2026; [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/10/sakana-ais-llm-peer-review-system-catches-73-of-core-claim-errors/))

Sakana's Multi-Layered Review (MLR) catches **73.43% of planted core-claim contradictions** against **~11–15% for the best baselines**, at about **$0.47 per review** on off-the-shelf API models. The benchmark is a Contradiction Benchmark of **1,164 modified variants drawn from 257 papers** across ACL, AISTATS, CVPR, ICML 2025 and NeurIPS 2024.

Read the second number, not the first. On **real** problems — documented errors in withdrawn arXiv papers — exact detection falls to **16.11%**. That is the whole finding: a system that is excellent at spotting a contradiction someone deliberately inserted is roughly four-and-a-half times worse at spotting the kind that actually gets papers withdrawn. Given the week's fight over AI-generated mathematics and the October 7 retraction of three OpenAI manuscripts over a sign error, this is the most load-bearing result of the day. Sources conflict on which models MLR runs on (one says Claude Sonnet 4 / Haiku 3.5, a Korean write-up says Gemini 2.5 Pro builds the knowledge graphs); figures are the authors' own.

### Learn2Play Bench: How Well Do LLM Agents Learn from Experience in Unfamiliar Environments?
**Yibo Li, Jinhang Qiu, Zhi Zheng, Qianyun Guo, Jiaying Wu, Shuo Ji, Bryan Hooi** ([arXiv:2610.08215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08215), Oct 6, v2 Oct 8; [GitHub](https://github.com/liushiliushi/Learn2Play-Bench))

Text games with **novel or deliberately counterintuitive rules**, built so that pretrained knowledge is a liability rather than an asset — the agent has to learn from interaction or fail. The practical finding is one line long and worth acting on: keeping **complete records of actions and feedback beats summarizing them into rules**, which is the opposite of what most agent memory layers do. Top human players still reach higher peak scores than every evaluated agent.

### TokenRouter: Efficient Serving System for Token-Level LLM Routing
**Tianyu Fu et al.** (Tsinghua NICS) ([arXiv:2610.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12242), Oct 8; [GitHub](https://github.com/thu-nics/TokenRouter))

Production routing happens at session or query granularity; token-level routing promises more but nothing serves it efficiently. TokenRouter reports **2.01–64.15× higher decoding throughput** than existing systems across routing algorithms, workloads and model pairs. The 32× spread between the low and high end of that range is doing a lot of work and the paper should be read for which configurations sit at the bottom.

### Also worth a look
- **OpenMOSS/EasyWAM** — a unified training/fine-tuning/evaluation framework for world-action models with FlashAttention 2/3/4 and LoRA support; MIT code, CC BY-NC 4.0 checkpoints. Infrastructure rather than a result, and the second world-action-model framework in this section, which is itself the signal.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Satya Nadella: frontier models need a human-controlled "emergency brake," and the controls must sit outside the model
([Nadella on X + companion essay](https://x.com/satyanadella), Oct 10; [tbreak](https://tbreak.com/nadella-ai-emergency-brake/), [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/satya-nadella-says-advanced-ai-models-need-an-emergency-brake), [AI Brief Oct 11](https://artificiallyintimidating.com/p/ai-brief-october-11-2026))

The most consequential statement of the window, because of who made it. Nadella argues that an **authorized person must always be able to pause or shut down a model mid-task**, and — the load-bearing part — that the controls governing what a model can access and do must live **outside the model, where the model cannot change or bypass them**. His framing is containment, not a kill switch: assume a model could be compromised or simply wrong, and engineer for that without having to assume malice. He pairs it with calls for model diversity, continuous testing, independent auditability, incident disclosure, and industry-agreed containment standards that scale with capability.

Why it matters more than the usual CEO safety post: Microsoft operates the largest AI deployment surface in enterprise software, and this is the first time its CEO has described models as an **insider risk class** requiring architectural containment rather than policy. It also lands directly on Google's **Gemini Agent** design from October 8, which gives coworker agents their own Workspace identity, email and cryptographically attested logs — identity and attestation are exactly the outside-the-model controls Nadella is describing, shipped three days before he asked for them. CNBC lines him up with Gates, Amodei, Altman and Musk; the distinguishing feature here is that he is specifying a mechanism.

### Andrej Karpathy: the LLM capability gap is "less two groups speaking past each other and more a sharp funnel"
([Karpathy on X](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2109361546505966046), Oct 11; [aifront-page](https://aifront-page.com/andrej-karpathy-ai-divide-llm-users/), [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-karpathy-puts-internal-frontier-ai-access-at-about-5000))

Karpathy dusted off an April tweet to say the gap in shared understanding of what LLMs can do is **widening**, and gave his napkin math for the funnel: **~6B people (~75%)** have barely come into contact with an LLM; **1–2B (~20%)** are casual, infrequent free-tier users; **~20M (0.2%)** have seen first-hand that large, complex projects can now be completed by agents from a prompt; and **~5,000** have access to frontier-grade systems inside labs.

The figures are his own estimates and should be quoted as such. What makes it worth the slot is the shape of the claim rather than the arithmetic: Karpathy — now at Anthropic leading pre-training research — is describing a world where the people with the information required to reason about AI governance number in the thousands, and the people who will live with the results number in the billions. Set it against Nadella the same week and the pair reads as a single argument: the brake is being designed by, and for, a population that can see the vehicle.

Consistent with his October 2 position that we will spend far more time trying to understand model outputs than producing them, and his ~October 5 line that **"you can outsource thinking but not understanding,"** quoted in the comment thread on Terence Tao's October 5 ["The Future of Mathematics"](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/05/the-future-of-mathematics) post. Standing correction, fourth day running: the third-party claim that Karpathy "released a prompt" turning Opus 5.5 into a job-finishing graph remains from an unaffiliated account and remains unverified — treat as false absent his own post.

([TIME exclusive](https://time.com/article/2026/10/08/senate-investigation-ai-data-centers/), Oct 8; [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/senate-investigation-says-that-some-ai-data-center-claims-are-misleading-senators-question-number-of-permanent-jobs-projects-bring-to-communities-but-companies-refuse-to-divulge-data), [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/senate-investigation-says-ai-data-144000973.html))

Legislatively the picture is a stall: the bipartisan **Ratepayer Protection Act** passed the House 417–3 in September but failed a Senate procedural vote **57–43**; **Sanders and Ocasio-Cortez** have introduced an **AI Data Center Moratorium Act**; **Markey** has reintroduced the AI Environmental Impacts Act and released a discussion draft of a national data-center framework. Caveats: most coverage traces to the single TIME exclusive, we could not reach the report itself, and no Republican response appears in the record — these are the senators' own characterizations. Read against Finland's order halting Google's site work on environmental-review grounds last week, the pattern is the same one twice: the binding constraint on AI buildout is turning out to be utilities and land-use regulators, not AI law.

Same org as `storytold/artcraft`, featured here October 9–10.

**2. [dexmal/opendm](https://github.com/dexmal/opendm)** — **~2,200 stars**, Python, Apache-2.0

**3. [OpenMOSS/EasyWAM](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/EasyWAM)** — star count not reliably sourced
A unified framework for training, fine-tuning and evaluating World Action Models — model implementation, data processing, distributed training and evaluation in one codebase, with native **FlashAttention 2/3/4** and LoRA from training through merged inference. A September update added dynamic batched evaluation that cuts evaluation GPU memory substantially. **Why it's here:** it is the second WAM *framework* to surface in a single digest alongside Stanford's OpenWAM, and that is the signal — the world-action-model field is building shared tooling, which is what a research area does right before results become comparable. License split matters for anyone evaluating it: **MIT code, CC BY-NC 4.0 checkpoints**; the released checkpoint builds on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B. Listed without a star figure rather than with an unsourced one.

**4. [odysseus-dev/odysseus](https://github.com/odysseus-dev/odysseus)** — **~92.7k stars, ~1.5k forks**, AGPL-3.0
A self-hosted AI workspace bundling an IMAP/SMTP inbox with triage, tags, summaries, reminders and reply drafts, plus notes, tasks and calendar. **Why it's here, with a caveat:** this one fails the velocity test and is included for a different reason. Launched by **PewDiePie** on May 31, it hit 30k stars in two days, 87k by September 12 and ~92.7k by October 8 — **under 6k in four months**, a decelerating curve on a huge base, exactly the profile this digest's rules deprioritize. Note the repo's default branch is `dev` and may be unstable; use `main`. Sources conflict on license (AGPL-3.0 vs MIT) and on whether the canonical home is `odysseus-dev` or `pewdiepie-archdaemon`.
