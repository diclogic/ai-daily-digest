## Notable Papers & Research

- **"The Deterministic Horizon: When Extended Reasoning Fails and Tool Delegation Becomes Necessary"** — Dongxin Guo (University of Hong Kong). Proves via an Attention Bottleneck Theorem that decoder-only LLMs hit a hard accuracy wall at 19–31 reasoning steps, after which tool-augmented reasoning (86–94% accuracy) vastly outperforms neural chain-of-thought (24–42%). Accepted as a main conference paper at ICML 2026. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00376))

- **"World Models: A Comprehensive Survey of Architectures, Methodologies, Reasoning Paradigms, and Applications"** — Comprehensive survey accepted by IEEE Access covering the architectures, training methods, and applications of world models — the paradigm Yann LeCun's AMI Labs is betting on as the successor to LLMs. Timely given the growing debate between scaling-era approaches and post-scaling research. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/current))

- **"LLM Reasoning Is Latent, Not the Chain of Thought"** — Argues that LLM reasoning should be studied as latent-state trajectory formation rather than faithful surface chain-of-thought, directly challenging the assumption that visible reasoning traces are the mechanism behind model performance. Complements the Deterministic Horizon paper's finding that CoT breaks down on deterministic tasks. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15726))

## Model & Product Launches

### Fable 5 & Mythos 5 — Day 3: The Amazon Backstory and Cybersecurity Pushback

The Fable/Mythos crisis deepened with two major revelations:

**The trigger was Amazon, not Pliny.** A Fortune investigation revealed that Amazon CEO Andy Jassy made a late-night call to senior White House officials after Amazon researchers used prompts to bypass Mythos's cybersecurity safeguards, extracting restricted information about cyberattack techniques. Jassy's warning — not the viral Pliny the Liberator jailbreak on June 10 — was the proximate cause of Commerce Secretary Lutnick's June 12 export control directive. The government also suspected a Chinese-linked group had exploited the same bypass, though Anthropic says the White House never raised this directly. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/14/how-a-warning-from-amazon-led-the-white-house-to-shut-down-anthropics-mythos-model/))

**Cybersecurity leaders are pushing back.** A group led by former Facebook CSO Alex Stamos sent a letter to the Trump administration arguing that the security capability that alarmed the White House — generating proof-of-concept exploit code — exists across GPT-5.5, Claude Opus, Claude Sonnet, and Chinese models like Kimi 2.7. Stamos, now CPO at Corridor, argues this capability is essential for defensive security teams and the ban is counterproductive. Anthropic's senior staff, including co-founder Tom Brown and policy chief Sarah Heck, continue in-person talks with White House officials this week. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/15/anthropic-fable-security-leaders-trump-admin) | [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/14/anthropic-white-house-mythos-fable))

**Collateral damage: Karpathy barred.** The export directive bars all non-U.S.-citizen Anthropic employees from accessing the models. Andrej Karpathy, who joined Anthropic's pre-training team under Nick Joseph in May, is a Slovak-Canadian citizen and cannot access the very models his team is building. ([Blockchain News](https://blockchain.news/flashnews/anthropic-karpathy-barred-top-ai-model))
