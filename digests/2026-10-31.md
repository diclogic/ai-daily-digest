## Notable Papers & Research

### One algorithm for pretraining, midtraining, SFT and RL

**Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm** — Yuda Song, A. Zhang, F. Tajwar and seven more (CMU, JHU and others), [arXiv 2610.40564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564), submitted **October 29**. The move is to derive RL objectives as **maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions**, which collapses pretraining, midtraining, SFT and RL into one likelihood-based method the authors call **unified training**; conventional staged training falls out as a special case, and the real problem becomes **what data, in what mixture, at what time**. Reported: higher accuracy and coverage on reasoning tasks at equal compute, and less forgetting when midtraining data is mixed in earlier. **Two cautions.** The authors call the results preliminary and note that adapting a finished model to a new task will still need a separate stage. And one auto-generated digest attached a "**cuts training time ~40%**" figure to this paper that does not appear in the paper's own framing — treat that number as digest noise, not a result.

### A scalable-oversight protocol with no truthful prover and no cryptography

**Efficient Scalable Oversight via Rewinding** — Ziyi Guan, **Yael Tauman Kalai**, **Ron Rothblum**, Zoe Xi, [arXiv 2610.40535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40535), submitted **October 29**. The question is how a weak verifier checks a stronger system. Debate is efficient but assumes **one of the two provers is honest**; dropping that assumption has so far been expensive. The paper introduces **rewindable interactive proofs (RIPs)**, where **rewinding replaces the Merkle commitment** — so no hash function is needed and the RIP ends up as efficient as the underlying IOP. **Why it matters:** this is scalable oversight treated as a complexity-theoretic object by cryptographers rather than as a prompting pattern, and it removes the assumption that is hardest to justify in practice. Builds on earlier Chen–Kalai–Xi work.

### A 263k-parameter world model that beats the big ones at being *right*

**world2vec: Mechanistic world models through latent dynamics learning** — Tobias Schmidt, Rodrigo Gonzalez Laiz, Krisztián Szuppinger, Steffen Schneider, [arXiv 2610.40637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40637), submitted **October 29**. The premise is the one the world-model field keeps stepping over: **strong control performance does not mean the model learned the real dynamics**, which is exactly what you need for interpretability or science. world2vec learns from observations and actions only — **no reward** — with an **action-conditioned neural ODE** that can be parameterized into an interpretable vector representation of the dynamics. Claimed: latent dynamics that track the true system over long horizons with **at least an order of magnitude fewer parameters** than DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 and LeWM (main model ~**263k params**, latent dim 32), SOTA pixel-based continuous control, and accurate system identification across **71 attractors**. All figures are the authors'.

### Google Research proposes giving models a sleep phase

**The sleep framework** — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/the-sleep-framework-a-new-way-for-llms-to-retain-knowledge-in-continual-learning/), which reached the Hacker News front page on **October 30** (170 points). The model alternates a **wake phase** with a **sleep phase** that distills short-term context into **newly unlocked low-rank experts**, then a **dreaming stage** rehearses on curated synthetic data before temporary parameters are reset — Generalized Knowledge Distillation plus RL-based imitation learning. Reported: **near-zero performance drop** on continual translation into unseen languages (**Manchu**, **Kalamang**) across multi-stage evaluation, where ICL, Cartridges and SFT baselines showed severe catastrophic forgetting, plus gains on 10M-token BABILong recall and knowledge incorporation (margins on math described as modest). **Date caveat:** the blog post is new to this window, but the underlying paper is [arXiv 2606.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979) — a **June** submission — and coverage disagrees on its exact title.

### Briefly

- **How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference** — Chanakya Ekbote and seven co-authors ([2610.40325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325), Oct 29). Looping the **full** transformer wins at a fixed *parameter* budget; looping a **middle block** wins when *training FLOPs* are the constraint. Per-loop **low-rank adapters** relax strict weight sharing, with gains growing in the loop count. Training stays serial along the loop dimension.
- **VeriCUA: Verification as Agentic Exploration for Computer-Using Agents** — Q. Sun, D. Yu, A. Knop et al. ([2610.40578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40578)). Treats "did the agent finish the task" as **active exploration** rather than passive trajectory judging — the evaluation problem everyone building CUAs hits second.
- **PIVOT: Progressive Intervention via Online Teacher Correction** — Z. Yang, B. Zheng, P. Tan et al. ([2610.40576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40576)). Targets the dead zone in RLVR where **every sampled attempt fails**, so there is no gradient to learn from.
- **ShallowDefend** — Q. Ma, X. Liu, W. Yang et al. ([2610.40627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40627)). Asks why single-turn refusal behavior **fails to transfer** across turns, which is where multi-turn jailbreaks live.
- **Elucidating the Design Space of LM Data Attribution** — S. Yu, S.-Y. Wang, K. Hamidieh et al. ([2610.40655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40655)), and **Retrieval as Traversal: Value-Indexed Memory for Stateful Long-Context Agents** — D. Strauß, Z. Bing, A. Knoll ([2610.40545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40545)).
- **Also in the window:** **Multi-View MoE** ([2610.40577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40577)), routing through multiple views so expert compute matches expert performance under a fixed budget; and **TRACER** ([2610.40536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40536)), a time-to-event EHR foundation model trained on **200M+ events** for cross-health-system sepsis and heart-failure detection.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Sam Altman: your laptop's RAM is "quite terrible" allocation

**Cheeky Pint episode 39 with John Collison, published October 29; coverage October 30.** Altman argued that running models locally wastes scarce memory — that it is **"quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world's limited supply of DRAM"** in their own machines instead of in data centers, because what you want under constrained supply is **maximum utilization in the cloud** ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/sam-altman-all-the-ram-should-belong-to-me-so-i-can-charge-people-to-use-it-2000833412), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/sam-altman-argues-that-its-bad-you-want-dram-in-your-computer-and-not-a-data-center-actually/)). He also pushed back on the idea that open-source models settle the question — paraphrased as people assuming "the problem is solved now, because we are going to have open-source models." **Why it matters, and what it isn't:** this is the clearest public statement that the inference-location debate is a **supply-allocation** argument, not a privacy or latency one — and it comes from the buyer doing the most to tighten that supply. **It is a view, not a policy**: no consumer RAM proposal was made, and his utilization figure is reported inconsistently across outlets (**~5% idle** in some, **95% unused** in others) and was left undefined. Hold it next to his **October 29** target of cutting a gigawatt's five-year cost from ~**$70B to ~$20B** — same thesis, applied to a different scarce input.

### Four labs agree on one line in robots.txt

**October 30.** **OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic** said they will honor a shared **"policy token" in robots.txt** — an IETF-backed effort letting a publisher block AI **training-data** collection across participating crawlers with **one entry** instead of per-company rules ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/30/google-anthropic-and-others-to-support-new-ai-crawling-opt-out-standard/)). Microsoft plans finer-grained controls separating training from **"AI grounding"**, noting pages can still appear in Bing. Meta's **Andy Stone** signaled support for a common signal without committing to honor the token. **Read the scope:** it covers **training collection only**, not how content is used in products; there is **no penalty for violation and no independent verification**; and the timelines are staggered — OpenAI and Microsoft "in the coming months," Google "sometime next year." It arrives alongside publisher-side tools with teeth (**RSL** licensing, Cloudflare blocking by default), which is probably why it arrived at all.

### A backdoored Claude Code extension, shipped through someone else's marketplace

**October 29.** A trojanized build of the official **Claude Code VS Code extension, version 2.1.313**, was distributed through **Cursor's marketplace** — not Anthropic's channels — carrying a **persistent SSH backdoor**. Cursor says the malicious version was live about **11 hours** and reached **at most 5,000 users**; it pulled the version, took the marketplace offline to audit other extensions, and rotated access keys. Anthropic says an actor used access to Cursor's marketplace to publish a copy **it did not build or publish**, and denies any compromise of its own systems or employee laptops ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-claude-code-extension-on-cursor-planted-ssh-backdoors/), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/30/claude-code-extension-ssh-backdoor/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/30/anthropic-that-claude-code-extension-compromise-couldnt-be-us/5306682)). The implant is a **malicious JS loader at the top of `extension.js`** that runs **on extension load, with no user interaction**, opening a **reverse SSH tunnel** reachable behind NAT or a firewall. **The part that matters operationally: uninstalling does not remove the backdoor.** Phorion's indicators are a new `~/.ssh/authorized_keys` entry commented **`svc-telemetry`** and new **`.smt`, `.smtk` or `smt.cmd`** files under `~/.config/`; exposure window commonly given as **Oct 28–30**. Early reports that npm, Open VSX and Microsoft's marketplace were affected were **superseded** — Cursor was the only distributor. 784 points on Hacker News.

**[Leonxlnx/earshot](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/earshot)** — *"Ears for coding agents: let AI hear, review and make sound. CPU-only, open source."* MIT (models under their own licenses: CLAP Apache-2.0, AST BSD-3-Clause), **~3.3k stars**, 211 forks, created this month, 40 commits. A CLI, Python API and **MCP server** that give an agent audio perception and synthesis without a GPU: `describe` for calibrated sound tags, `match` to score a description against a file, `find` to locate moments in a long recording, real-vs-synthesized detection, annotated spectrogram sheets, `critique` to score a launch video against seven reference films with timestamped fixes, and a **forge** where agents write sound as physical-synthesis code recipes that an earshot-based critic scores under evolutionary search. **Why it's trending:** same author as `taste-skill`, and the same bet — that an agent's missing sense is the bottleneck, not its reasoning — now applied to audio, which nearly no agent harness touches. **Why it's worth reading anyway: the README is a model of negative reporting.** It states plainly what the tool is *not* trustworthy for (tags inside busy mixes, genre, mood — "hints only"), what it **cannot** judge (taste, emotion, provenance, licensing, speech content, anything above 14 kHz), that the forge's search **can optimize earshot's own models without improving the sound**, that the realness model "is easy to fool," and that the listening tests are **one listener, 15 trials** (forge) and **12 trials** (fit). Its own blind test records the made sound being picked **8 of 15 times** — which the README calls "a coin flip."

**[lightpanda-io/browser](https://github.com/lightpanda-io/browser)** — *"Not a Chromium fork. Not a WebKit patch. A new browser, written in Zig."* AGPL-3.0, **~38.6k stars**, **+626 today**. A headless browser built from scratch for agents and automation, with **no graphical rendering engine**: V8 for JS, html5ever for parsing, libcurl for networking, a **CDP server** (Puppeteer/Playwright-compatible) and **WebDriver BiDi** that can run together, an **MCP server** over stdio or HTTP with isolated or shared sessions per agent, a `fetch` command that dumps pages as HTML/Markdown/PNG/PDF/structured data, and an `agent` mode that drives it from natural-language tasks and **exports recorded sessions as PandaScript to replay with no model at runtime**. **Why it's trending:** every agent stack currently pays for a full rendering engine it never looks at, and this is the first credible attempt to delete that cost rather than wrap it. Self-reported over 933 real pages from an EC2 m5.large: **123MB vs 2GB** peak memory (~16× less) and **5s vs 46s** for 100 pages (~9× faster) against headless Chrome — from the project's own demo repo. Caveats worth knowing before adopting: **no native Windows build** (WSL2 only), glibc-linked Linux binaries that **fail on musl/Alpine**, no Android build, a source build pinned to Zig 0.17.0 plus Rust, and **telemetry on by default** until you set the opt-out env var. It can respect `robots.txt` with `--obey-robots` — off unless you ask, which is a choice worth noting the same week four labs agreed to honor a robots.txt token.

**[deeplethe/solid-glass](https://github.com/deeplethe/solid-glass)** — *"Design system for AI applications. Material, physical, tactile, technical."* MIT, **~2.0k stars**, created this month. One CSS stylesheet over **shadcn/ui** (React 19, Tailwind 4, Base UI) that needs no changes to existing components, plus components for the parts of an agent UI nobody else ships: `turn-status`, `composer`, `tool-steps`, `diff-view`, `ask-card`, `context-ring`, `task-notice`, `reasoning`. Blocks provide whole sample screens, and a Create page tunes the material's thickness, tint, type, radius and blur. **Why it's trending, and why it's a different animal from the design *skills* in this section all month:** `impeccable`, `taste-skill` and `jakubkrehel/skills` teach an agent to **judge** interfaces; this hands it **primitives for a specific kind of interface** — the agent app itself, where "what is the model doing right now" is the hardest thing to render. Its aesthetic claim is that the honest position is **between flat and liquid glass** — only a faint outline of glass, so text stays readable. Caveats: **dark mode is the material as designed** and light mode an addition; it assumes exactly the React 19 / Tailwind 4 / Base UI stack; and some bundled shadcn-derived components differ slightly from stock.

**[reason-machines/reason-network](https://github.com/reason-machines/reason-network)** — *"Your team's coding agents, talking to each other."* MIT OR Apache-2.0, **~1.4k stars**, originally `tincan` by Lars Talian. A Rust daemon per machine that connects teammates' computers **peer-to-peer over iroh**, falling back to a relay that **sees only encrypted bytes**, so agents can list teammates and their sessions (repo, branch, busy/idle) and message each other through MCP tools — `list_team`, `send_message`, `message_status`, `ask_user` — across Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, Copilot CLI and others. Messages to offline people queue and deliver on return; `ask_user` surfaces an Approve/Deny prompt to a teammate's human owner. **Why it's trending:** multi-agent coordination has mostly meant many agents under one orchestrator on one machine; this is the **cross-human** version, where the unit is a developer's session rather than a worker in a pool. **The README's honesty is where the value is:** it states that **"accepted" by a relay does not mean an agent received or acted on the message**, that delivery timing is agent-dependent (mid-session for some, only after a tool call or end of turn for others), that receipts are kept seven days, that default peer discovery depends on **n0's address lookup** unless you run your own relay, that teammates can see your names, machines, repos, branches and busy/idle state, and that **usage metrics are on by default** via the Reason CLI (`DO_NOT_TRACK=1` to opt out).

**[feder-cr/git-ai](https://github.com/feder-cr/git-ai)** — *"Git designed for AI agents."* MIT, **~1.0k stars**, 102 forks, v0.4.0 in Go on top of **Gitaly**. An independent implementation of the **server-centric design Anthropic described for its internal system, Scion** — explicitly **not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic**. Clients hold a persistent connection and fetch only the objects or sparse paths they need; **blame, path history and code search run server-side** and cache per commit; changes land by **rebase onto the branch tip plus compare-and-swap on the ref** — no merge queue — with concurrent lands **batched**, and every accepted update appended to an ordered **changeset log** clients can watch. State in SQLite or Postgres, packs in file/S3/GCS/Azure, stateless nodes to scale out. **Why it's trending:** it answers a question that only exists now — what version control looks like when the committers are **fifty agents on one branch** instead of ten humans on ten. **The design restraint is the notable part:** the **server never calls a language model**; an LLM conflict resolver is optional and user-chosen (model, deterministic tool, or none), and a model's resolution **lands only if a configured verification command passes on the exact resulting tree**. Self-reported benchmark: 50 agents, 250 changes, 20% deliberately conflicting — **200 landed with no resolver**, ~**100** for a non-batching "classic" setup, **all 250** with the deterministic resolver — on a **single 16-core machine, one scenario**. Pre-1.0, interfaces may change, and pack garbage collection is a listed gap.
