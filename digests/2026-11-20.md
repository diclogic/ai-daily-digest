## Notable Papers & Research

### Machine learning doubles the known atlas of topological quantum field theories

**"Learning the Anyon Atlas"** — Jonah Berean-Dutcher, Clay Córdova, Julio Parra-Martinez, Richard Tuddenham, Zekai Zhu, [arXiv:2611.24752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24752) (posted **November 18–19**, hep-th, cross-listed cs.LG).

The search for modular data of 2+1-dimensional topological quantum field theories is extended **from rank 22 to rank 42 anyons**, yielding **150,679 new modular data sets**; the authors estimate roughly **80% of ranks 23–42** is now identified after closure under known constructions. The machine learning is deliberately modest and worth noting for that reason — **three rejection gates, two of them logistic regressions**, that discard SL(2,ℤ) representations unlikely to produce valid modular data, making an otherwise intractable enumeration tractable. Along the way they find **counterexamples to a Geiko–Moore conjecture** on time-reversal symmetry in Chern-Simons theories, propose a refined criterion, and exhibit **prime modular data at rank 100 realized by no Chern-Simons theory**. Data through rank 42 is published at **AnyonAtlas.org**. This is the shape of ML-in-science that tends to stick: not a model that replaces the mathematician, but a classifier that prunes a search space by orders of magnitude.

### If everyone fine-tunes the same base model, do all the copies fail together?

**"Monoculture Robust Learning"** — Lee Cohen (Stanford), Jon Kleinberg (Cornell), Omer Reingold (Stanford), [arXiv:2611.24881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24881) (submitted **November 18**, cs.LG).

A clean theoretical treatment of the risk everyone gestures at and nobody formalizes: **algorithmic monoculture**, where models built on shared components fail on the same inputs. Working in PAC learning, the authors ask whether a single learner given a **single dataset** can emit **k** individually accurate hypotheses whose **joint** failure rate matches what k independent datasets would buy — measured as *conjunctive error*, in expected and individual variants. The results hinge on a link to **replicability** (Impagliazzo et al., 2022): if the base learner is **ρ-replicable**, running it k times independently on the same sample drives individual conjunctive error **down exponentially in k**; the matching lower bound says hitting a target error γ can demand a **sample-size multiplier on the order of log(1/γ)**. The practical reading: correlated failure across a fleet of derived models is not inevitable, but decorrelating it is something you pay for in data.

### A model that gets generally better can relearn what you withheld from it

**"General progress substitutes for specific training data in language models"** — Mark Marosi, Peter Antal, [arXiv:2611.24844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24844) (posted **November 18–19**, cs.CL/cs.AI).

Across chess, Othello, molecular strings, bracket languages and web text, the authors withhold a skill's data and find that **progress on the rest of the corpus substitutes for it**: models recover the withheld capability from a **small fraction** of the data they would otherwise have needed, and that fraction **shrinks as the models improve**. The headline demonstration is a programming language removed from a **7B** model's training text and recovered the same way, and the **delay** a withheld skill suffers in larger models was **predicted from smaller ones**. If it holds up, this is an uncomfortable result for anyone treating data exclusion — licensing carve-outs, capability withholding, safety-motivated filtering — as a durable lever: scale appears to route around the hole.

### Chiron: predicting individuals rather than role-playing them

**"A Foundation Model to Measure and Predict Human Behaviour"** — Haoran Ye, Tianrui Huo, Hangzhen Li, Xin Zhang, Guojie Song (Peking University), [arXiv:2611.24830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24830) (submitted **November 18**, cs.CL). [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24830)

The complaint is that LLMs asked to simulate people produce plausible answers that **misrepresent individuals and distort population statistics**. **Chiron** is a single fine-tuned model trained to do three things against human ground truth: estimate psychological constructs, predict individual behaviour, and match population response distributions. The corpus is the interesting part — **288,307 person records**, **1,411,750 atomic behavioural decisions** and **99,953 psychometric assessments** over **109 constructs**, spanning **36 behavioural domains** and **78 social-science experiments**. Reported gains: up to **28.4% relative improvement** over frontier LLMs on individual behaviour prediction, and **82.2–94.3% lower effect-size estimation error** when simulating social-science experiments. Architecturally it sidesteps the roleplay trap by **reading the model's hidden state** at the end of a person's records instead of generating a text answer.

### Catch-up: Claude reportedly closed two open problems in black-hole physics

**"Claude Fable 5.2 solves two open problems in the physics of black holes"** — Anthropic research post, published **November 17–18** ([anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics); [Claude on X](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2119899484373369278)). *This landed just outside yesterday's window and was not in previous digests — flagging it now.*

Two claims. First, a **Hawking–Page transition** calculation — whether the transition from hot gas to black hole stays abrupt at weak coupling — that Anthropic says had **gone uncompleted for 23 years** (secondary coverage traces it to a 2003 calculation); the reported answer is that the jump stays abrupt, which would mean black-hole formation is baked into the underlying quantum theory. Second, an exact ceiling for the **modular bootstrap** — reported as about **c/9.1266** — together with a proof that this is the best the method can achieve, which matters because it **cannot reach c/12**, where black holes first appear. Process, per Anthropic: a researcher asked Claude for open problems, got 22 candidates, and autonomous agents worked them for about **six weeks**, with results **formally verified in Lean** and checked by a physicist. **Caveats worth keeping:** sources disagree on which model did the work — some name Fable 5.2, while the modular-bootstrap preprint reportedly credits an internal research model with a later reproduction by Opus 5.5 — the preprints (2611.00174, 2611.00175) are not peer reviewed, and the second result **does not prove pure 3D gravity exists**.

### OpenAI's own numbers on how much of its research agents actually do

**"Research acceleration: The jagged frontier"** — OpenAI, published **November 18** ([openai.com](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier/)).

OpenAI reports that the share of delegated tasks its agents finish **without human intervention** rose from **23% in March to 46% in October** for work estimated at **two to four hours**, and from **13% to 35%** for four-to-eight-hour work. "Jagged" is the operative word: success **falls with task length** and swings wildly by category — one summary cites **79% on monitoring against 25% on serving code**. Reported median spend is around **$2,400 per researcher per day**, though that is at **API list prices**, not OpenAI's internal cost. Documented safety and alignment effort rose **77%** from January to September, the largest increase of any area. The company's own framing is the honest one — uneven performance means the **weakest categories become the bottleneck** — and the method (internal activity data, surveys, interviews, classification from company-visible Slack and code-change requests) is self-reported throughout.

### OpenAI says its models redesigned AMD's flagship GPU circuits for a 14% PPA gain

**"Advancing chip design with OpenAI models"** — published **November 18**, front-paged on Hacker News **November 19** ([openai.com](https://openai.com/index/advancing-chip-design-with-our-models/)).

The strongest concrete claim of AI-for-hardware to date. OpenAI's models rewrote **SystemVerilog RTL** for **AMD flagship GPU IP** and report **up to 14% power/performance/area improvement** against heavily optimized baselines — with **some changes already in a design sent for manufacturing** and others in production files for future parts. On OpenAI's own **Jalapeño** inference chip, model-proposed changes reportedly cut standard-cell area by about **13% in the vector unit** and **10% in the matrix unit**. A benchmark across model generations on two open RISC-V cores (**PicoRV32**, **Ibex**) put **GPT-6.2 Astra** — released the day before — on top. Next step: a partnership with **Synopsys** to train **GPT-Synopsys** to drive Synopsys EDA tools for design and verification. **Read the caveats:** no block-level results and no independent audit have been published; critics note the RISC-V test can flatter models, since designing from a written spec lets them **drop features** such as performance counters, and **post-synthesis timing was not checked**.

### Three more banks tell customers they own whatever their AI agent does

Following JPMorgan and Bank of America earlier this year, **Citi, PNC and Citizens** all moved on **November 18**. **Citi** added a **Section H** to its online terms under which any access or action by a third-party agent given the customer's credentials counts as **customer-authorized** — and reserved the right to block such agents **without notice**; Citi's *own* agents carry no added liability. **PNC** amended its terms to allow only bank-approved connection methods, **bar sharing passwords or one-time codes** with third-party agents, and generally prevent agents from initiating payments. **Citizens** changed nothing but emailed customers a warning that they are responsible for what their assistants do. **The open question is legal, not contractual:** US rules requiring banks to investigate unauthorized electronic transfers and cap consumer fraud liability **cannot simply be waived by agreement** — their application to a customer-authorized agent is untested. ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383677/citi-customers-responsible-ai-banking-agents-rogue-hack))
