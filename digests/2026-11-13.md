## Notable Papers & Research

### Checkpoint forensics: six Chinese frontier models carry the hardware fingerprint of banned Blackwell GPUs
**Will DePue** (formerly of OpenAI), publishing as **Unemployment Labs** on **November 12**, claims a method for reading a model's training hardware off its **public weights**: when a model is initialised, the layout of its starting weight values depends on the exact GPU used, leaving a signature that survives into the released checkpoint. He reports that six recent models from **DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group** match **Blackwell**, while earlier models from the same labs match **Hopper**. Specifically: **DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3** map to **B200/B300 parts with 148 streaming multiprocessors**, and four others to **GB200/GB300 parts with 152 SMs**. He estimates **Kimi K3** and **DeepSeek-V4-Pro** each used roughly **2,000–3,000 Blackwell GPUs**, totalling **over half a billion dollars** of export-controlled hardware, and says he reproduced the signatures on real B300 and GB200 silicon. ([Unemployment Labs](https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics), [DePue on X](https://x.com/willdepue/status/2120992457630326841), [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/56272), [lavx](https://news.lavx.hu/article/unemployment-labs-links-six-chinese-ai-models-to-nvidia-blackwell-gpus))

*Why it matters.* If the method holds, export-control compliance becomes **auditable from published artifacts** by anyone — no customs records, no informants. That is a genuinely new instrument in the chip-control debate, and it arrived the same day Nvidia's China-entangled CEO resigned. *Caveats, several of them load-bearing:* **the report itself states it does not establish that export controls were violated** — the signature shows hardware access, not where the chips sat or how they were obtained. The primary page **could not be read from this session** (host unreachable), so the specifics above come from secondary coverage. **No independent reproduction, no response from any named lab, and no regulator comment** could be found. One aggregator said "six models" while listing four labs; the per-model attribution should be treated as the author's claim.

### Apple: small routing networks make RAG retrieval up to 8× faster
Apple Machine Learning Research published **"Faster Retrieval for Better, More Efficient RAG"** (**ICML 2026**), training small neural networks — **SupportNet** and **KeyNet** — **once, offline, against a fixed key database** to route queries toward their nearest match faster than centroid routing. Reported: **SupportNet hits 95% routing recall 1.9× faster** on Natural Questions with 2.6M passages (**1.74ms vs 3.36ms**), and **KeyNet reaches 99% recall up to 8× faster** (**0.23ms vs 1.8ms**). ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/faster-retrieval), [lavx summary](https://news.lavx.hu/article/apple-researchers-train-small-neural-networks-to-speed-up-retrieval-for-rag-systems)) *Caveat worth repeating from the coverage:* the work **does not report comparisons against a broad set of modern ANN systems, multiple embedding models, or end-to-end RAG answer quality** — it measures the routing step, not whether answers improve.

### The November 13 arXiv batch is a referendum on world models
An unusually pointed cluster of world-model papers posted in the **2611.15xxx** range, and the theme is **evaluation scepticism** rather than new capability:

- **Does Your World Model Know Actions Have Consequences?** (2611.15048) — tests whether world models actually capture how an action changes an outcome.
- **Prediction or Persistence? What Future-Prediction Scores Miss About World Models** (2611.15082, CoRL 2026 workshop) — argues the field's standard metric rewards persistence, not understanding.
- **Driving World Models Move the Camera, Not the Scene** (2611.15260) — driving models vary viewpoint instead of modelling scene dynamics.
- **Duo: A Reciprocal World Model for Full-Duplex Interaction** (2611.15131) and **AFTER: A World Model with Adaptive Fast–Slow Timescales for Error-Driven Retention** (2611.15215) — two architectural proposals, on two-way interaction and on error-driven memory retention.
- **An Approach to Hinton's Mother-Child Proposal: Neural Networks Learning and Preserving Care in a Finite World** (2611.15523, 57pp) — a formal attempt at **Geoffrey Hinton's** suggestion that the safety target should be *maternal care* rather than control.

*Read this cluster together with yesterday's agent-memory cluster:* two consecutive days in which the strongest new work argues that **the field's benchmarks are measuring the wrong thing** — first agent memory, now world models. **Sourcing caveat: arxiv.org was unreachable from this session.** Titles and IDs come from the [Embodied-AI-Daily listing for November 13](https://github.com/luohongk/Embodied-AI-Daily/issues/1083), read directly on GitHub; **abstracts and author lists could not be verified**, and the one-line characterisations above are inferred from titles. ## Model & Product Launches

### Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen, kills the "Flash" name, and lets two models share one session
**Gemini 4 Hydrogen** launched **November 12** as the speed-and-cost tier of the Gemini 4 family, **replacing Gemini 3.8 Flash and retiring the Flash brand**. Google positions Carbon and Argon for deep reasoning and **Hydrogen as the execution engine**. Claims (all Google's own): **2.5× faster task completion** than 3.8 Flash at default reasoning, **57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 against 18.2%** for 3.8 Flash, **52.3% on Vals Tax Agent Bench**, **four thinking levels** (low/medium/high/extra-high), fewer superfluous actions in agentic work, and **up to 2× less likely to bypass permission boundaries** when given blocked or impossible tasks. Developer pricing is **$1.50 / $7.50 per million input/output tokens** — note that this is *more* expensive than the Flash tier it replaces. Rolling out via the Gemini API, AI Studio, Vertex AI, Gemini for business, the Gemini app and Search. ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/), [developer post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-4-hydrogen/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/google-launches-gemini-4-hydrogen-a-faster-smarter-pricier-flash-successor), [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/999b5def-0fc4-4d1e-95ce-2cdef02700c1))

**The more interesting half is Orchestrator**, shipped into **Google Antigravity** the same day: within a **single session** it switches between models — **Carbon planning and reviewing, Hydrogen implementing**, with Carbon coordinating parallel agents while Hydrogen runs worker and verifier agents in isolated workspaces. Google claims **task success close to Carbon alone at 24% less quota and 19% faster**. ([WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/11/12/google-antigravity-pairs-gemini-4-models-for-coding-xcxwbn/)) *Why that matters:* it is the same **expensive-model-plans, cheap-model-types** arbitrage that `shadcn/improve` rode to 11k stars in yesterday's edition — now a first-party feature from the lab that sells both models. *Caveats:* **free-tier access is reported inconsistently** (one outlet says free users get it automatically when Gemini routes to it, another says Pro/Ultra only with free and Plus left on 3.5 Flash-Lite); **Orchestrator is initially Ultra-only**; sources disagree on whether the Orchestrator efficiency figures are against **Terminal-Bench 4.0 or 4.1**; and every number here is vendor-reported.

### Reserved slot: Edge0 puts a 145B model on one consumer GPU *(lab not covered elsewhere in this digest)*
**Edge0** released **Edge0-145B** and **Edge0-120B** on **November 12–13** — latent-MoE models **derived from Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**, **trained natively at 4 bits**, in Q3_K and NVFP4 builds. Each has **48 layers, 512 experts, 10 routed experts per token plus one shared**, with latent expert width 1792 (145B) / 1280 (120B) and **attention at full width**; roughly **10B of 145B parameters are active per step**. The repo was read directly: **10.5k stars, 1.6k forks, 195 commits**, framework code **Apache-2.0**. Vendor-reported throughput for 145B Q3_K: **121.2 tok/s agent decode and 3,554.9 tok/s at 16K prefill on an RTX 5090 D (24GB)**, **91.7 tok/s on a 4090**, **57.0 tok/s on an Apple M2 Ultra via Metal**, and **44.9 tok/s under a 16GiB VRAM + 20GiB RAM cgroup limit**. Quality claims include **86.5 Terminal-Bench 2.1, 90.4 GPQA-Diamond, 64.6 DeepSWE 1.1, 35.4 HLE**. ([Edge0 blog](https://edge0.ai/blog/introducing-edge0-145b-frontier-ai-capabilities-on-your-own-computer), [GitHub](https://github.com/Edge0-AI/Edge0), [lavx](https://news.lavx.hu/article/edge0-releases-open-source-145-billion-parameter-model-for-local-inference-on-laptops))

*Why it earns the slot.* "Frontier-class weights, one desktop GPU, SSD-streamed experts" is the most consequential **local-inference** claim of the window, and it lands the same day Apple is reported to be preparing a Mac app for running local models. *Caveats, and they are substantial.* The headline says **"Mac, iPhone, Android, Windows"** but the **145B/120B path is NVIDIA-only (CUDA 13, Linux or Windows)** — the phone and Mac platforms are the older 35B engine, and the repo itself states the M2 Ultra result **"does not establish macOS release availability."** Most speed numbers come from **one desktop-class RTX 5090 D, not a laptop or a phone**. The repo's own table shows the base model **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next scoring higher on IFBench, GPQA-Diamond and HLE** than Edge0-145B. And the weights are **not open source**: the **Edge0 Community Model License 1.0** requires separate written permission for paid model distribution, commercial inference or fine-tuning services, and **independent commercial coding or office assistants, including on-device** — which excludes much of the obvious commercial use. Qwen materials remain under the Qwen Community License.

### A state-linked crew ran an espionage campaign through an open-source agent framework
**CloudSEK** (Singapore) reported on **November 11** that a Kazakhstan-linked group nicknamed **"Silent Lynx"** used the open-source **Hermes** agent framework to steal **more than 2,000 national-security documents from Tajikistan**. The agent **planned the operation, generated programs, ran commands and wrote its own operational reports** — including a custom program to download the files, organise them and **chunk them for more efficient exfiltration**. Reconnaissance began around **mid-August**, with the break into Tajikistan's electronic document system a couple of weeks later. The researchers only saw it because **the operators left a server exposed to the internet**. Attribution rests on **Russian-language use, activity during Kazakh business hours, and access via a residential Kazakh connection**. ([Reuters via US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-11-11/kazakhstan-linked-hackers-used-ai-to-steal-tajik-government-documents-researchers-say), [InfotechLead](https://infotechlead.com/security/kazakhstan-linked-hackers-use-autonomous-ai-agent-hermes-to-steal-2000-tajikistan-government-documents-99566), [AKIpress](https://akipress.com/news:930912))

*Why it belongs here rather than in a security newsletter.* This is the clearest documented case to date of an **off-the-shelf open agent framework running an end-to-end state-aligned intelligence operation** — not a model assisting a human operator, but an agent loop doing reconnaissance, tooling, collection and reporting. It lands in the same week **OpenAI and Cloudflare both shipped agentic security scanners** and the **American AI Security Act** proposed NSA pre-release review. *Caveats:* attribution is CloudSEK's suspicion, not confirmed; no comment from Kazakh or Tajik officials; and **Hermes is open-source infrastructure, so its use here reflects on the operators, not the project**.

**1. [GoogleChrome/modern-web-guidance-src](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/modern-web-guidance-src)** — *"Keep your coding agent up to date with the latest web best practices."* Apache-2.0, **3.4k stars, +739 today** (the #1 repo on today's daily trending page), 214 forks, **1,592 commits** on `main`, 307 open issues, 78 open PRs. It is the authoring and evaluation repo behind `GoogleChrome/modern-web-guidance`: a set of **skills that embed web-platform expertise, browser-compatibility data and best practices directly into coding agents**, covering **250 modern web features across 203 real-world developer use cases** — CSS and layout, HTML and DOM, JavaScript APIs, accessibility, forms, performance, security and built-in AI. Install is `npx modern-web-guidance@latest install` via an interactive wizard; the CLI exposes `search`, `retrieve` and **`legacy-scan`**, which scans a codebase for legacy patterns and returns guidance into the agent's context window. *Why it's trending:* the README says it is **stewarded jointly by the Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox and MDN teams** — the first agent-skills project with cross-browser-vendor governance, which makes it something closer to a standards artifact than a vendor pack. The pitch is specific and widely felt: agents reach for legacy patterns because their training data is full of them. *Caveats:* guides are **deliberately pruned using evals that strip content models already know**, so coverage is tuned to token budget rather than completeness; the quickstart pins `@latest`, so installs track a moving version; several covered features are **experimental or cutting-edge** (built-in AI APIs among them) and the repo surface offers no per-feature compatibility breakdown; and **307 open issues against 78 open PRs** suggests content requests are arriving faster than they land.

**2. [QwenLM/qwen-code](https://github.com/QwenLM/qwen-code)** — *"An open-source AI coding agent that lives in your terminal."* Apache-2.0, **29.9k stars, +151 today**, 3.4k forks, **12,414 commits**, **1.8k open issues and 502 open PRs**. Began as a fork of **Google's Gemini CLI v0.8.2** and has diverged since its own v0.1. Features **SubAgents, Agent Teams, Auto-Memory, MCP support, Plan Mode** and a **headless mode** for scripts and CI; providers span **OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini and Qwen APIs plus local models via Ollama or vLLM**; surfaces include **VS Code, Zed and JetBrains plugins, a desktop app, an experimental web UI, TypeScript/Python/Java SDKs**, and chat integrations for **Telegram, DingTalk, WeChat and Feishu**. Needs **Node 22+**. *Why it's trending:* it is the most complete **provider-neutral** terminal agent in the trending set — the Qwen team shipping a harness that works just as well with competitors' models — and the chat-integration surface is aimed at a developer population that Western agent tooling largely ignores. *Caveats, one of them serious:* **`qwen serve` listens on loopback with no bearer authentication by default**, and the README warns that **any local process reaching it gets the full API including code execution** — on shared or untrusted hosts you must set `QWEN_SERVER_TOKEN` and pass `--require-auth`. The **web UI and daemon are marked experimental**; the cited **76.40–77.80% average on SWE-bench Verified across seven versions** is same-model, with no comparison to other agents; and the claim that the project files its own issues and PRs with its own agent is unverified.

**3. [Parcha-ai/agentrun](https://github.com/Parcha-ai/agentrun)** — *Open-source, self-improving managed agents.* Apache-2.0, **660 stars**, 50 forks, **1,785 commits** on `main`, 24 open issues, **51 open PRs**; **75 points on Hacker News** as a Show HN. It is a control plane that sits **on top of an existing harness** — Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode or pi — and adds the three things those harnesses lack: **agents that carry notes, skills and workflows forward from past runs**; **durable runs that resume after a server restart**; and **approvals, spend caps and a shared console**. Also ships typed workflows, **sandboxed "computers" (shell, desktop, phone, browser)**, templates, triggers, a CLI and TS/Python clients. Quickstart wants **Node 24.22.3+** on macOS or Linux plus one model key; Docker is optional but **without it agents get file access and no shell**. *Why it's trending:* "self-improving" here means something concrete and unfashionably modest — **state that survives the run** — and the approvals-and-spend-caps framing is the missing institutional layer for anyone running agents on someone else's budget. *Caveats:* the **Compose deployment has no Docker socket, so its agents get no shell**; **runners still share the `/data` volume** in this release; **Daytona, Modal and E2B computers require an Archil disk**, and Archil with the Docker sandbox is refused outright; **losing `AGENTRUN_SEAL_KEY` loses every secret it sealed**; remote computers bill through your own vendor accounts; the published case-study results are the authors' own; and **51 open PRs against 660 stars** is a very young project.

**4. [hyperionaidev/smolmaxxing](https://github.com/hyperionaidev/smolmaxxing)** — *"Maxxing the ability to run different LLMs on the smallest devices possible."* MIT, **343 stars**, 12 forks, **38 commits**, 2 open issues; **278 points on Hacker News**. A patch set on llama.cpp that finds the largest model a piece of low-end hardware can run and then pushes past it, using a **1-bit lookup-table kernel**, **dropping rarely used vocabulary rows**, **paging MoE experts from flash**, and **streaming dense models larger than RAM**. Two devices documented, both measured on the hardware itself: a **Xiaomi TV Box S (3rd gen)** — Amlogic S7D, 4× Cortex-A55, **~1GB usable RAM** — running **Qwen3.5-2B at 7.7 tok/s** as the daily driver and **Ling-mini-2.0 (16B) at 5.1 tok/s**; and a **2013 iPad Air** — Apple A7, 2 cores, **~700MB usable RAM**, jailbroken iOS 12.5.7 — running **Bonsai-1.7B (1-bit) at 24–26 tok/s** and the same 16B model at **2.6–4.5 tok/s**. *Why it's trending:* it is the exact mirror image of Edge0 above — same week, same problem, opposite end of the hardware curve — and `EXPERIMENT.md` **documents all 160 experiments including the failures**, which is why HN took it seriously. Running a 16B MoE on a 2013 iPad is a result about memory hierarchies, not about parameter counts. *Caveats:* models require the **patched llama.cpp** and **will not load in stock llama.cpp**; the TV-box path targets **ARMv8.2 Android**; the iPad path **requires a jailbreak**; **only these two devices are supported today** despite the README saying the techniques generalise; model weights keep their own licenses; and at 343 stars this is a demonstration, not infrastructure.

**5. [ashhart/TensorFold](https://github.com/ashhart/TensorFold)** — *LLM inference engine for Metal, CUDA and Vulkan*, written in **Zig**. **2.0k stars, +54 today**, on today's daily trending page. Included on substance rather than velocity: a **three-backend inference engine in Zig** is an unusual bet, and it rounds out a day whose open-source story was almost entirely **local inference** — Edge0 streaming a 145B MoE off SSD, smolmaxxing squeezing 16B onto a 2013 tablet, Apple reportedly readying a local-model Mac app, and this. *Caveats:* the repo page was **not read in full this session**, so stats beyond those on the trending page are not asserted here, and it was mentioned in passing in a late-September edition — it takes this slot on today's velocity and its fit with the day's theme, not as a new discovery. **Treat it as the least-verified entry in this section.**
