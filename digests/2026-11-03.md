## Notable Papers & Research

### Fifty agents, three hours, one Fertilizer Officer: the Civ50 report
**Y Combinator Labs with Olam Labs.** Fifty **GPT-6.1 Sol** agents were dropped into a Minecraft survival world with the goal of building a thriving civilization. They did not. Over three hours the colony logged **34 deaths** (22 of them early), exchanged roughly **5,900 messages**, and produced permits, ledgers, food rationing and distinct roles — including four agents spending **11 minutes** appointing a "sole Bone Meal operator" for a single piece of fertilizer — while never solving the problem of systematically feeding itself. The competitive runs inverted the result: across **seven Mindustry matches**, GPT-6.1 Sol swarms beat **Claude Opus 5.5** swarms **6–1**, with the GPT side behaving like a centralized hive and the Claude side running a democratic "+1" proposal vote; a nine- or ten-agent Bed Wars series also went to GPT (reported 6–3). **Why it matters:** it is the first large public swarm study where the failure mode is *organizational* rather than technical — the agents were individually competent and collectively paralyzed, which is the specific risk in multi-agent products now shipping. **Caveats, including the authors':** the researchers framed these as observations, not conclusions. One three-hour civilization run, seven Mindustry games and one Bed Wars series cannot establish that bureaucracy is inevitable or rank the models generally. Coverage is secondary and rewrites the YC write-up; one outlet says "hundreds" of agents, which contradicts the 50 in every other account. The harness is open source at `yc-software/minecraft-agent-harness`, so the replays are checkable. [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-brutal-minecraft-night-scared-50-ai-agents-into-inventing-chicken-handoff-protocols-a-fertilizer-officer-and-fishing-shifts/) · [YC on X](https://x.com/ycombinator/status/2115904922017579275) · [HN, 419 pts](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50296486)

### Reserved slot: 42AI exits stealth with a world model of people, not places *(company not covered elsewhere in this digest)*
Israeli startup **42AI** came out of stealth on **November 2** with **$62.5M in seed funding** and a *behavioral* world model — an unusual entry in a category otherwise fixated on 3D scenes and physics. The architecture turns observations into trait representations that feed a **transformer-based world model**, with a separate language model generating explanations for each prediction; the company stresses it is probabilistic rather than deterministic, and rests on the assumption that people carry stable behavioral tendencies across contexts. It is live at **10 brokerages** screening trader behavior, generating "more than seven figures" in annual revenue on flat fees or value-sharing deals, and the company is exploring gaming, fraud prevention and cybersecurity. **Why it matters:** if world models are the post-LLM bet, 42AI is testing whether the same framing works on human behavior as a dynamical system — and doing it in a domain where predictions are scored daily. **The caveats are substantial.** The headline **98% accuracy** claim is, per the CEO's own clarification, whether *aggregate trading flows* fall within a predicted range — not whether individual trades were predicted; the per-direction and per-volume **F1 scores are 0.8–0.85**, a much more modest figure. The money came from the founders and **Infinity Group** over three years rather than primarily outside investors, and Infinity was founded by the co-founder's father, who also chairs 42AI — so this is closer to a founding disclosure than a market-priced round. No valuation, brokerage names or audited revenue were disclosed, and the founders concede the model degrades when situational context is missing. Sources also disagree on the co-founder's name (Amit vs. Amir Gal-Or). [Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/42ai-secures-62-5m-in-seed-funding-for-behavioral-world-model/) · [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/israeli-startup-42ai-says-its-world-model-can-predict-human-behavior/)

### Heretic 2.0 — censorship removal becomes general-purpose model surgery
**p-e-w/heretic** shipped **v2.0.0 on November 1** (13:03 UTC), a deliberately breaking release that **replaces classic abliteration with Arbitrary-Rank Ablation (ARA)** — editing a model without first having to locate a single "refusal direction." The project is explicitly broadening from decensoring to general training-free modification: suppressing slop, reducing sycophancy and altering emotional alignment, with censorship removal remaining the default. Reported adoption is the striking part: **~38k GitHub stars, 8,500+ derived models on Hugging Face, 88M total model downloads**. **What to know before using it:** **1.x config files do not work** with 2.0 (a migration guide ships with the release), and the repo is **AGPL-3.0** — one widely-shared write-up (PromptZone) describes it as MIT and as using activation steering, both of which contradict the repository and should be disregarded. ### Altman in Abu Dhabi: AI has passed the best scientists, and there is "no room for debate" about who stays in charge
Speaking at the **ADIPEC opening ceremony on November 2**, **Sam Altman** said AI had moved over the course of this year from mostly trailing the world's best scientists to **mostly outperforming them** — immediately hedging that the comparison is "very approximate because it's spiky and it's not true in all ways." He also said there is **"no room for debate"** that people and not AI must remain in control of the future, and that AI can now do "a lot of parts" of many white-collar jobs. **Why it matters:** the two claims sit in tension by design — a capability boast and a control reassurance delivered in one speech, to an audience of energy and sovereign-wealth buyers. **One claim needs flagging:** Altman repeated on stage that OpenAI had **solved one of the Millennium Prize problems**. This is not new and is not settled. It refers to the **September Navier-Stokes result** from an unreleased internal model; OpenAI said then that it does not intend to claim the prize; mathematician **Tristan Buckmaster has accused OpenAI of academic fraud** over the credit, which Altman rejects; and the Clay Mathematics Institute said only that the problem had "apparently been settled," with evaluation still to come. The sole Millennium problem officially resolved to date remains the Poincaré conjecture. ### Marvell buys Lumentum for ~$115B in stock — the lasers, not the chips

### The NCSC puts a date on AI-enabled attacks against UK infrastructure: 2028
The UK's **National Cyber Security Centre** published its annual **State of the Cyber Nation** report **today, November 3**, judging it **highly likely** that AI-powered malware will be used in a ransomware attack on **UK critical national infrastructure by 2028**. CEO **Richard Horne** said in the launch speech that AI could find and exploit long-ignored weaknesses "at a scale that was previously unimaginable," and pointed to recent attacks on refrigeration systems in French supermarkets as evidence that hostile actors already reach civilian infrastructure. ## Trending Open-Source Projects

**[vercel-labs/agent-desktop](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-desktop)** — *"Computer use for AI agents: read and operate desktop apps through the OS accessibility tree."* Apache-2.0, Rust, **~10.5k stars**, **+2,275 today** — about **22% of its lifetime stars in one day**, the steepest curve on the board and the #1 trending repo overall and in Rust. A CLI plus C library for macOS, Windows and Linux: a snapshot returns JSON with a `ref` per actionable element, the agent acts on the ref, then re-snapshots to verify — no pixel matching, and **headless by default**, so ref actions neither move the pointer nor steal focus (`--headed` opts into physical input). **Why it's trending:** accessibility-tree automation is the credible answer to screenshot-and-click computer use, and with the same lab's `agent-browser` (45.3k stars) Vercel now has both halves of an open computer-use stack. **What the README itself constrains:** snapshots, clipboard reads and screenshots **may capture private desktop content** and should be kept out of logs and uploads, and the optional JEV helper **sends screen descriptions to a model provider**; `key-down`/`key-up`/`mouse-down`/`mouse-up` **refuse to run** until a daemon can release held inputs; refs are snapshot-bound, so stale UI returns `STALE_REF` or `AMBIGUOUS_TARGET` and multiple windows need `--window-id`; macOS needs Accessibility permission, Linux needs a graphical session with `at-spi2-core`; feature support **varies by OS**; and Homebrew and Scoop packages are **still under submission**. The README gives no first-release date.

**[Nasiko-Labs/nasiko](https://github.com/Nasiko-Labs/nasiko)** — *"The Open Runtime for AI Agents."* Apache-2.0, Rust, **~11.5k stars**, 3.1k forks, **+293 today**, 2,327 commits. Aimed at developers already running Claude Code, Codex, Cursor CLI or OpenCode who want to know **what those agents cost, by harness and model**, and optionally route their LLM calls elsewhere. `nasiko agents discover` detects installed harnesses without touching their settings; beyond metering it offers A2A-protocol agent hosting with an embedded OCI registry, a **permission-filtered MCP gateway**, encrypted per-agent secrets, human-in-the-loop pauses for input or tool approval, and **cascade limits** on depth, fan-out, token budget and cycles. **Why it's trending:** the governance layer is the gap in every multi-agent deployment, and cost attribution across harnesses is a problem teams hit on day one. **What the README states plainly:** connecting an agent **edits per-user global config** (`~/.claude/settings.json`, OpenCode's config), affecting every process on the machine; **Cursor CLI has no routing yet**; OpenCode must be restarted and existing sessions keep their old model; cost and token attribution **requires** `CODING_AGENT_OTLP_ENDPOINT` or turns are ingested but unattributed; regenerating `SECRETS_ENCRYPTION_KEY` makes existing secrets unreadable; `InMemoryTaskStore` **loses paused tasks on restart**; native Docker on Linux lacks `host.docker.internal`; and the CLI is **0.1.0** with older builds sharing that version, so `nasiko --version` cannot tell you whether a build has `agents discover`. Early-stage despite the star count — 60 open PRs against 20 open issues.

**[leepokai/Codync](https://github.com/leepokai/Codync)** — *"Message the coding agents on your own computer as chat bots, from anywhere."* Apache-2.0 (MIT through 2.11.0), Rust host with a native iPhone app, Electron desktop and TUI, **~1.8k stars**, **+206 today** — roughly **12% of lifetime stars in a day**, the best ratio among the larger-velocity repos. Runs Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, Copilot and ~40 more agents from the **Agent Client Protocol** registry as persistent bots you message from an iPhone, desktop or terminal; bots work in group chats, ask each other for help, request approvals and notify on completion, reachable remotely over a free **end-to-end encrypted relay**. **Why it's trending:** it inverts the usual remote-agent pitch — the agents stay on your machine with your existing subscriptions, and only the chat transport is hosted. **Caveats the README gives:** on **Windows** the installer is **unsigned** (SmartScreen prompts) and remote screen, on-device speech and the Claude Code status-line hook are **not yet available**; the iPhone app **trails** desktop releases because of App Review; some agents need **Node.js** (they run via `npx`); **sign-in with Apple, Google or GitHub is required** even though pairing still needs a QR or 6-digit code; updates can fail, with manual recovery steps and an explicit warning **against "Reset all data"**; the stated iOS floor **contradicts itself** (text says iOS 26+, the badge says iOS 18+); and the trademark file requires hosted or multi-tenant services to run their own relay and accounts. The README also warns that **`@trycodync` is the only official account** — impersonation is apparently already a problem.

**[kgoedecke/doop](https://github.com/kgoedecke/doop)** — *"The open-source alternative to Paper.design"* — a multiplayer design canvas where humans and agents work on the same artboards. **AGPL-3.0**, TypeScript, **~1.6k stars**, 185 forks, **+125 today** (~8% of lifetime in a day), 429 commits. Frames render HTML in sandboxed iframes; the browser side is a real editor (shapes, pen paths, text, flex layout, rulers, guides, 8%–1600% zoom) with comments, clickable prototypes, version history and an activity feed. Agents join through an **MCP server at `/mcp`** with OAuth or an agent key, acting as the approving user across **42 tools** — streaming HTML into frames, screenshotting to review their own work, reading comments, managing style guides — and appear on the canvas with **their own cursors, status and task history** that humans can follow. A server-side Doop Agent picks up queued cards and `@mentions` without a client. **Why it's trending:** agent presence as a first-class canvas object, rather than a chat sidebar, is a genuinely different answer to human-agent design collaboration. **Unusually candid README caveats:** driving a ChatGPT subscription from a third-party server **"is not something OpenAI's terms sanction,"** and heavy use may bring rate-limiting or suspension — an API-key path is the supported alternative; without a key or connected account the built-in agent is **off** and queued work just waits; **Figma, Canva and PSD import/export are Cloud-only**, not in the open-source build; models without image input run **degraded, with no visual review**; sibling-position selectors **break when HTML is restructured**; the embedded PGlite database is **single-process only** (Postgres recommended for real deployments); the iOS app is "in development"; and AGPL means modified versions offered as a service must be published under the same license, with the name and logo excluded.

**[D4Vinci/Scrapling](https://github.com/D4Vinci/Scrapling)** — *"Web scraping for everything from a single request to a full-scale crawl."* BSD-3-Clause, Python, **~93.2k stars**, 9.8k forks, **+706 today**, 1,785 commits — the #1 trending Python repo.
