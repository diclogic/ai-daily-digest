## Notable Papers & Research

The November 21 arXiv listing (covering Thursday/Friday submissions) is unusually heavy on *verification* — of hardware, of agents, and of models' own honesty.

### Verify the Verifier: specification-grounded cross-checking for bugs that pass the scoreboard
Sanchez Villalobos et al. ([arXiv:2611.26588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26588)) target a specific blind spot in chip verification: bugs that pass UVM scoreboard checks but still violate design intent. The method cross-checks RTL behaviour against the written specification with an LLM rather than against the scoreboard's own model of correctness. This lands in the same week that **Architect Labs' "Frostbite"** AI-designed CPU claim (covered yesterday, 667 points on HN) is drawing scrutiny — the gap between "it verified" and "it is correct" is exactly the gap being argued over.

### Depth Pro 2: the paper behind Monday's open-weights drop
Hu et al. ([arXiv:2611.26603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26603)) — **follow-up to yesterday's launch item.** Apple shipped Depth Pro 2 weights earlier in the window; the paper is now out, and it claims full-resolution **metric** monocular depth that is both sharp at boundaries *and* temporally consistent across video, which have historically traded off against each other. That combination is the part that matters for AR and driving stacks, where flicker between frames is as damaging as absolute error.

### AgentRoboCritic: what frontier agents actually get wrong at direct robot control
Du et al. ([arXiv:2611.26613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26613)) stop reporting success rates and start taxonomising failures when frontier agents are given low-level robot control rather than high-level planning. Paired with **Mind2Act World** ([arXiv:2611.26444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26444)), a diagnostic benchmark that deliberately separates embodied reasoning, precise execution, and the *composition* of the two, the two papers make the same argument from different directions: aggregate manipulation scores are hiding which half of the problem is broken.

### One Prior, Two Dynamics: a video generator as a free prior for latent action learning
([arXiv:2611.26617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617)) Repurposes a pretrained video diffusion transformer as the prior for self-supervised latent action learning, rather than learning action structure from scratch. This is the world-model thesis cashed out as a training shortcut — the generative video model already encodes how things move, so the action space can be read out of it. Relevant to the **world models / spatial intelligence** thread this digest tracks, and the most load-bearing of the window's world-model papers.

### The Geometry of Lies: persona directions for detecting deception
([arXiv:2611.26545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26545)) Extends a "persona bank" of linear probes to detect deception in 70B-scale models, including lies about the model's own knowledge. Worth reading against the **Claude Mythos 5.2 system card** line that the model "shows stronger covert capabilities than prior Claude models... particularly when its reasoning is hidden from the monitor" — probe-based detection is the countermeasure that claim implies is needed.

### Briefly

- **AutoMaster: autonomous post-training by making the same model a better teacher** ([arXiv:2611.26510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26510)) — introduces Skill-FT, which uses natural-language skill descriptions to steer fine-tuning so a model bootstraps off itself. A third distinct self-improvement mechanism in four days, after the self-editing-weights and weights-compilation papers in yesterday's digest.
- **SoK: Assessing the Evolution of LLM Capability in Software Vulnerability Research** ([arXiv:2611.26601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26601)) — a systematisation of how model capability at finding, validating and patching vulnerabilities has actually moved. Timely given the gated-access regime around Mythos.
- **TideCache** ([arXiv:2611.26619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26619)) — transparent KV-cache management plus layer-wise streaming for hybrid CPU–GPU superchip serving.
- **"Programming in the Machine Code Space"** ([write-up](https://sgiannopoulos.github.io/blog/machine-code-programming/), 189 points on HN) — a frontier model given only a hex dump, with no disassembler or compiler, explains compiled binaries, patches features in byte by byte, and writes working executables from scratch; outputs were required to match a GCC reference build byte for byte on stdout and exit code. The constraint is context, not capability: a hex dump costs ~2.4 tokens per byte, so a 67 KB binary ran ~163,000 tokens. *Not a peer-reviewed paper, and the specific model attribution in the write-up could not be verified.*
- **AURA** ([arXiv:2611.26540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26540)) and **PlayCraft** ([arXiv:2611.26624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26624)) — low-latency streaming audio-driven avatars, and game design driven by an agent's own play experience.

## Model & Product Launches

### Reserved slot: Thinking Machines Lab ships Inkling 2 Mini — a looped 26.4B open model that runs on a 32GB laptop *(a lab not covered elsewhere in this digest)*

Released **November 20** and the **#1 story on Hacker News across this window (1,383 points, 280 comments)**, [Inkling 2 Mini](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) is the most interesting *architectural* release of the week, not just another small model. It is a **looped transformer**: **26.4B total parameters, 3.4B active**, but the middle layers are **run three times per token**, giving roughly **5.4B of effective compute per token** at 3.4B of memory-resident activation. Weights are on Hugging Face under **Apache 2.0** with a **1M-token context window**, and it also runs on the lab's Tinker platform; the footprint is about **20 GB**, which is the whole pitch.

Thinking Machines claims it leads all open-weights models under 100B parameters on agentic coding — **48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1** vs **42.2%** for Qwen3.8-27B, and **79.2% vs 77.8%** on SWE-bench Verified (bash only) — while **losing** on Terminal Bench v2.1 (**71.6% vs 73.0%**), MultiChallenge (**50.0%**) and ComplexConstraints (**33.4%**), and tying on the Artificial Analysis Index v5.0 (**20.1** each). *All of those are self-reported.*

The genuinely novel result is the ablation, and it is the reason this is the reserved slot. Looping helps **long-context** benchmarks (GraphWalks BFS, MRCR v2) and **does not** help knowledge-intensive ones (SimpleQA Verified) — so depth-recurrence appears to buy reasoning over long traces, not stored facts. And the economics invert the usual story: a **non-looped** model trained with **7.5% less** compute needed **140% more inference compute** to match the looped version's loss on 512k-token agentic traces. The lab is also releasing a **research model that loops one to five times** so outsiders can study the effect directly — the rare case of a company shipping the knob rather than just the setting.

## Trending Open-Source Projects

All four below were **created within the last two weeks** (they surface under a `created:>2026-11-08` filter), so their totals *are* their velocity — which is the comparison config asks for. None has appeared in a previous digest. *Caveat on method: GitHub's search API and per-repo API were both unavailable from this session, so star counts are from repository and search pages rather than API snapshots, and exact per-day deltas could not be computed for these four.*

**1. [erkamyaman/rust-anything](https://github.com/erkamyaman/rust-anything)** — **7.5k stars, 601 forks, 351 commits, MIT, Rust.** "Rewrite your app in Rust, and prove it still behaves the same." It records what the original program does, has a coding agent port it one unit at a time, and **gates each unit against that recording** before moving on — `record`, `diff`, `fuzz`, `gate`, plus `bridge` to emit a Rust crate behind the original API. Works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI and OpenCode via an MCP server and a workflow skill; supports TS/JS, Python, C, Go, Ruby, Java, Kotlin. **Why it's trending:** it attacks the actual blocker on agentic migration — not generating the code, but *proving* it equivalent. Reported results are specific enough to check: Prettier **19x faster** on 2,360 files (128.6s → 6.9s) with byte-identical output, json-server idle memory **74MB → 3.5MB**, and an ffmpeg `libavutil` port matching all **226,447** recorded calls. **Note the README's own warning:** `record` executes the original code on many generated inputs, so run it on a copy with test credentials.

**2. [simplexai-labs/linger](https://github.com/simplexai-labs/linger)** — **3.5k stars, 327 forks, 712 commits, TypeScript.** An open-source personal agent pitched as "a Claude-class brain running locally on your PC" — no cloud tokens, no provider account. Its **PolyStrata** engine keeps a MoE model's hottest experts on the GPU and pages the rest to RAM/SSD, claiming a **125B model on a 12GB GPU with 64GB RAM**; a "Model Autopilot" installer picks a model that fits your hardware and verifies it. Also ships nightly compilation of mail/chat/meetings into a searchable wiki (claiming **95% on LoCoMo**), desktop and signed-in-Chrome control, agent teams coordinating through a shared `team.md`, and a bring-your-own-runtime mode for Claude Code, Codex or Pi. **Why it's trending:** it is the most complete attempt yet at the "local, proactive, personal agent" that several labs have demoed and none have shipped. **Two things to check before adopting:** the licence is a **custom "Linger Open Source License"** (Apache-2.0 with added conditions), not Apache-2.0 proper, and **86 open PRs against 712 commits** suggests review is not keeping up with contribution. All performance numbers are the project's own.

**3. [FrigadeHQ/yogurt](https://github.com/FrigadeHQ/yogurt)** — **2.9k stars, 243 forks, 479 commits, MIT, Electron.** Private AI meeting notes that never leave the machine, positioned squarely against Granola, Otter and Fireflies: it records mic plus system audio rather than **joining the call as a bot**, then transcribes with **whisper.cpp** (Apple SpeechAnalyzer on supported Macs) and summarises with local open-weight models through **llama.cpp**. Live transcripts, speaker names for Zoom/Teams/Meet, 99-language mode, cross-meeting Q&A that cites its source meetings, Markdown export, auto-stop when a call ends, and an **optional, off-by-default local MCP server** giving other apps read-only access to notes. Goes online only for model downloads and update checks; 8GB minimum. **Why it's trending:** "no bot in the meeting" is a compliance argument as much as a privacy one, and it is MIT with a real commit history behind it.

**4. [apex-compute/ANR](https://github.com/apex-compute/ANR)** — **2.8k stars, 62 forks, 18 commits, C++.** Apex Neural Rendering: neural frame generation for games, installed into a title's folder through **OptiScaler**, with super-resolution and ray reconstruction listed as "beta soon." The most useful piece today is **`nr-record`**, which captures a game's real frame-generation/upscaler inputs so you can replay them through ANR FG, **NVIDIA DLSS-G** and others and diff the results as video or stills. Runs across **NVIDIA RTX 20–50 / GTX 16, AMD RX 5000–9000 and Steam Deck, Intel Arc A/B**, on Windows and Linux/SteamOS under Proton. **Why it's trending:** a vendor-neutral, reproducible harness for comparing upscalers is something the PC graphics community has wanted for years, and 2.8k stars on 18 commits is almost entirely interest in that. **Early and thinly licensed:** the README still says "source code: coming soon," **no licence is stated for ANR itself** (only OptiScaler's GPL-3.0 is noted), and 44 issues are open against those 18 commits.

**Also worth noting, as a theme rather than a repo:** two Show HN projects in this window both address agent *supply chain* risk — `agent-mcts` (Monte Carlo tree search over Claude Code) and **ClawVet**, an offline AST scanner and "rug-pull diff" for agent skills. Both are tiny (12 and 8 points), and **ClawVet is a name now shared by several unrelated repos** — one audit flagged that the most-cited version's bundled API ships persistent auth, telemetry and webhook behaviour worth reviewing before deployment. The category is real; these specific tools are not yet vetted. Separately, **[deepseek-ai/chat-demo](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/chat-demo)** (**5.2k stars, 1.2k forks, MIT**) is rising fast but is excluded from the ranked list above on merit: at **7 commits** it is a backendless static chat client for the DeepSeek API, and the stars are brand, not substance.
