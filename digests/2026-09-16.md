## Notable Papers & Research

### Corrupt Plans, Clean Traces: Evading Chain-of-Thought Monitoring with Plan Injection
Keertana Chidambaram, Andrew Ilyas, Vasilis Syrgkanis (**Stanford, CMU**) — [arXiv:2609.15989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15989) (September 15)

The most consequential paper of the window, because it attacks the mechanism that nearly every safety proposal now quietly assumes works. Chain-of-thought monitoring inspects an actor model's reasoning trace for signs of unsafe planning. The authors show you can plant **harmful but benign-sounding reasoning in the actor's context** and steer it into adversarial actions whose traces read clean — **25–33% monitor evasion** across monitorability benchmarks, including on models as large as DeepSeek-R1. The detail that should worry people: actor models don't just follow the injected plans, they **paraphrase them as their own reasoning**, with no attribution to the injection. Amodei's pacing framework and Altman's "independent evaluators" both lean on being able to read what a model is thinking; this is a demonstration that the trace can be authored by someone else.

### Stellar Colosseum: A Many-Agent Harness for Long-Horizon Research in Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science
Honghao Lin, David P. Woodruff, Yuan Deng, Jieming Mao, Song Zuo, Vahab Mirrokni (**CMU, Google Research**) — [arXiv:2609.15983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15983) (September 15)

The systems paper behind the genre of result OpenAI claimed on Navier–Stokes. Colosseum is a **model-agnostic harness for allocating inference** across long-horizon math research: it explores alternative strategies before committing to a proof, uses a **readiness gate** to decide when a route is mature enough to decompose, represents the plan as interdependent section-level subproblems, and routes verifier findings back to the specific part of the argument they break. At each stage it generates candidates in parallel, attacks them with targeted falsification, and merges candidates with their critiques via overlapping random-sample tree aggregation. Reported to have contributed to new research results, plus strong TCS-Bench and competitive-programming numbers. The thesis worth extracting: at the research frontier, **the harness is the capability** — which is exactly why "pace the frontier" is hard to define in terms of model releases.

### SocioVerse: A World Model for Social Simulation Powered by LLM Agents and a Pool of 10 Million Real-World Users
Accepted to **ACM Multimedia 2026 (MM '26)**, surfaced in the September 15 arXiv listing

The world-models entry this cycle, and a different sense of "world model" than Atlas or Genie: not 3D space but **social dynamics**, with agents grounded in a pool of ten million real users rather than invented personas. Logged here mainly as a marker of where the term is drifting — a digest whose central dispute is whether anyone can forecast AI's effects now contains two separate research programs both called world modeling, one simulating physics and one simulating publics.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Sam Altman — endorses a federal safety framework, and separates "pacing" from "stopping"
Posted on X **just after midnight Monday**: *"We welcome a federal framework that sets consistent safety requirements for frontier AI."* He added that *"consistent rules to manage frontier risk so that we can maximize the benefits are a good idea (and we are excited by ideas like independent auditors)"*, and drew the line his critics have been probing: *"When we talk about 'pacing,' we do not mean 'stopping.' Progress has been rapid and will continue to be… Pacing will be well worth this cost; no amount of American competitive pressure should justify recklessness, or let capabilities get ahead of alignment and monitoring."* The framing he gave CNBC is the two-failure-modes version: losing **"control of the future to AI"**, or too much power concentrating around a single person or company. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/sam-altman-ai-slowdown-anthropic-amodei-musk.html), [Epoch Times](https://www.theepochtimes.com/business/openais-sam-altman-backs-ai-slowdown-calls-for-federal-safety-rules-6087167))

### Anthropic's $517B compute ceiling, read against its CEO's essay
Per **The Information's** analysis, circulating this week: Anthropic signed for **14.8GW of compute capacity over the eleven months through August 2026**, with contract values totalling up to **$517B over the next decade** — against the roughly **$180B through 2029** it had previously told investors. **Google and AWS together account for ~11GW**; Microsoft committed at least **$30B** for ~1GW of Azure capacity plus additional NVIDIA-powered capacity; SpaceX/Colossus adds ~$45B; AMD signed a 2GW MI450 deal. The figure is a time-bound capacity ceiling, not cash out the door — but 14.8GW is roughly ten advanced nuclear reactors' worth of power, contracted by the company whose CEO published "We Must Pace the Frontier" four days ago. Both things can be true — pacing capability gains is not the same as pacing capex — but the juxtaposition is the argument Aidan Gomez and Michael Burry were already making. ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/anthropic-signed-517bn-in-compute-agreements-in-past-11-months/), [Forkast](https://forkast.news/anthropics-517b-compute-ceiling-reached-in-11-months-even-as-its-ceo-called-to-slow-down/))

### Cornelis Networks raises $205M and puts compute inside the network
Announced **September 14**, led by **IAG Capital Partners** with a strategic collaboration with **Qualcomm Technologies**. The Intel spinout (2020) unveiled **Active Compute Fabric**, an open, GPU-agnostic architecture that embeds programmable compute directly into the network fabric — offloading collective operations, adapting behavior to the workload, and letting the network act on data as it traverses the system rather than merely moving it. The 400 Gbps **CN5000** is shipping; an 800 Gbps **CN6000** generation is next. The interesting part is the strategic shape: a credible non-NVIDIA interconnect story with Qualcomm attached, in a week when the market is repricing compute demand. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/ai-infrastructure-company-cornelis-raises-205m-to-chip-away-at-nvidias-dominance/), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/14/cornelis-networks-raises-205m-and-scales-up-and-scales-out-with-its-new-active-compute-fabric/), [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4221872/cornelis-lands-205m-to-make-ai-networks-compute-not-just-connect/))
