# AI Daily Digest — October 6, 2026

**Top story: an AI model proved the Linear Hadwiger Conjecture.** Sergey Norin (McGill) and Raphael Steiner (ETH Zürich) posted a proof that K_t-minor-free graphs are Ct-colorable — the linear relaxation of Hadwiger's 1943 conjecture, one of the oldest open problems in graph theory — and disclose that **GPT-6 Astra found the proof**, following research directions from the authors, with almost none of their own specific proof ideas surviving into the final argument. Separately, New York City became the first legislature anywhere to put OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta under oath on existential AI risk, where three former frontier-lab researchers testified against their old employers.

## Notable Papers & Research

### A Proof of the Linear Hadwiger Conjecture
**Sergey Norin, Raphael Steiner** ([arXiv:2610.05291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05291), [Lean 4 formalization](https://github.com/snorin239/LinearHadwiger))

Hadwiger's conjecture (1943) says a graph with no K_t minor is (t−1)-colorable; it is widely considered the deepest open problem in graph coloring. The *linear* version — that some constant C exists with Ct colors always sufficing — had been pushed to O(t log log t) by Delcourt and Postle and then O(t log log log t) by Liu and Luo. This paper closes it: **there is a constant C such that K_t-minor-free graphs are Ct-colorable.**

The provenance is the story. The authors state the proof **was found by GPT-6 Astra following their directions**, that they suggested several approaches and "almost none" of their specific proof ideas were retained, and that the Lean 4 formalization — which contains no `sorry` placeholders and no user-declared axioms — **was produced by OpenAI Codex versions 6 Sol and Astra**, with the build verified on GitHub Actions on September 25. This is a different category from the AI-assisted results of the past two months: not a bound improved by recombining known lemmas, but a named conjecture settled, with the human contribution reduced to direction-setting and the machine-checkable artifact also machine-written. Submitted October 4.

### A Simple Algorithm Breaking the 1/3 Barrier for Approximate Nash Equilibria in Bimatrix Games
**Dongchen Li, Hanyu Li** ([arXiv:2610.05317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05317), [code](https://github.com/lhydave/new-approx-ne-algo))

The second AI-driven theory result posted the same day. Computing exact Nash equilibria in bimatrix games is PPAD-complete, so the question is how small an ε you can reach in polynomial time; 1/3 has stood as the barrier and breaking it was a known hard open problem. This paper gives a **deterministic, symmetric, hyperparameter-free polynomial-time algorithm achieving ≈0.30954+δ**, enriching the Tsaknakis–Spirakis framework with strategy-mixing blocks and selecting the best of five profiles. The repository states that **OpenAI Codex "played a central role in discovering the algorithm and developing its proof," including the exact verification tools**, and the paper itself discusses the role of LLM agents in the process. Two independent barrier-breaking results in one arXiv batch, both credited to agents, is the thing worth noting.

### ThinkingBox: grading agents on what they left behind, not what they said
**Microsoft and Hugging Face** ([hyper.ai writeup](https://hyper.ai/en/stories/016378578c1d36c27e1ac93e928d4a5e), [explainX analysis](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/microsoft-thinkingbox-agent-benchmark-database-state-reliability-2026))

A benchmark for stateful enterprise workflows that ignores the agent's text and tool-call syntax entirely and runs executable checks against the **final database state and side effects**: 507 workflows, each run **20 times across 18 models** to separate one-shot capability from reliability. The findings are the useful part: in a 12-model ablation, **67% of failed attempts looked clean** — no tool errors at all — yet checks found wrong field values in **77.6%** of those failures. The best models, Claude Opus 5.5 and Opus 5, passed all 20 attempts on only **241 of 507 tasks (47.5%)**. Released through the OpenEnv interface on Hugging Face under permissive licenses. Read against yesterday's branch-steering paper: the agent-evaluation field is converging on the view that self-reported success is nearly uncorrelated with having done the job.

### EnvDreamer: Large-Scale Multimodal-to-Environment Generation for Embodied AI
([arXiv:2610.04301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04301))

Vision-language models plan and drive **Unreal Engine 5** directly: structured layout plans and task lists in JSON compiled into Python against an EnvDreamer API, with an Evaluator Agent iterating on layout, furniture placement and plausibility until the scene passes validation. Policies trained purely in generated environments — no explicit mapping, no human task supervision — are competitive on navigation, rearrangement and manipulation benchmarks. Ships **EnvDreamer-20k**: 20,000 validated environments with task programs, scene graphs, trajectories and metadata. The pragmatic counterpoint to the generative world-model labs: rather than learn a simulator, have the model operate one that already has physics.

### From Scientific Observations to Mechanisms: Benchmarking Hypothesis Generation by AI Scientists
**Xiaxun Xie, Qingqing Long, Meng Xiao, Wei Ju, Yuanchun Zhou, Xuezhi Wang, Hengshu Zhu** ([arXiv:2610.05197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05197))

**MechHypoBench** asks whether an AI scientist can get from data to a *mechanism* — not a correlation or a restated finding. It pairs paper-derived mechanisms across 14 fields with real datasets totaling 17.98M records, and scores open-form hypotheses by their consequences under withheld conditions rather than by text similarity. General agents and purpose-built AI scientists both show a substantial gap to the true mechanisms. Timely given the Hadwiger result above: machines are closing problems handed to them while still struggling to say *why* a phenomenon happens.

### Same Predictions, Different Harms: Causal Auditing of Patient World Models
**Yicheng Qi, Xiyi Xiong (Imperial College London)** ([arXiv:2610.05198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05198))

Two clinical world models can agree exactly on transition kernels and arm-specific risks and still disagree on **what fraction of patients are harmed by switching treatment**, because counterfactual harm depends on how potential outcomes co-exist within the same patient — something predictions do not pin down. The authors audit the gap in a two-stage shared-response SCM and report honest negative results on public simulators (EpiCare, sepsis). A concrete protocol for when a patient world model is safe to trust, and a warning that matching benchmarks is not the same as matching consequences.

### Reflection AI — Beam, a 501B open-weight model *(reserved slot: lab not covered elsewhere in this digest)*
([Reflection blog](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/reflection-debuts-beam-a-open-weight-ai-model-to-rival-chinese-models-at-lower-compute-cost/), [Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/reflection-ai-unveils-beam-a-501b-parameter-open-weight-model/))

Launched October 5: the NVIDIA-backed lab's first open-weight model, a sparse MoE with **501B total / 23B active parameters**, pretrained on **23.8T curated tokens** with a **1M-token context window**, aimed at coding, reasoning and agentic work. The claim is efficiency rather than a new ceiling — on par with Z.ai's GLM-5.2 on advanced reasoning while using **3–4× less inference compute**, approaching Qwen 3.8-Max on coding and agentic tasks. Benchmarks are self-reported and not yet independently verified. **Weights under Apache 2.0 land later this month**, with the technical report, model card and fine-tuning tooling. Strategic read: this is the explicit US answer to Chinese open-weight dominance, backed by **$7B+ in compute commitments to SpaceX and Nebius** for GB300 capacity through 2029 — so the interesting question is whether the weights actually ship on time, not whether the benchmarks hold.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Three former frontier-lab researchers testified against their old employers, under oath
([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/05/anthropic-openai-google-meta-execs-testify-nyc-council-ai-hearing.html), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/05/new-york-city-council-hearing-openai-anthropic-ai-safety-google-meta/), [amNY](https://www.amny.com/news/ai-giants-nyc-council-whistleblower-warnings/))

At the New York City Council's AI oversight hearing — which the city says is the first of its kind anywhere — **Jacob Coxon** (ex-Anthropic) said: *"From my experience, the companies are being extremely reckless given the stakes. The companies run on a startup mindset: move fast, break things, fix them later. It does not work for building the most powerful technology ever built,"* adding that *"on the current path, I think it is more likely than not that humanity loses control to these AIs, and it could end in human extinction."*

**Daniel Kokotajlo** (ex-OpenAI) gave the line that traveled: the industry risks *"mistakenly thinking that it has solved the problem when really it just applied some duct tape that will fall off later."* **Alex Turner** (ex-Google DeepMind) put his personal estimate of an eventual AI takeover at **roughly one in three**, and argued that *"with reasonably high chance, we are racing to build and grow our own adversary here at home, which is misaligned AI"* — a sharper claim than the China-competition framing that usually dominates these hearings.

Why it matters: three people who wrote the safety frameworks at three different labs said under oath that the frameworks are not working, in the same week that OpenAI's own safety-report lead resigned calling the culture broken. It is no longer a dissident position.

### Andrej Karpathy — almost everyone paying attention arrived in the last year
*"My mental model for what is happening is that 99%+ of people who are now paying attention have been onboarded to anything related to AI in <1 year. This is very confusing to the AI dinosaurs (anyone in AI for pre 2026. Or even, gasp, pre 2012)."* ([Karpathy on X](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2106806571321966793))

Posted in reply to Elvis Saravia. A throwaway line with real explanatory force for the week's arguments: the audience judging the Hadwiger proof, the NYC testimony and LeCun's attack on Amodei is overwhelmingly one with no prior context, and no memory of the last three times a capability jump was oversold or undersold. Karpathy is describing why the same result reads as either routine or apocalyptic depending entirely on who is reading it.

### Demis Hassabis — imagination and memory are finally tractable
Hassabis says the two capabilities he spent his neuroscience career on — **imagination and memory** — were missing from AI and are now tackleable, and that DeepMind could only begin to attack those gaps five or six years in ([via @rdominguezibar](https://x.com/rdominguezibar/status/2107131430912331855)).

His 2007 dissertation work found that memory and imagination share a constructive mechanism — patients with hippocampal damage lose not only recall but the ability to build any rich mental scene — and his framing has hardened into *"imagination is a type of simulation."* Worth reading alongside his standing position that AGI needs world physics, memory, consistency and continual learning before it arrives around 2030: he is now saying two of those four have moved from intractable to merely hard.

### The AI companies gave few clear answers in New York
([CBS New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/new-york-city-council-ai-oversight-hearing/), [Quartz](https://qz.com/openai-anthropic-google-meta-nyc-council-ai-hearing-100526), [Washington Times/AP](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/oct/5/local-governments-take-safety-concerns-ai-industry-insiders-voice/))

The company side of the same hearing: **Logan Graham** (head of Anthropic's Frontier Red Team), **Morgan Dwyer** (OpenAI's head of policy development and operations), **Alice Friend** (Google's director of AI and emerging tech policy) and **Shane Cahill** (Meta's AI policy director for legislation) testified that their systems are already improving daily life and that they are focused on safety — and then declined to answer the central question. Asked by Speaker **Julie Menin** who is responsible if a model goes rogue, Dwyer said *"we are responsible for developing and evaluating our systems to ensure that they are safe"* without saying whether OpenAI would bear legal liability.

The bills on the table are the reason this matters more than a symbolic hearing: a **whistleblower incentive program** (cash rewards), a **private right of action** for New Yorkers harmed by AI agents, **independent third-party validation** requirements, and mandated **kill switches**. A private right of action in New York would do more to change lab behavior than anything in Washington's voluntary accord.

## Trending Open-Source Projects

**1. [michael-denyer/pstack-claude](https://github.com/michael-denyer/pstack-claude)** — **1,469 stars, +223 today**, TypeScript
A port of Lauren Tan's `pstack` — an opinionated agent skill stack — that translates Cursor's primitives for **Claude Code, Codex, Pi, GitHub Copilot and other harnesses**. You tell `poteto-mode` a goal and it selects the workflow: playbooks for bugs, planning, features, refactoring and performance, with skills named architect, arena, swarm, interrogate, tests and measurements. Per-role model and reasoning-effort configuration, no telemetry, local script execution. MIT, 211 commits. Why it's trending: the day's best velocity relative to size, and it is the clearest evidence yet that **agent skill stacks are becoming portable artifacts** rather than harness-specific config — the thing Karpathy called discardable software a week ago, hardening into something people maintain and port.

**2. [lexmount/moli](https://github.com/lexmount/moli)** — **9.6k stars** (created within the last two months), Rust
A headless browser built for agent workloads rather than retrofitted from one built for humans: full HTML5 parsing, native DOM, V8, CSS cascade, Fetch/XHR/WebSocket, cookies and storage, but **structure-first** — layout and rendering are skipped entirely for DOM-only work and triggered only behind `--layout`. Speaks CDP, WebDriver Classic and WebDriver BiDi from one binary; emits HTML, Markdown, JSON, semantic trees and PDFs. The numbers are honest about the trade: **81.88% success on 1,308 Lexbench headless tasks against Chrome's 99.85%**, for **92 MiB median peak memory against Chrome's 697 MiB** and ~15% of the CPU time. Apache-2.0/MIT, 1,619 commits. Why it's trending: every agent harness is paying Chrome's rendering bill for text it never looks at, and this is the first credible attempt to stop.

**3. [yetone/magpie](https://github.com/yetone/magpie)** — **5.2k stars** (created in the last ~2 weeks), Go
A menu-bar gateway that centralizes model choice across **35+ coding agents** — sign in once, pick any model for any agent, instead of editing a config file per tool. Runs a local gateway at `127.0.0.1:3425` speaking OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini APIs, with **routing groups that fail over between providers when quotas run out**, plugin hooks for auth and middleware, usage and cost tracking, on macOS, Windows, Linux, Docker and Termux. MIT, 1,962 commits. Why it's trending: the quota-failover routing, not the gateway — developers now hold three or four subscriptions and hit limits on each, and this is the first tool that treats that as the primary problem rather than a footnote.

**4. [cloudflare/cloudflare-os](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-os)** — **11.1k stars, +101 today**, TypeScript
Cloudflare's "operating system for AI workloads": an agent workspace on Workers, Durable Objects and Dynamic Workers where you describe an app in natural language and get a sandboxed, deployable **"Gadget,"** with real-time multiplayer collaboration, Blueprint sharing, multiple LLM providers, and a capability-based security layer called **Gatekeepers** mediating access to external services. Apache-2.0, 888 commits; v2 is a complete rewrite still in early access with acknowledged rough edges. Why it's trending: it is the one entry in this category that can be self-hosted on the open-source `workerd` runtime, which makes the capability model inspectable rather than a vendor promise.

**5. [facebookincubator/muse-gadget-sdk](https://github.com/facebookincubator/muse-gadget-sdk)** — **1.5k stars**, C
Meta's open-source firmware and SDKs for building your own hardware that talks to **Muse**, its personal AI agent: an **ESP32 device SDK** (screens, audio in and out, sensors) and a **Linux device SDK** that turns a Raspberry Pi into a Muse gadget, with reference configurations for Waveshare AMOLED, Seeed e-ink, M5Stack StickS3, AiPi Lite and the Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition. Apache-2.0, 51 commits, pair over the Muse app with an SDK token. The Muse Gadgets launch itself was covered October 4; the repo is new here, and a community ecosystem has already formed around it (Waveshare ports, Home Assistant add-ons, a desktop bridge). Why it's trending: Nat Friedman shipped it as a side project and it is the first time a frontier lab has handed out the firmware rather than the API — the cheapest possible way to find out what an assistant with hands wants to be attached to.
