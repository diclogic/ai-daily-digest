# AI Daily Digest — September 15, 2026

**Top story:** **The pacing consensus met every constituency it had not consulted, and lost most of them in a single day.** President **Trump** rejected it outright from his Doonbeg golf resort — "whoever wins AI, wins" — and named Dario Amodei personally, adding on Truth Social that a "SICK conspiracy" is driving data-center backlash and that "the only one that is happy about it is China." Beijing answered on the same day: Foreign Ministry spokesman **Guo Jiakun** called the push "fearmongering," and the *Global Times* framed Amodei's essay as a "Cold War script" aimed at curbing Chinese AI through "technological barriers and regulatory monopolies." Wall Street voted too — the **PHLX semiconductor index fell almost 6%, its worst day since early July**, with Nvidia down 3.4% and Marvell down 7%.

Then the flank nobody scripted: the criticism that the pacing plan is a **cartel** came not from accelerationists but from inside the industry. **Cohere's Aidan Gomez** — "the firms drafting the framework are the firms sitting at the top of the market today" — and, from the opposite direction, Montreal's **David Krueger**, who says pacing is far too weak and calls for "an immediate, indefinite international moratorium." **Michael Burry** called the whole thing "self-serving" pre-IPO puffery. Among tracked leaders, **Andrej Karpathy** broke a long quiet period to endorse the plan, and **Yann LeCun** called the danger warnings outright fake.

Launches were near-empty for a second day: no tracked model release on September 14 or 15. The most significant recent launch from a lab not otherwise covered here is **Andon Labs' Pion**, an agent built to run a company autonomously.

## Notable Papers & Research

### Why don't machine learning research agents overfit? — Amazon Science
**Amazon Science** ([blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/why-dont-machine-learning-research-agents-overfit), surfaced September 15, 103 points on Hacker News)

The finding is a compression test. When a successful research agent's strategy is squeezed through an information bottleneck — **as few as 16 tokens** — a fresh agent with no memory can reproduce the original's performance, which means the strategy captured real structure rather than memorized validation noise. Strategies that genuinely overfit fail the test: their validation-specific gains vanish through the bottleneck. The authors' compressed formulation — *"what fits (into few tokens) doesn't overfit"* — doubles as an explanation for why LLMs reconstruct whole ML pipelines from terse expert shorthand, and as a cheap, practical diagnostic for anyone running agents against a fixed benchmark.

### Augmented Lagrangian Predictive Coding — training 1,000-layer networks without backprop
Jeffrey Seely, Julian Gould (**Sakana AI**) — [arXiv:2605.31022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31022) · [pub.sakana.ai/pc-alm](https://pub.sakana.ai/pc-alm/) · [code](https://github.com/SakanaAI/pc-alm)

Resurfaced on Hacker News today; the paper itself dates to **May 2026** and is logged here as context, not as news. PC-ALM keeps predictive coding's inference budget but accumulates per-layer constraint errors into a **layer-local Lagrange multiplier**, so each layer recovers a gradient signal of the global loss using only communication with its neighbors. In linear networks it converges to exact backprop gradients; in nonlinear networks up to depth 128 it matches backprop across all width-depth regimes — including the deep-narrow case where plain predictive coding falls apart — and it trains **residual MLPs up to 1,000 layers**. Worth knowing about in a week when the entire policy conversation assumes backprop-on-a-single-cluster is the only way to build a frontier model.

### SolarWM: open data and scalable training for long-horizon video world models
Junchao Huang et al. — [arXiv:2609.02886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02886)

The most substantive open contribution to the world-models topic in the current cycle (posted early September). A reconfigurable data engine converts **1.43M canonical clips from 10 datasets** into one frame-aligned contract covering visual observations, metric camera geometry, captions, quality metadata and provenance — deliberately decoupling source processing from mixture construction. Four models at 5B–33B are instantiated on Wan2.2, LTX-2.5 and MiniMax-H3 while preserving each backbone's native objective. Trained on only **5-second sequences**, the resulting causal models sustain real-time interactive rollouts from minutes to hours. Data, pipeline, recipes, weights and framework are all released — the contrast with Atlas, Genie and Cosmos, none of which are open, is the point.

### Recursive Criticality of AI Self-Improvement
Mikhail Burtsev — [arXiv:2609.00137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00137) (September 1)

Included because it supplies the missing formalism for the argument consuming the week. Burtsev derives a **recursive reproduction number R_AI** that compares the strength of AI-on-AI R&D feedback against the rate at which further progress gets harder; above 1, improvements compound across development cycles. Two results land directly on the pacing debate: a system can **enter the self-amplifying regime before any acceleration is visible**, and — extending to multiple actors — improvements shared across organizations can make the *ecosystem* self-amplifying even when **no individual lab is**. That second result is an argument for coordination that does not depend on trusting any lab's self-assessment.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Andrej Karpathy — endorses the pacing plan
Karpathy, now on **Anthropic's pretraining team**, posted on X that he **"love[s] this and really hope[s] we can come together as an industry and make it happen."** Notable for two reasons: he has been quiet through four consecutive digests, and he is the tracked leader least associated with safety advocacy — his public identity is built on making frontier technique legible and reproducible for everyone. An endorsement from the person who writes 200-line GPTs to demystify the field carries different weight than one from a lab CEO with a safety brand to defend.

### Yann LeCun — the warnings are "fake"
LeCun called the danger warnings behind the slowdown push **outright fake**, per September 2026 reporting. His objection runs on a separate track from Burry's and Gomez's: not that the motive is commercial, but that the **premise is wrong** — he has long rejected the claim that current LLMs are on any straightforward path to human-level intelligence, so in his reading there is no catastrophic capability to pace. He lands on the same side as Beijing and a short-seller while sharing none of their reasoning, which is the cleanest illustration of how incoherent the anti-pacing coalition is. ([Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/14/china-michael-burry-and-yann-lecun-reject-openai-and-anthropic-calls-to-slow-ai-development-lecun-calls-warnings-fake/))

### Sam Altman — no new statement
Yesterday's two positions still stand and were not extended: support for pacing ("we need ways to ensure that alignment and safety techniques stay ahead of progress in model capabilities," X, Sunday) and the confirmation that **OpenAI will not go public in 2026** — "right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/sam-altman-openai-ipo-delay-ill-advised-moment-safety-concerns/)). Burry's attack below is aimed squarely at the seam between those two statements.

### Dario Amodei — the target, not the speaker

Nothing new in this window. His prior position on the pacing plan ("the direction is correct," September 13) stands.

### Lina Khan: there is no AI exemption, and the precedent is from 1934
The former FTC chair argued that **no new legislation is required** to prosecute AI executives: "Law enforcers already have authority to charge companies and their CEOs for creating and releasing dangerous, unvetted, or defective products." She notes that some **state attorneys general are already exploring criminal liability** for executives whose models are involved in criminal activity. The pointed part is her citation — *FTC v. R. F. Keppel & Bro., Inc.* (1934), which outlawed "competition which casts upon one's competitors the burden of the loss of business unless they will descend to a practice which they are under a powerful moral compulsion not to adopt, even though it is not criminal." That is a 92-year-old description of the **race-to-the-bottom dynamic** the pacing plan says it exists to solve — and it suggests the legal tool already exists without an industry body to administer it. ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/14/ex-ftc-boss-khan-urges-uncle-sam-to-break-out-the-handcuffs-for-ai-ceos-citing-1934-precedent/), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/a-reminder-from-lina-khan-we-dont-need-new-laws-to-prosecute-ceos-at-ai-companies-2000811503))
