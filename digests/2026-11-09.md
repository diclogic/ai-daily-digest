## Notable Papers & Research

### An unreleased Anthropic model produced a Lean-checked proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture
**Physical Superintelligence PBC (PSI)** announced on **November 6** that an internal research system, running instances of a **non-public Anthropic model**, found a proof of the **Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture** — posed by Scott Aaronson and Andris Ambainis in **2008**, and one of the central open problems in quantum complexity, bearing on when quantum computers can achieve exponential speedups over classical ones. PSI says the system produced a complete proof on the **third day** of a run on its own infrastructure; it then pointed the system at related open problems and the result was **improved twice** before **Lean 4** accepted the final version. The manuscript and formalization are public. [PSI on X](https://x.com/psipbc/status/2118706269506949152) · [Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/psi-reports-machine-checked-proof-of-aaronson-ambainis-conjecture/) · [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/psi-anthropic-model-aaronson-ambainis-conjecture/)

**What to hold against it.** The paper has **not been peer reviewed**. Lean checks the formal statement it was given, so human mathematicians still have to confirm the formalized statement matches what Aaronson and Ambainis actually conjectured — a gap several outlets flag explicitly. **Which** Anthropic model PSI used, and on what access terms, is **not stated**, and no Anthropic statement confirming the work could be located. Coverage conflicts on whether Stanford researchers (who proved a special case in August) are reviewing it; that claim traces to PSI. No public assessment from Aaronson or Ambainis was found.

**Why it matters beyond the result.** Set this next to **OpenAI's** late-October claim to have proved the **local smoothing conjecture** in all dimensions (covered here October 27 and 29) and the pattern is now a trend rather than an incident: frontier labs announcing machine-generated mathematics, with a **proof assistant standing in for peer review**, on a timeline that journals cannot match. The verification bottleneck has moved from "can a model do it" to "who confirms the formal statement is the right one."

### Google DeepMind: agents found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA
Also **November 6**. DeepMind reports that **human-guided AI agents** produced two results against **1024-bit RSA**: a padding-related **signature-forgery** path estimated at roughly **1,600× cheaper** than the previous best method when an attacker controls part of the signed data (the agents also re-ran a published 1024-bit forgery at about a third of the original compute cost), and a **bulk-factoring** method cutting the marginal cost of each additional key in a large batch by about **30×**. Research led by **Dj Dvijotham** and **Abhradeep Thakurta**, with outside cryptographers **Nadia Heninger** and **Emmanuel Thomé**. [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/google-deepmind-ai-rsa-1024-bit-weaknesses/) · [Digg](https://digg.com/ai/4ij1uxax)

**The careful reading:** the figures are **theoretical estimates**, the bulk method is still well short of breaking any single key faster than the general number field sieve, and **2048-bit and larger keys are not affected**. The interesting claim is methodological — agents contributing to cryptanalysis rather than cryptography being broken. Neither DeepMind's own post nor the IACR paper was reachable from this session, so the specific numbers rest on secondary coverage.

### OpenAI: BioBasicsBench, and what agents get wrong about lab evidence
**November 6.** OpenAI released **BioBasicsBench**, built with the enzyme-design company **Ligo**: **100 tasks** drawn from real experiments across protein methods, imaging and cytometry, quantification and assays, nucleic acid methods, and molecular cloning. It deliberately tests **analysis after data collection** — reading raw data, spotting errors — not pipetting or instrument operation. Each task was reviewed by a Ligo scientist who did not write it, an external PhD-level wet-lab scientist, and a final internal pass. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/measuring-how-ai-agents-handle-wet-lab-science/) · [OpenAI on X](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2118782708767965238) · [Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/openai-introduces-biobasicsbench-to-test-ai-agents-on-wet-lab-data/)

The number worth keeping is the **failure mode**, not the score: on a colony-counting task the leading model separated close colonies poorly, missed visible ones and counted empty agar — a roughly **12% overcount**. One secondary source puts the top model at a **66.7% mean partial-credit reward** at high reasoning effort; that figure appears in only one place and could not be checked against OpenAI's post from this session. Sources also disagree on whether outside labs can currently reproduce OpenAI's scores.

### Co-Design Bench: the benchmark gap, measured on real coding sessions
**Co-Design Bench** (arXiv **2610.35138**) builds **273 tasks** from **37,463** deduplicated queries across **26,314 real user coding sessions**, split into a Multimodal Editing track and a Repository Editing track. The reported result: the strongest model **fully solves 26.4%** of tasks — about **41%** on multimodal, just under **18%** on repository tasks — against **above 99%** for the same models on older benchmarks. The paper also notes that flaws in test environments can let agents pass without actually solving the task. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35138) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35138) · [GitHub](https://github.com/Wiselnn570/Co-Design-Bench) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-coding-agents-still-need-human-cleanup-on-nearly-three-out-of-four-tasks-in-a-real-world-test/)

**Caveat:** the 26.4% headline and the per-track split come from **The Decoder**, not from the abstract as reachable here, and the model it names could not be corroborated elsewhere. This is the fifth such result in six months — **Real-SWE** (38.8% best), **SWE Marathon** (26%), **UC Berkeley RDI** (~1 in 4), and the Alibaba secure-code benchmark (25.1%, a *different* benchmark — don't conflate the numbers). The convergence around a quarter to a third on realistic work is now the more robust finding than any single paper.

## Model & Product Launches

### Reserved slot: Nous Research's Arete-27B — the "open weights are editable values" experiment *(lab not covered elsewhere in this digest)*
**November 6–7** (sources split on the day). **Nous Research** released **Arete-27B**, a plain supervised fine-tune of Alibaba's **Qwen3.8-27B** — no architecture change, **~52.8M tokens**, 64k context, 2 epochs, 1,202 steps, **no RL** — aimed squarely at the political framing baked into a Chinese open-weight base model. On Nous's own **803-prompt** values evaluation, answers classified as containing state-aligned propaganda fall from about **22% to 0.9%**, and the mean factual score rises from **2.32 to 2.98** out of 5. General capability barely moves: **Hermes Bench 65.77% → 65.60%** (inside noise), and **Terminal-Bench-2.1 67% → 70%**, an improvement Nous itself declines to over-credit given harness and inference differences. **Apache 2.0** on Hugging Face, with the **weights, evaluation prompts and training data all published**. [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/NousResearch/Arete-27B) · [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/07/nous-research-arete-27b-qwen3-8-27b-propaganda-fine-tune/) · [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/nous-research-unveils-arete-27b-post-trained-qwen3-8-27b/)

**Why it's the reserved item:** most of the open-weight conversation is about capability per dollar. This is the first release this autumn to treat a base model's *values* as a cheap, reversible post-training variable and to publish enough — data, prompts, weights — for someone else to check the claim. If a 53M-token SFT can move a model's political framing by an order of magnitude without touching its agentic scores, "whose values are in the weights" becomes a line item rather than a property of the lab that trained them. **What to hold against it:** every number is Nous's own, **Hermes Bench is Nous's internal 150-task suite**, Terminal-Bench ran on Nous's hardware and harness, and the evidence covers one base model, one training mixture, one values rubric and one agentic benchmark. No independent replication yet.

### "Yes, and don't ask again" put 2,539 secrets on public GitHub
Security firm **Apiiro** published research (**November 5**, widely picked up November 8–9) on **Claude Code's** permission-persistence file. Approving a shell command with *"Yes, and don't ask again"* writes the **full approved command** — including any password or API key inside it — in plain text to `.claude/settings.local.json`. Apiiro found **104,185 copies** of that file across **103,617 repositories**; about **1.1%** (**1,122 files**) contained at least one secret, yielding **1,379** distinct secrets, and **2,539 secrets across 1,994 repositories** once shared settings files, older versions and agent chat logs are included. More than a third open a database directly — **379 database passwords**, plus **43 SSH logins** for public servers, **42** GitHub/GitLab/npm tokens and **23** AWS keys. Of **16 coding agents** whose documentation Apiiro compared, Claude Code was the only one confirmed to write the full approved command to a project file by default. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/claude-codes-dont-ask-again-file.html) · [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/dont-ask-again-in-anthropics-claude-code-leaks-developer-secrets-630203) · [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/claude-codes-yes-and-dont-ask-again/)

**The mechanism detail matters and the reporting disagrees on it.** Claude Code **gitignores** the file on creation, so Apiiro reasons the exposed copies were added and committed **manually** — the ignore rule lives on one developer's machine while the file lives in the project. One researcher-based account says Claude often commits the file itself. Either way the remediation is the same: deleting the file does not remove secrets from git history or forks, so **rotate**. No Anthropic response was located, and the documented behaviour is reported unchanged.

**1. [Vincentwei1021/video-shotcraft](https://github.com/Vincentwei1021/video-shotcraft)** — An AI video skill for Claude Code and Codex that builds product promos. Apache-2.0, **17,972 stars, +2,171 this week**, 1.7k forks, **111 commits**. The README's own framing is the point: *"It is not a video generator."* The agent screenshots your real pages, picks from **124 shot recipe cards (150 shots)** across 10 categories, animates in **Remotion** so every frame is drawn in code from your screenshots and copy, beat-syncs audio (Freesound SFX, 5 bundled BGM tracks), checks frames, and emits an MP4 — plus a browser-based **Motion Workbench** editor and **JianYing/CapCut CN draft export** with per-shot clips, captions and audio tracks. *Why it's trending:* it is the counter-position to diffusion video for the one case businesses actually need — a promo of software that must show the software accurately — and the output stays **editable** rather than regenerated. *Caveats:* **Remotion is free for individuals and small teams but companies may need a paid licence**; tested on **macOS** only (the Windows installer is untested, JianYing export verified on Pro 11.2 macOS); headless Linux/CI may need `--concurrency=1` and a local chrome-headless-shell path; the bundled demo screenshots must be replaced before publishing; and **Freesound audio carries its own per-file licences, some requiring CC BY credit**. At 111 commits against 18k stars, this is very young code.

**2. [liguobao/ds-harness-remote](https://github.com/liguobao/ds-harness-remote)** — Reach a **DeepSeek Harness** instance on your own machine from a PC, phone or browser. MIT, **2,325 stars, +1,091 this week** — about **47% of its total stars in seven days**, the highest relative velocity in this section — 182 forks, 1,165 commits, current target `ds-harness-remote@0.6.4`. The host opens **only outbound** connections, so no public port or IP is needed; it tries **LAN → P2P → TURN → encrypted WebSocket relay** in order, with Harness traffic under **Noise_IK_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256** so the relay carries ciphertext it cannot read. Also ships remote file browsing, terminals, local dev-service previews, and **experimental ACP workspaces** for Codex, Cursor, Claude, Hermes, OpenCode, Pi and Antigravity. *Why it's trending:* "my agent runs at home, I drive it from my phone" is the shape a lot of people want and almost nobody ships with a real transport story. *Caveats, and there are several:* it is an **independent community project, not an official DeepSeek product**; the one-command Linux installer creates a **systemd service running as root**; **remote terminals run as the host user, independent of agent approval prompts**; same-account devices connect **without extra confirmation**; the service can see account/device ownership, online status and connection times even though content stays encrypted; the bundled self-host server lacks the hosted Remote Web experience and needs your own coturn; the iOS client is **TestFlight only**; and **registration requires an invitation code**.

**3. [Mesh-LLM/mesh-llm](https://github.com/Mesh-LLM/mesh-llm)** — *"Distributed AI/LLM for the people. Share compute privately or publicly to power your agents and chat."* Rust, Apache-2.0, **3,994 stars, +259 this week**. Pools GPU and memory across machines and presents the result as a single **OpenAI-compatible endpoint** on port 9337: serve a model on one node, **split a large model across nearby peers**, route requests to specialized models, and let agents coordinate over **mesh gossip**. `mesh-llm serve --auto` joins a public mesh, `--model` runs a private one, `--join <token>` joins by invite, `client --auto` is API-only; integrations for Goose, OpenCode and Claude Code, install scripts for Linux/macOS/Windows and a Homebrew formula for Apple Silicon. *Why it's trending:* the quiet counter-trend to the $3.4B-of-turbines story at the top of this digest — inference as a peer-to-peer network rather than a procurement problem. *Caveats:* the lowest absolute velocity in this section; the README calls it **experimental distributed-systems software**; a **public** mesh means your prompts traverse other people's machines, which the project does not resolve for you; and GitHub is full of near-identical **forks** under other usernames carrying the same description — the upstream is the `Mesh-LLM` org. The repository page itself could not be loaded from this session, so these details come from search results and the organization page rather than a direct README read.

**4. [agentgateway/agentgateway](https://github.com/agentgateway/agentgateway)** — *"Next Generation Agentic Proxy for AI Agents and MCP servers."* Rust, Apache-2.0, **6,044 stars, +191 this week**. A data plane for agent traffic built on **MCP and A2A**: MCP gateway with tool federation and OAuth, A2A gateway with capability discovery and task collaboration, MCP/A2A-aware **RBAC**, dynamic config via **xDS**, LLM provider routing, and it will front ordinary HTTP and gRPC services too. *Why it's trending:* the governance, as with `pr-agent` last week. Created by **Solo.io**, which **donated it to the Linux Foundation** (accepted August 25, 2025 at Open Source Summit Europe, on an explicit vendor-neutral, community-governed footing) and it is now an **Agentic AI Foundation** project — the same foundation Anthropic donated **Agent Skills** to on October 22. The pattern of the last fortnight is agent infrastructure moving into neutral foundations, and this is the plumbing layer of it. *Caveats:* modest velocity on a mature repo; and despite frequent confusion in coverage, **it is not a CNCF project** — the CNCF references trace to an ambassador's quote, not to its host. Its repository page also could not be loaded from this session; details are from the project's own site, the Linux Foundation announcement and search results.
