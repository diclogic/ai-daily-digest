## Notable Papers & Research

### A 2015 CNN beat GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 at skin cancer diagnosis, 62% to 42%

**Magnus Lynch**, consultant dermatologist at Guy's Hospital and King's College London, gave **200 biopsy-proven lesions** to **GPT-6 Astra**, **Claude Fable 5.1**, and a fine-tuned **Inception v3** — a 2015 architecture — trained on public dermatological images, with six diagnoses to choose from. First-choice accuracy: the CNN **125/200 (62%)**, the two LLMs **84 and 85 (42%)**. Allowing a second choice, the CNN reached **173/200** against **134 and 138**. On the 30 melanomas the CNN caught **13** first-choice, against **8** for GPT-6 Astra and **11** for Fable 5.1. ([Lynch Lab write-up, October 25](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer), [Hyper.ai summary](https://hyper.ai/en/stories/1786dbecdb7427e1a4e20b85fba1ab94))

**Why it matters, and the part that is worse than the headline:** asked the same question *as patients would ask it*, both frontier models advised seeing a doctor for nearly every lesion — including most melanomas. That is not caution, it is an unusable triage signal: a referral rate near 100% carries no information. The accuracy gap is the quotable number; the refusal to discriminate is the clinically disqualifying one. The result also cuts against the standard argument that general models subsume narrow ones — here a task-specific net two model generations old, running on a laptop's worth of compute, holds a 20-point lead. **Caveats:** 200 cases, a six-way multiple-choice format, one clinician's test, and no peer review; Hacker News commenters (~400–450 points across snapshots) pressed on methodology and generalizability. A separate PromptZone article describing **ResNet-50 at 81.5% vs GPT-4o at 60.3%** is a *different* comparison with different models — do not merge the two.

### FrontierMemory — five agent harnesses took the same memory test, and the harness mattered as much as the model

**Letta** released FrontierMemory, which scores agent memory by **downstream task performance** rather than recall — the stated motivation being that recall benchmarks are saturated by agentic search. Agents receive transcripts of earlier successful and failed attempts and must write their own memories to do better next time: **674 past agent trajectories across 15 memory-dependent tasks**. Harness scores: **Letta Dream 73.3%**, Prime Intellect's **Prime Agent 60%**, **OpenAI Agents SDK 40%**, **Claude Managed Agents 33.3%**, **Hermes Agent 26.7%**. Holding models constant, Dream still beat the OpenAI Agents SDK **60% to 40%**. As memory *generators*, **Claude Opus 5.5** and **Z.ai's GLM-5.3** tied at **73.3%**, ahead of **Kimi K3 (66.7%)** and **GPT-6.1 Sol / Gemini 4 Argon (60%)**. ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/letta-frontiermemory-agent-harness-benchmark/), [Letta's announcement](https://x.com/Letta_AI/status/2112957074644873289))

**Why it matters:** this is the second measurement this week pointing at the scaffold rather than the weights — Harness² (yesterday's digest) found up to 11.8 points from scaffold recursion on frozen models; FrontierMemory finds a 20-point harness gap at fixed models. Two different teams, two different methods, same direction. **Caveats to apply hard:** it is **Letta's own benchmark and Letta's harness wins it**, which is the oldest conflict in benchmark publishing. Letta itself notes that with only **15 tasks a single result moves a score ~6.7 points** — so the 60/40 head-to-head is six tasks' difference, and the Opus/GLM tie at the top is within one task of several rivals. Treat the ranking as a hypothesis, not a leaderboard.

### Forced to name a candidate, Grok picked the Republican 92% of the time and GPT-6.1 Sol picked the Democrat 97%

Researchers at **Convovo**, a conversational-AI studio led by Cornell professor **Tapan Parikh**, prompted models through developer APIs with an instruction demanding a single candidate and no hedging, across nearly **500 US midterm races**. **Grok 4.7** backed the Republican in **92%** of answers; **GPT-6.1 Sol** backed the Democrat in **97%**. The two agreed **less than 10%** of the time. Grok leaned on sources such as Ballotpedia; GPT drew on Wikipedia and Reddit. Parikh's reading is that both models went looking for evidence supporting an existing lean. ([TechCrunch, October 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/forced-to-pick-groks-ai-model-chooses-republicans-openais-picks-democrats/), [The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/10/25/study-finds-grok-and-gpt-models-show-opposing-partisan-leans-on-u-s-midterm-races/))

**Why it matters:** near-total disagreement between two widely deployed models on the same factual-looking question is a measurement of divergence, not of bias in one direction — whichever model a voter happens to use is doing the choosing. **Caveats, which are load-bearing here:** the **consumer apps refused to answer at all**; the result exists only under an API prompt engineered to forbid refusal, which is exactly OpenAI's response — a spokesperson said ChatGPT is objective by default and that this setup does not reflect normal use. It is also **not an official Cornell study** despite wide framing as one, and the primary report has not been reviewed here.

### "Is this machine playing?" — 13 agents, 30 hours, no task

Thirteen agents running **Claude Opus 4.7** inside **Claude Code 2.1.116** were left on a simulated island for **30 hours** with nothing assigned. They climbed, built, and invented challenges — turning blocks and a ring into hoop and target games and something resembling "the floor is lava" — and six independently discovered they could throw blocks farther by releasing mid-jump. Given explicit challenges afterwards, the agents that had explored generally outperformed identical agents that had not. Authors: **Nathan Cloos, Antonio Norelli, Daniel Durbin, Jacob Andreas, Daniela Rus, Phillip Isola** ([arXiv:2610.07130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07130); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/left-alone-with-nothing-to-do-ai-agents-started-playing/), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mit-claude-agents-virtual-island-play)).

**Included because it has not appeared in a previous digest and ran through the weekend's discussion, not because it is fresh** — the preprint is dated October 5. **Caveats the coverage mostly buried:** the agents were given a **persona file describing them as curious**, which plausibly produced much of what is being read as spontaneous play, and the paper is not peer reviewed. UC Berkeley cognitive scientist **Eunice Yiu** told MIT Tech Review she was impressed but wanted evidence about the underlying cognition. The transferable result is the *downstream* one — unstructured exploration improved later task performance — which does not depend on whether "play" is the right word.

### Cerebras maps a ~3-trillion-parameter model across 59 wafers

Cerebras published an engineering post on **October 22** laying out how it would serve a trillion-parameter-class model on wafer-scale hardware, using **Kimi K3** as the worked example: **24 dense layers** at one wafer each (to leave room for KV cache) plus **69 linear-attention layers** packed two per **44GB** wafer, for **59 wafers** total, assuming **~3T parameters**, **4-bit weights** and a **256,000-token** average request. It cites generation rates of **1,000 to >2,000 tokens/second**. ([VentureBeat on the earlier benchmark](https://venturebeat.com/technology/cerebras-says-its-chips-run-a-trillion-parameter-ai-model-nearly-7-times-faster-than-gpu-clouds), [analysis](https://kenashe.ai/blog/2026-10-25-cerebras-says-its-running-trillion-parameter-models-what-that-actually-means))

**Read the genre correctly before reading the numbers:** this is a **layout calculation, not a shipped product** — Kimi K3 is an illustration, and no public endpoint is announced. The structurally interesting claim is that each added wafer brings compute *and* on-chip memory, so a bigger model is mostly a longer pipeline rather than a memory wall; that is a theoretical property, not a demonstrated one. It drew ~350 points and 127 comments on Hacker News, where the engineering was praised and the economics questioned. For context, Cerebras ran **Kimi K2.6** at **981 output tokens/second** in May 2026 enterprise trials, independently described as **6.7×** the next-best GPU cloud. Availability is the open question: a trillion-parameter deployment can mean a callable endpoint or a capability reserved for a handful of government and enterprise buyers.

### "Zero financial payoff": Apollo's chief economist charts the gap between the Mag 7 and everyone else

**Torsten Slok** argued in an **October 24** note that AI has delivered **"zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech."** His charts: **Magnificent 7 margins near 30%** against the **S&P 493 around 10%** and flat; revenue per worker rising for the Mag 7 and flat for the 493; **"still no signs of AI boosting productivity growth."** The concentration figure is the one to carry: **the top 10% of firms account for 99.5% of spending on model serving and inference.** He reports mainstream firms now treating token spend as a **cost center that weighs on margins**, and judges the problem **structural rather than a timing lag**. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/25/ai-delivers-zero-financial-payoff-to-most-companies-according-to-apollo-economist/), [Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/apollo-torsten-slok-warns-ai-zero-payoff-firms-outside-tech-sp-493-mag-7/cZUcu7qRBUn))

**Why it matters:** "structural, not a lag" is the whole claim. A lag resolves on its own; a structure does not, and the 99.5% concentration figure suggests why — if essentially all inference spend sits inside the firms selling inference, the revenue is substantially circular and the diffusion story has not started. Set this beside Woolf below: prices falling 80% has not yet moved adoption outside the top decile, which is the uncomfortable reading for both sides of the trade. **Caveats:** these are secondary summaries of a client note, not the note; outlets differ on whether 493 margins are "flat" or "~10%" (same data, different framing); and one source in the result set is an unverified blog.

### Why the price cuts happened: cache reads are 96–99% of agentic coding spend

**Max Woolf** published an accounting of the June–September price collapse on **October 25**. The mechanism is not the headline rate: **cache reads make up 96–99% of token spend in agentic coding**, so Anthropic's **September 1** cut of cache reads from **$1 to $0.25** (75%) lowered the real cost of running a coding agent by roughly **70–75%** while input and output prices barely moved. OpenAI's **July 30** cut took **GPT-5.6 Luna** down **80%** to **$0.20 / $1.20 / $0.02**. He attributes the cuts to competition — cheap open-weight models and a crowded field of inference providers establishing a floor — rather than loss-leading, and notes **Vercel gateway data showing cost per token at 43.3% of its April 2026 level**, meaning serving costs fell enough to defend share without destroying margin. He expects prices to keep falling even as both labs prepare to go public. ([minimaxir.com](https://minimaxir.com/2026/10/cheap-llms/), [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4225637/openai-anthropic-cut-ai-model-costs-as-price-performance-race-intensifies.html))

**Why it matters:** this reframes the price war. Published per-token rates have been the industry's scoreboard all year, and if 96–99% of real spend runs through a line item nobody quotes, every "X% cheaper than Y" comparison in circulation — including several carried in these digests — is measuring the wrong column. It also explains how the cuts and the margin story coexist: a 57% drop in serving cost funds a 75% cut on the line that actually bills. **Caveats:** the essay reaches us through secondary summaries; those summaries **disagree on model names** (one writes "Opus 5.5" where another writes "Fable 5.1"), and a separate tracker claims frontier token prices actually *rose* over the last twelve months while sitting 84.5% below March 2023 — different baskets, not necessarily a contradiction, but do not combine them.

### PHP2Shell escalates: a government network in two hours, Mirai operators, and pre-disclosure exploitation

Following the AI-assisted discovery and **October 21** patches covered October 22–24, the exploitation picture moved sharply. **Unit 42** reports attackers hit a **European government entity** and, **within two hours of initial exploitation**, had **password hashes and multi-factor-authentication secrets**. **Amazon's MadPot** identified exploitation attempts dating to **October 17 — four days before public disclosure**. **Cloudflare** has logged **millions** of attempts; **Sophos** saw attempts to install a Mirai variant it calls **Potassium**; **SecurityWeek** reports Mirai botnet operators joining and **ASLR-bypass exploits emerging**. The flaws are **CVE-2026-109618 / CVE-2026-109619**, memory-safety bugs in **PHP 5.4–8.5**, patched in **8.5.12 / 8.4.27 / 8.3.36 / 8.2.35**. ([Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/php2shell-php-rce), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/php2shell-exploitation-hits-government.html), [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/amazon-threat-intelligence-identifies-php2shell-exploitation-prior-to-public-disclosure-in-madpot/), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/mirai-botnet-operators-join-php2shell-attacks-as-aslr-bypass-exploits-emerge/))

**Why it matters:** the two-hour credential-to-MFA-secret timeline is the number to carry — it is shorter than most organizations' patch windows, and the pre-disclosure probing on October 17 means the defensive clock started before defenders knew there was a clock. Wiz's own framing is the uncomfortable part: the bugs sat in PHP for decades and were previously unexploitable because the chain was too complex, and **frontier models lowered that barrier**. This is the first large-scale test of Altman's "can't patch all the bugs" admission. **Caveats:** severity ratings conflict (**9.2** CVSS v4.0 from PHP's developers, **10.0** from watchTowr), and watchTowr reported in-the-wild exploitation by October 21 while **CISA said it had observed none** as of the same day. The fix is unambiguous: update to the patched versions.
