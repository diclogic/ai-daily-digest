# AI Daily Digest — September 25, 2026

**Top story: two days after asking the UN Security Council for international AI standards, the three largest US frontier labs were reported to be building their own private standards body instead.** The Information reported on September 24 that Google, OpenAI and Anthropic have agreed to stand up a FINRA-style self-regulator — tentatively the **Frontier AI Standards Agency** (also reported as the Standards Authority for Frontier AI) — with no government oversight, targeting a launch by end of 2026 or early 2027. They have approached **Sriram Krishnan**, the Trump White House's senior AI policy adviser until June 2026, to run it. Overnight, a second governance story landed: Anthropic is asking shareholders to approve a Palantir-style share class giving its **seven co-founders 50.1% of voting power** ahead of a potential IPO. Both stories are about the same question — who actually gets to say no to a frontier lab — and both answered it the same way.

## Notable Papers & Research

### CounterRoute: Self-Routed Reasoning via Hierarchical Counterfactual Credit Assignment
Ruochen Jiao, Besnik Fetahu, Zhenyu Shi, Priyanka Nigam — [arXiv 2609.29109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29109) (submitted Sept 24)
An online RL framework that teaches a dual-mode model to decide **for itself** whether a query needs a long chain of thought, learning the routing decision and the mode-conditioned response in one shared policy straight from a native dual-mode checkpoint — no method-specific SFT warm-up. Paired counterfactual rollouts assign cross-mode credit to the **routing token only**, while within-mode GRPO trains the response tokens. The practical point: "thinking budget" is currently a user-facing dial, and this is an argument that it should be a learned one.

### To Think or Not to Think: Allocating Reasoning Where It Helps
Zhengdong He, Yunfan Zhou, Jianguo Yao, Haibing Guan, Xijun Li — [arXiv 2609.29664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29664)
Attacks the same waste from the opposite direction and lands a more interesting negative result. The standard assumption behind length-adaptive methods — harder questions benefit monotonically from more reasoning — **does not hold**. The accuracy gain from extra reasoning length is concentrated on *partially solvable* questions; on genuinely hard ones it buys little. Their method, **CARE** (Contrastive Accuracy Reward Estimation), allocates budget by measured per-question benefit rather than by difficulty proxy, and improves Pass@1 across several reasoning benchmarks.

### TimeBraid: Unifying Time Series and Language for Understanding and Forecasting
UC San Diego, USC, Aether AI — [arXiv 2609.29792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29792) (submitted Sept 24)
Aligns a pretrained language model and a pretrained time-series foundation model through **interleaved global residual attention layers**, rather than tokenizing numbers into the LLM or bolting a text encoder onto a forecaster. Worth watching as the "two pretrained backbones, one braid" pattern shows up in more modality pairs.

### PUBG Ally: A Conversational Embodied Agent as an AI Teammate
KRAFTON — [arXiv 2609.29837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29837) (submitted Sept 24)
A rare paper about an agent that actually shipped to the public. Ally plays PUBG: BATTLEGROUNDS alongside a human as a voice-enabled squadmate: an LLM agent inspects game state through a controlled interface, interprets player speech, and issues high-level action choices that steer a faster low-level control layer for movement and combat, with speech kept synchronized to actions under hard latency budgets. Built on a **2B-parameter small language model** with ASR and TTS, running on NVIDIA ACE. It ran as a public Arcade beta from **June 17 to July 1, 2026**, and among players whose sessions were confirmed in game records, recommendation was **+25.1 points net positive**. The contribution is less the architecture than the deployment data. ([NVIDIA technical blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/))

### Amazon — Seller Central opens to third-party agents *(reserved slot: company not covered elsewhere in this digest)*
At **Amazon Accelerate on September 23**, Amazon opened its Seller Central APIs to outside AI agents via a new **Selling Partner plugin**, in US beta. ## Other Notable AI News

### Google, OpenAI and Anthropic are building a private AI regulator — and courting a former White House AI adviser to run it
Per **The Information (September 24)**, the three labs have agreed to establish a voluntary standards body, tentatively the **Frontier AI Standards Agency**, that could launch by the end of 2026 or in early 2027. Pillars under discussion include shared technical evaluations, **pre-release audits**, independent testing frameworks and standardized safety protocols — modeled on **FINRA**, the securities industry's self-regulatory organization. The leadership approach list is unusually senior: **Sriram Krishnan** (Trump White House senior AI policy adviser, January 2025 – June 2026) for CEO, plus **Arati Prabhakar** (Biden-era OSTP director), **Condoleezza Rice** and **David Friedberg**. OpenAI's chief global affairs officer **Chris Lehane** had confirmed on September 15 that the three had been coordinating on safety protocols for weeks. The structural objection is not new and not trivial: an SRO is a private body exercising quasi-governmental power with no democratic accountability, and the historical record of self-regulators is that they secure a social license to keep scaling rather than function as a brake. ([BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/google-openai-anthropic-plan-frontier-ai-standards-body-a-32926), [Lawfare — Designing a FINRA for Frontier AI](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/designing-a-finra-for-frontier-ai), [Yahoo Finance analysis](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/three-frontier-labs-building-finra-194134029.html))

### Anthropic asks shareholders for a founder supervoting class: 50.1% for seven people holding ~2% of equity
Reported **September 25**: Anthropic is asking shareholders to approve a special share class granting Dario Amodei and his six co-founders a combined **50.1% of voting power** on most corporate matters, contingent on **three of the seven** retaining a minimum stake. The structure mirrors Palantir's and is being put in place ahead of a potential IPO. Two checks survive: board elections are carved out, and the **Long-Term Benefit Trust** — whose members include former Fed chair **Ben Bernanke** — keeps authority to appoint a majority of the seven-seat board, though the co-founders' own allotment rises from two seats to three. Employees would get a separate class acting as tie-breakers on some issues. A company whose public argument is that frontier AI needs external accountability is simultaneously locking internal control against its future public shareholders. ([Reuters via Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/world-news/anthropic-seeks-50-1-voting-control-for-co-founders-ahead-of-ipo-126092500159_1.html), [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-seeks-palantir-style-voting-control-seven-co-founders-ahead-ipo), [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-proposes-supervoting-shares-to-give-cofounders-majority-control-report-4916355))

### Google is putting TPUs in orbit on October 1
**Project Suncatcher's** first satellite — a refrigerator-sized prototype built with **Planet**, carrying **four Trillium-generation TPUs** — flies on **SpaceX's Transporter-18** from Vandenberg on October 1. The mission is a survivability test: can data-center-class accelerators withstand launch g-forces, thermal cycling and radiation for about a year in low Earth orbit. Ground testing had TPUs and their HBM enduring 2–15 krad without hard failures, above the dose expected over a five-year mission. The long-range plan is a constellation of ~81 satellites linked by free-space optical comms, with a two-satellite link experiment in 2027. First hardware step for a moonshot announced in November 2025. ([Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/google-project-suncatcher-orbital-ai-data-center-tpu/), [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/chloe-aiello/google-is-about-to-launch-project-suncatcher-its-the-start-of-a-new-era-for-data-centers/91410240))

## Trending Open-Source Projects

**1. [vectorize-io/hindsight](https://github.com/vectorize-io/hindsight)** — ~28.1k stars, **+1,668 today**
"Agent memory that learns" — a memory system built around biomimetic structures rather than conversation-history retrieval, with four memory types (world facts, experiences, observations, mental models) and three operations: **retain, recall, reflect**. Claims state-of-the-art on **LongMemEval**, ships 60+ integrations across coding agents and frameworks, 25+ LLM providers, Docker/bare-metal/Kubernetes/managed deployment, and optional secrets/PII defense on stored memories. *Why it's trending:* by far the highest star velocity on the board today, and it lands on the consensus 2026 bottleneck — agents that re-learn the same thing every session. The "reflect" operation is the differentiator: most memory layers store and retrieve, this one claims to consolidate.

**2. [HKUDS/CLI-Anything](https://github.com/HKUDS/CLI-Anything)** — ~50.4k stars, **+413 today**
A pipeline that reads a piece of software's source and automatically generates a structured, Click-based CLI that agents can drive, then tests, documents and publishes it to PyPI — seven phases, one command. Ships **50+ generated harnesses** for Blender, GIMP, Inkscape, LibreOffice, ComfyUI, QGIS, Godot, Audacity and others, with **2,461 tests passing**. Critically, the CLIs delegate to the real application backends: Blender actually renders, LibreOffice actually writes the PDF. *Why it's trending:* it is the anti-computer-use bet — instead of teaching agents to click GUIs, mechanically generate the interface professional software should have had.

**3. [rohitg00/ai-engineering-from-scratch](https://github.com/rohitg00/ai-engineering-from-scratch)** — ~56.8k stars, **+347 today**
A free MIT-licensed curriculum: **523 lessons across 20 phases**, ~342 hours, from math foundations through transformers, agents and production systems, in Python, TypeScript, Rust and Julia. Each lesson runs problem → concept → build from scratch → use with frameworks → ship a reusable artifact (prompt, skill, agent, MCP server). Also packaged as a six-volume EPUB/PDF set with interactive tutors for Claude Code and Codex. *Why it's trending:* sustained daily accumulation rather than a spike — the "learn agents properly" demand is not slowing, and this is currently the most complete free answer.

**4. [router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI](https://github.com/router-for-me/CLIProxyAPI)** — ~53.1k stars, **+124 today**
Wraps multiple AI coding assistants as OpenAI-compatible API services, so subscription-based CLI agents can be addressed programmatically from other tools. *Why it's trending:* steady rather than viral, but it is the plumbing layer for the multi-agent orchestration category that dominated this month's trending list — if you want to fan work across five agents, you first need all five to speak one protocol.
