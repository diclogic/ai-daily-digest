# AI Daily Digest — September 10, 2026

**Top story:** Apple's September 9 event was the company's AI reset, and the headline detail is who built it. **Siri AI** ships in iOS 27 on **September 14** powered by **five new Apple Foundation Models custom-built in collaboration with Google** — two on-device (AFM 3 Core, AFM 3 Core Advanced) and three server-side on Private Cloud Compute, one of them running on **NVIDIA GPUs on Google Cloud**. The last large technology company without a frontier model of its own has now formally rented the model layer from the company that has one, and shipped it as the centerpiece of the iPhone 18 Pro launch.

Also today: the **Clay Mathematics Institute declined to call Navier–Stokes solved**, still listing it among the unsolved Millennium Prize problems as OpenAI's claim went to review — while **Sam Altman** publicly defended Sébastien Bubeck against Tristan Buckmaster's plagiarism and intimidation allegations. A 27-year-old **Anthropic** pretraining researcher, **Jacob Coxon**, quit the AI industry entirely and posted a warning that drew ~76 million views overnight. And AI capital had one of its largest single days on record: **Cognition** raised $2B at $48B and **Harvey** $550M at ~$15.6B.

### Introducing the Third Generation of Apple's Foundation Models
*Apple Machine Learning Research — [machinelearning.apple.com](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models)*

The technical report behind today's Siri AI launch, and the most substantive research publication in the window. The family is **five models**: **AFM 3 Core**, the next generation of Apple's ~3B-parameter dense on-device model; **AFM 3 Core Advanced**, Apple's most powerful on-device model and **natively multimodal** (driving expressive voices and higher-accuracy dictation); and **three cloud models** on Private Cloud Compute, including a **Cloud Pro** tier that runs on **NVIDIA GPUs on Google Cloud**.

The interesting admission is the collaboration itself. Apple built these **with Google**, after announcing at WWDC in June that the new Siri would be built on Gemini. A company whose entire AI pitch is on-device privacy is now shipping a stack whose top tier runs on a competitor's cloud, on a third party's silicon.

Two caveats worth keeping: **no official benchmark suite** has appeared in DeepSeek's public release documentation, and the speed figures are community-measured, not vendor-published. Note also the timing — this lands two days after US agencies named DeepSeek first in a joint distillation advisory.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Clay Mathematics Institute — Navier–Stokes is still listed as unsolved

The institution that owns the question answered it. **Martin Bridson**, president of the Clay Mathematics Institute, called OpenAI's announcement exciting but said evaluation would be **"deliberately unhurried"** and **"absolutely rigorous."** The CMI **has not accepted the result** and continues to list Navier–Stokes among its unsolved Millennium Prize problems.

The substance behind the refusal is the one this digest flagged yesterday: OpenAI's proof relies on a **smooth external force**, and most mathematicians regard the **unforced** options (A and B) as the deeper question — those remain definitively open. There has also been **no independent peer review**, and Clay's own rules require a result to stand in the published literature for **two years** before it can even be considered. OpenAI, for its part, says it does not intend to claim the $1M prize.

Why it matters: this is the cleanest available answer to "did AI solve a Millennium Prize problem," and it is *not yet, and possibly not that problem*. Yesterday the question was a press fight; today it is an institutional process with a two-year clock.

([Implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/clay-institute-navier-stokes-openai-proof-claim/) · [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/) · [DataCamp explainer](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/openai-navier-stokes-math-problem) · [Business Model Analyst on the two-year rule](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/openai-navier-stokes-verification-clock/))

### Sam Altman — "acted with integrity and generosity throughout"

Altman broke his silence on the dispute, backing **Sébastien Bubeck** on X: the team **"acted with integrity and generosity throughout,"** he wrote, saying it had been suggested that Buckmaster and Alpöge receive the award and that the team was met with **"unfounded accusations of plagiarism."**

He also gave the clearest account yet of how the effort started: because of **"rumors on the internet last week that Anthropic's models had solved a millennium problem and we were curious if ours could do it too."** That confirms the trigger Buckmaster alleged — OpenAI began on September 1 after hearing about the unpublished work — while framing it as ordinary curiosity rather than appropriation. Bubeck separately posted texts he says show he offered Buckmaster's team the option to publish first.

Why it matters: the timeline is no longer contested; only its meaning is. Altman has conceded the causal link between a rumor about a rival lab's result and a 10,000-agent run — which is precisely the dynamic **Terence Tao** warned about yesterday, now confirmed by the CEO rather than inferred by critics.

### Jacob Coxon (ex-Anthropic) — "gambling with our lives"

Not a tracked leader, and the loudest thing anyone said in the window. **Jacob Coxon**, a 27-year-old **pretraining researcher at Anthropic** — previously OpenAI technical staff from 2023 to July 2026 and a core contributor to GPT-4o — told the **Wall Street Journal** he is leaving not just Anthropic but **the AI industry entirely**, because he no longer believes any individual lab can safely build increasingly capable self-improving systems while racing competitors.

His claim: **"We're on track for a lot of the most aggressive of these scenarios where by the end of next year things could be out of control already."** He says competition between OpenAI and Anthropic, and with Chinese rivals, makes safety trade-offs inevitable, and that researchers inside the industry increasingly use words like **"crunchtime"** and **"endgame."** His conclusion is not that his employer is uniquely reckless but that the problem is **structurally bigger than any one company**, requiring industry-wide or governmental coordination. His seven-part thread on X drew roughly **76 million views** overnight.

Why it matters: this is a pretraining researcher — someone who works on the capability side, not the safety side — at the company that markets itself on safety, saying the race structure defeats any single lab's good intentions. It arrives while Anthropic is inside an IPO quiet period and cannot easily respond.

([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/anthropic-jacob-coxon-resignation-artificial-intelligence-1237072134/) · [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/anthropic-researcher-quits-warns-ai-could-kill-everyone-12418798) · [Common Dreams](https://www.commondreams.org/news/jacob-coxon-anthropic-whistleblower) · [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/gambling-lives-ai-researcher-quits-050000528.html) · [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/anthropic-researcher-coxon-resigns-warns-of-ai-endgame))

## Other Notable AI News

### Meta ran 332 ads containing AI-generated child sexual abuse material

A **Tech Transparency Project** investigation published **September 8–9** found **332 advertisements containing CSAM** that passed Meta's ad review and ran across **Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and Threads**. Most showed a photo of a real child digitally altered with AI to depict a sex act; TTP identified multiple real children, including **a young member of a European royal family**.

The finding that should end the "review at scale is hard" defense: TTP reported **129 active CSAM ads** directly to Meta, and in **73 cases — 57%** — Meta responded that the ad **did not violate its advertising standards**. The majority of the ads directed users to AI image and video generators from Chinese developers, many of them verified "nudify" apps. Meta both approved and **monetized** this inventory.

### Qualcomm lands AWS for custom inference silicon — up to $60B through 2036

Announced **September 8** and still moving markets: **Qualcomm** and **AWS** signed a multi-generation deal to co-develop **custom AI inference ASICs** and **optical interconnect at up to 1.6 Tbit/s**. The structure is a **warrant for up to 25 million QCOM shares at $161.26**, vesting as AWS hits purchase milestones, against a **$60B ceiling** on purchases through **2036**. Revenue starts in Qualcomm's fiscal Q1 2027 (the December 2026 quarter); chips are already in production. QCOM rose ~9%.

This is Qualcomm's **first Western hyperscaler** customer for datacenter silicon, targeting decode-heavy inference with a memory-capacity-optimized architecture it calls **High Bandwidth Compute**. Read alongside OpenAI's Jalapeno disclosure above: the inference layer is being deliberately de-NVIDIA'd from several directions at once.
