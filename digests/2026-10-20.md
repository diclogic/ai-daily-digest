## Notable Papers & Research

### CXBench: agents that get the right answer the wrong way, and the graders that never notice
*[arXiv:2610.25954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25954)* (submitted Oct 19) — the only major agent benchmark I could confirm as posted in the last 24 hours.

A benchmark for **process-compliant** LLM agents in regulated customer-experience workflows — refunds, account changes, disclosures — where *how* the task was done is as auditable as whether it ended correctly. Across **seven models and 120 tasks**, a large share of runs that passed outcome checks failed process checks, and only a small fraction passed both. The authors report that adding process checks **reorders the model ranking**, and ship trace-linked criteria so each failure can be inspected rather than just counted.

**Why it matters:** every agent eval that scores only the final state is, on this evidence, overstating deployability in exactly the industries that will be asked for an audit trail. Specific pass rates come from the abstract summary; I could not open the paper to confirm the per-model numbers.

### Pack Light, CarryOn: stop replaying the transcript, carry forward objects instead
*Yuchen Lu, Julian McAuley, Lei Zhang* — [arXiv:2610.18784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18784) (submitted Oct 13), [code](https://github.com/Ethannju/carryon)

CarryOn is a context runtime that rebuilds an agent's working context out of **reusable task objects** — carrier records for completed work, an execution context for what remains, and a short live tail of recent steps — instead of re-feeding interaction history. On each fold it revises only the carrier records that new evidence actually affects and passes the rest through unchanged. Reported gains: **+5.8% on OSWorld 2.0** and **+7.0% on BrowseComp-Plus**, with token use down **51.9%** and **22.9%** respectively; the repo README reports Claude Opus 4.7 going from **43.9% → 49.7%** partial score on OSWorld 2.0 at **52% fewer tokens and 36% lower cost**.

**Why it matters:** it was the **#1 paper on Hugging Face's daily list on October 19**, five days after posting, which is the pattern of a result practitioners are actually trying. Authors' own numbers, no independent replication.

### Learning Self-Orchestration in Decentralized Agent Swarms: let agents bid for the right to act
*Guowei Xu, Zhenting Qi, Amani Kiruga, Himabindu Lakkaraju, Yilun Du* (Harvard-affiliated) — [arXiv:2610.22322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22322) (submitted Oct 15), [code](https://github.com/Embodied-Minds-Lab/MPT)

**Market-based post-training (MPT)** trains the action policy and the orchestration policy together: each agent submits a **bid** expressing willingness to participate, the bids decide who acts, and the collective outcome is routed back as local training credit. Tested on embodied crafting, robot navigation and deep research, it beats monolithic post-training. Ablations find that *both* the acting and the bidding benefit from being learned, and that **learned bids produce better training signal for the action policy** than hand-built routing. Trained swarms also adapt faster to related tasks, improve with larger populations at inference time, and **degrade less under agent dropout**.

**Why it matters:** multi-agent systems today are orchestrated by a hand-written supervisor. This is a credible argument that the supervisor should be learned from the same reward as the workers — and the dropout robustness is the part that matters for anyone running swarms in production.

### Has GPT-6-Astra Achieved Embodied Intelligence? — competent, not yet practical
*Zhongang Cai and 59 co-authors (SenseNova joint research team)* — [arXiv:2610.22471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22471) (submitted Oct 15), [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22471)

A 60-author evaluation across **12 benchmarks, harness ablations and eight real-robot platforms**. The verdict is deliberately split: the model has largely reached embodied **competence** but not embodied **intelligence**. It approaches saturation in simulation, while real-robot tests expose two costs that benchmarks hide — the in-context demonstrations it needs are expensive to collect, and **longer execution does not reliably buy higher success** under physical constraints. The authors propose a prototype benchmark built from real-world needs whose **clock keeps running while the agent thinks**.

**Why it matters:** it sat at the top of Hugging Face's Daily Papers this week, and the wall-clock-during-reasoning framing is the sharpest critique yet of how embodied evals are scored. Note this is a third-party evaluation of a competitor's model, published by a lab with its own embodied ambitions.

### DHS bars DeepSeek from federal civilian agencies — including models running on American servers
DHS ordered federal civilian agencies to stop acquiring **DeepSeek** products and to **remove them from existing IT systems**, invoking the **Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018**. The order was posted **October 19** and scheduled for Federal Register publication **October 20** (docket DHS-2026-0628), and it reaches **third-party products built on DeepSeek models**, not just DeepSeek's own services. Cited risks: the ability to "track or digitally fingerprint" users, unauthorized transfer of sensitive data to China, intelligence gathering on US persons, and introduction of malware ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/19/trump-administration-orders-ban-on-deepseek-in-federal-agencies/), [Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/102669-dhs-banning-deepseek-from-us-federal-civilian-agencies), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/dhs-bans-deepseek-across-federal-government-networks/)).

It lands two days after DeepSeek opened its Search and Fetch APIs to all developers, and the same week DeepSeek said it plans to pre-train on Huawei **Ascend 950DT** silicon. **Reporting caveat:** signing dates conflict badly across outlets (July 24 vs June 26), and no outlet I could reach quoted the Federal Register text directly.

### Microsoft: treat your own agents as insiders
Microsoft's **October 19** announcement introduces **Guardian Agents** that continuously supervise agent behaviour, plus three identity controls: **Dynamic Permissions** (authority scoped to a specific task), **Dynamic Agent Risk** (continuous behavioural evaluation), and **Dynamic Conditional Access** (permissions that move with the risk score). The design point that matters: security teams can **restrict an agent without revoking the permissions of the employee who authorized it**. Defender's discovery now recognizes **60 types of local agent** after 35 were added — reportedly including **Claude Code**, **OpenAI Codex** and **GitHub Copilot** ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/19/microsoft-targets-the-agentic-insider-with-new-controls-for-rogue-ai-agents/), [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/10/microsoft-introduces-new-controls-to-limit-ai-agent-privileges/)).

**Why it matters:** this is the first major vendor to put the "agentic insider" threat model into shipping identity infrastructure rather than a whitepaper, and the fact that **detecting developer coding agents is now a security feature** tells you how enterprises have started to classify them. Microsoft's own framing describes a planned approach, not a guarantee.
