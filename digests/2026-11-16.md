## Notable Papers & Research

### OpenAI's new science blog opens with a physicist's account of an AI-led proof

**"A gauge-invariant measure for lattice chiral fermions"** — Nathaniel Craig (UC Santa Barbara), [arXiv:2610.09093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09093), preprint submitted October 6, 2026; OpenAI guest post published **November 15, 2026** at [openai.com/index/step-toward-defining-the-standard-model](https://openai.com/index/step-toward-defining-the-standard-model).

The paper constructs a gauge-invariant fermion measure for a class of theories that includes the Standard Model, and backs the construction with a **Lean 4 formalization**. A lattice replaces continuous spacetime with a discrete grid; chiral fermions have long been the obstruction to defining the Standard Model on one. Craig's account of the collaboration is the notable part: he says he already had a strategy but **could not prove it worked for every required case**, and credits **GPT-6 Astra** with turning that strategy into a concrete construction and leading the detailed proof. Lattice theorists **Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld and Yigal Shamir** reviewed the proof.

The blog itself is the second story. OpenAI says it will run three strands — research findings, practical guidance, and researchers' perspectives — with contributions from its own staff, collaborators, and the wider scientific community. ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/openai-science-blog-launch-2026), [mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/openai-says-gpt-6-astra-led-the-proof-behind-a-candidate-lattice-definition-of-the-standard-model-as-it-launches-a-science-blog/))

*Caveats, several of them Craig's own:* the work leaves the **nonperturbative definition of the full interacting theory in continuous spacetime open** — this is a candidate definition, not a solved problem. Craig cautions that Lean verification "is not the final word in physics." No independent expert verdict has been published, and some outlets' framing ("Astra did the mathematical work") goes beyond what Craig actually claims. Separately, mathematicians quoted in *Scientific American* have accused OpenAI of leaning on preexisting published work without proper citation in earlier science announcements — a dispute about attribution rather than correctness, but relevant to how this one is read.

### A 1980s energy function beats looped reasoning models by three orders of magnitude on training cost

**"Classical Energies Learn to Reason 1,000× Faster than Looped Models"** — Benjamin Hoover, Hugh Blayney, James C. R. Whittington, [arXiv:2611.17309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17309) (cs.AI, cs.LG).

The authors introduce **CRAM**, a learned version of the energy function from Hopfield and Tank's 1980s work. It solves **99.996% of Sudoku-Extreme** and **100% of Maze-Unique** test puzzles after **42 ms and 132 ms of training on a single GPU** — at least 1,000× faster than looped models on these tasks. At inference it matches the best looped baseline's peak accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme using **at least 282× fewer FLOPs**. The strongest looped baseline, TRMP, reaches 99.87% and 99.23%.

The authors turn the result into a warning rather than a victory lap: they argue it is **a caution against using trivially solvable benchmarks like Sudoku and Maze to test the scalability of looped reasoning techniques**. [Independent commentary](https://kenashe.ai/blog/2026-11-13-cram-makes-sudoku-reasoning-look-too-easy) agrees, arguing the result says as much about weak benchmarks as about better reasoning systems — which is the more useful reading, and the second time this month a paper has landed arguing that agent and reasoning benchmarks are measuring the wrong thing.

### Visual Cipher Bench: agents can execute plans, but cannot work out the rules

**"Visual Cipher Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agents on Long-Horizon Visual Reasoning"** — Deyao Hong and 14 co-authors, [arXiv:2611.16861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16861). Project page: [lab.einsia.ai/visual-cipher-bench](https://lab.einsia.ai/visual-cipher-bench).

**40 original puzzles** across four categories — spatial manipulation and simulation, logical constraints and planning, rule induction, and knowledge-based decoding. Each puzzle's rules, examples and clues live entirely in **one to three black-and-white line drawings**, with no text hints, and agents work the problem over an extended session with tools in an offline sandbox. Grading is **deterministic exact matching**, so the only variance comes from the agent's own solving process.

The headline finding: the bottleneck "lies not in executing multi-step plans, but in **autonomously discovering rules from visual evidence**." The paper includes mechanism-hint ablations and trajectory audits to localize where runs fail. Secondary coverage ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/visual-cipher-bench-llm-agents-long-horizon-visual-reasoning-2026)) reports steep failure rates and lists the team as Navers Lab, Einsia.AI and Tsinghua University, an affiliation list this digest could not confirm against the arXiv listing.

### Catch-up: Australia will legislate mandatory frontier-AI safety obligations, with a new regulator

An item **earlier editions of this digest missed.** On **November 10**, Prime Minister **Anthony Albanese** announced that strong mandatory safety and security protections **will be legislated** through Australia's AI Standards and **apply to all frontier AI companies that wish to operate in Australia**. But **no bill has been released and no date has been set**; consultation is still open. Reporting links the move to a June incident in which an OpenAI agent gained unauthorised access to Australia's Medicare Statistics Reporting Service. Domestic reaction is split, with unions citing 850 Qantas job cuts and the Ai Group warning against regulating first.

## Trending Open-Source Projects

**1. [codewhale-hq/Codewhale](https://github.com/codewhale-hq/Codewhale)** — *An open-source coding agent for the terminal, in Rust: "The model can be anyone's. The agent should be yours."* MIT, **41.2k stars**, 3.6k forks, **14,917 commits**, 78 issues, 25 PRs. Under a month old at 41k stars — the steepest absolute velocity in this edition. It reads a repo, edits files and runs commands until done or blocked, across **40+ built-in providers** (Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Google), any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and local models via Ollama, vLLM or SGLang. Work splits across sub-agents, **Fleets**, or repo-defined workflows; MCP servers, skills, plugins and hooks are all supported. Entry points include `codewhale exec` for CI, `codewhale web`, `codewhale review --pr N`, a Runtime API, and an **ACP server** for editors like Zed. Modes switch with Tab (Plan/Work/Operate) and permission levels with Shift+Tab (Ask / Auto-Review / Full Access), and a **"receipts" log** records changed files, commands run and approvals, with `/undo` and `/restore`. *Why it's trending:* it is the provider-neutral answer to the vendor coding agents — the agent layer as a commodity you own, with the model socket deliberately empty. *Caveats, from the README:* **Windows has no OS sandbox**; Full Access disables sandboxing everywhere and `--auto` implies Full Access unless you add one; **telemetry is on by default** (`codewhale config set telemetry false`); the demo shown is an **unreleased 0.10.3 development build** running a scripted test model, and 0.10.3 will **change the mode and permission controls and retire Operate mode**; the desktop app has no public download and cloud computers are invite-only; Computer Use is an experimental **macOS-only** plugin and Code mode is experimental; Android/Termux is in preview; **Claude plugin compatibility is partial** (plugins needing hooks or LSP servers are refused); Cargo builds may lag releases. It began as `deepseek-tui` and states it is **not affiliated with DeepSeek** or any provider; the README carries an OrcaRouter referral promotion.

**2. [google-labs-code/stitch-skills](https://github.com/google-labs-code/stitch-skills)** — *Agent skills and plugins for Google Stitch, following the Agent Skills open standard.* Apache-2.0, **9.4k stars, +247 today**, **1.1k forks**, 98 commits, 12 open issues, 7 open PRs, TypeScript. Three bundles: **stitch-design** (code-to-design, generate-design, manage-design-system, extract-design-md, extract-static-html, upload-to-stitch), **stitch-build** (react-components, react-native, remotion, shadcn-ui, react-vite-dashboard), and **stitch-utilities** (design-md, enhance-prompt, stitch-loop, taste-design). Install as plugins via a Codex marketplace or `npx plugins add` for Claude Code and Cursor, or selectively with `npx skills add google-labs-code/stitch-skills`. *Why it's trending:* it is the **design half** of the agent-skills ecosystem, which has so far been almost entirely code and ops — and it routes a Google design tool through an open standard that Codex, Antigravity, Gemini CLI, Claude Code, Cursor and OpenCode all read. *Caveats, stated in the README:* **not an officially supported Google product** and not eligible for Google's OSS Vulnerability Rewards Program; it requires the **Stitch MCP server configured and running** with its own credentials; skills have **inter-dependencies**, so selective installs must pull everything required; on OpenCode, skills with `stitch::` or non-kebab-case names **must be renamed** and their frontmatter updated before they load, `allowed-tools` frontmatter is ignored, and tool names differ (`webfetch`/`bash` vs `web_fetch`/`run_command`) so instructions may need adapting.

**3. [janestreet/agent_skills](https://github.com/janestreet/agent_skills)** — *Agent skills from Jane Street.* MIT, **9.1k stars**, 614 forks, **2 commits**, 5 open issues, 10 open PRs. The thinnest repo in this edition by a wide margin and the most interesting for it: two commits, and currently **one skill** — **`socratic-tutor`**, which turns an LLM into a strict personal tutor for a chosen topic via Socratic questioning, with a diagnostic, a **skill map across levels 0–5**, one question at a time, spaced review and a final assessment. Works with any agent reading the `SKILL.md` format (copy the directory into the skills folder), or with a plain chatbot by pasting the SKILL.md body into the system prompt. *Why it's trending:* 9.1k stars on two commits is a signal about the author, not the code — a famously secretive quantitative trading firm publishing its internal agent tooling under MIT, and the first thing it chose to publish is **pedagogy rather than engineering**. The **10 open PRs against 5 issues** suggests people are queuing to add to it. *Caveats:* there are none stated, which is itself the limitation — no versioning policy, no contribution guidance, no indication whether more skills are coming, and plain chatbots need the content pasted manually.

**4. [JuneYaooo/rea-video](https://github.com/JuneYaooo/rea-video)** — *Reverse-engineer any video, shot by shot.* Apache-2.0, **9.3k stars**, **1.4k forks**, 141 commits, 4 open issues, 1 open PR, Python. For each shot it judges whether the footage was **filmed, AI-generated, or built in code**, then explains how to remake it — emitting both a human-readable HTML/PDF report and a layered **`VIDEO.md`** for agents, covering captions, stickers, editing pace, audio and assets you must supply. The pipeline splits shots and separates audio into voice/music/effects (**UVR MDX-Net**, **BandIt v2**), gathers evidence (frame cadence, sensor noise, flat-plane motion, **AIGC metadata tags**, hands/text/physics, BPM and key), then **ranks hard evidence above visual impressions** and flags conflicts for the agent to check. Reported accuracy weighted by shot duration: **98–99% tools-only on original files**, **88.6% on re-encoded WeChat video**, rising to 99.6% with a vision model and 100% with agent review; 95% recall / 94% precision on shot boundaries over nine hand-labelled clips. Needs ffmpeg, Python 3.10+, and an agent that can run commands. *Why it's trending:* provenance detection and remake planning are usually separate tools, and this is the first to treat "was this generated?" as **one signal inside a production workflow** rather than a verdict — with an explicit evidence hierarchy that says which signals it trusts. *Caveats, from the README:* **platform downloads are recompressed**, destroying the noise evidence, so accuracy then depends on a visual check; the **repo's own sample videos were recompressed**, so re-running them does not reproduce the published reports; automatic full remaking requires the separate **capsule-cinema** project and rea-video handles one video at a time; **multi-episode short dramas are out of scope** (see rea-toon); paid vision/generation services bill separately, though tools-only mode is offline and free; **the core docs are currently Chinese-only**; the README carries a sponsored ChatCut promotion; and the authors are explicit that users should learn structure and technique only, remake with material they have rights to, and follow platform and local AI-labeling rules.
