## Notable Papers & Research

### Claude found 663 theorems in Gödel's notebooks — and about 50 of them are wrong
The **Guardian reported on November 13** that **Jan von Plato**, a logician at the University of Helsinki, gave Claude his transcriptions of **Kurt Gödel's 16 notebooks (1,009 pages, early 1940s, written in Gabelsberger shorthand already obsolete in Gödel's lifetime)**, along with six of his own books on Gödel and other scholarly literature. Von Plato had been working at roughly **a page a week since 2017**; Claude produced an **~650-page explanation of the whole corpus within days** and identified **663 results for which Gödel had a proof, a sketch, or an idea of one**. On von Plato's account, roughly **400 verified as stated, ~200 needed repair** (a sharpened statement or an added assumption) **and ~50 were false**. The headline find: a **nearly complete proof of the independence of the axiom of choice**, which Gödel missed because one definition he used was wrong — though he had written the correct definition two years earlier. Von Plato checked Claude's reconstruction **in the Coq proof assistant and it passed**, and believes Gödel might have won the **1940 Fields Medal** had he published. *Why it matters:* this is the most concrete case yet of a model doing archival mathematical reconstruction rather than competition problem-solving. *Caveats, and they are large:* **Joel David Hamkins** (Notre Dame) told reporters that **"how much is Claude and how much is Gödel is a job for the historians"**; von Plato says every page still needs checking; the verification split differs between outlets, so treat 400/200/50 as provisional; and **the Guardian piece itself could not be read from this session**. ([Guardian via ua.news](https://ua.news/en/world/shi-dopomig-viiaviti-v-notatkakh-gedelia-sotni-nevidomikh-teorem-the-guardian), [mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/claude-helped-decode-kurt-godels-shorthand-notebooks-turning-up-663-theorems-and-a-near-proof-he-claimed-in-1966/), [Archyde](https://www.archyde.com/ai-helps-unlock-hundreds-of-theorems-in-godels-notebooks/), [newsbytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/helsinki-team-uses-claude-to-uncover-previously-unknown-godel-theorems/tldr))

### Anthropic: our own safety evals are saturated, gameable, and configuration-dependent
**"Building strong safety evaluations"** (November 13; Appel, Frondorf, Hadfield, Hiscott, Jagadish, Longpre, Tucker, Wittmershaus and others) was **read directly today** and is unusually candid. The argument: safety evals measure *propensity* to cause harm, not capability, and the field is heading for **"a replication crisis"** of the kind psychology had — evals that are easy to game, don't measure the construct they claim, and don't reproduce. The numbers do the work. **Single-turn harmless-response rates sit at 94–98% across recent models**, which means those suites are saturated and now function only as regression checks. A romance-scam eval put **Opus 5 at 98% and Opus 5.5 at 94%** — a gap that **was not statistically significant**, and **multi-turn confidence intervals can exceed ±10 points**. Most striking: for **Claude Fable 5.1**, the **appropriate-response rate differed by 34 points between the API and claude.ai** on multi-turn safety evals, and a single system-prompt self-harm mitigation produced **94% on claude.ai against 60% on the API** — i.e. the same weights score very differently depending on deployment surface. Recommendations include preregistering analyses, validating graders against human judgment, reporting uncertainty, sample-size floors (**100 items for complex multi-turn, 1,000 for simple single-turn**), and continuous evaluation during training rather than only before release. The post also notes Anthropic **hardened its eval environments and paused internet-access evals after incidents**. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations))

### OpenAI ships ChatGPT Wallet — agents that pay, with the card networks on board
**ChatGPT Wallet** launched **November 13** as the payment layer for ChatGPT's **"dot" agents**, built with **American Express, Mastercard and Visa**. Cards are **tokenized**, so neither the agent nor the merchant sees the number; before each purchase the user reviews **items, total and card** and approves with a **biometric check (Face ID or fingerprint)**. At launch it is **US-only, ChatGPT Pro, 18+**, and at least one report says **virtual cards cannot be added**. OpenAI also published a **Commerce Partner Playbook** aimed at stopping merchants from rejecting legitimate agent purchases. *Why it matters:* the hard part of agentic commerce was never the shopping, it was the liability and fraud model — and OpenAI has answered it by borrowing the networks' existing one rather than inventing a new rail. *Caveats:* **some sites will block wallet purchases**; sources disagree on card eligibility (GSMArena adds Chase, Android Authority lists only the three networks); and **openai.com was unreachable from this session**, so none of this was read from the source post. **1. [google/elephant-goldfish-model](https://github.com/google/elephant-goldfish-model)** — *An open-source plugin for designing and reviewing software with coding agents.* Apache-2.0, **18.7k stars, +5,560 this week** (the week's largest gain by a repo not previously featured here), 1.4k forks, **202 commits**, 70 open issues, 34 open PRs. The idea is in the name. **"Elephant"** is the agent holding your full project context: it explores the problem and drafts the specification. **"Goldfish"** reviewers are fresh subagent contexts that read only the draft and its referenced source material — **no conversation history** — which is what surfaces the assumptions the Elephant stopped seeing. The human makes trade-off calls and approves before implementation. Ships skills for brainstorming, drafting, resolving review findings, bootstrapping docs, reviewing designs, configurable review loops, and **drift reports comparing docs against code**; installs into **Claude Code, Codex CLI, Antigravity CLI** and the ChatGPT/Codex desktop app, with slash commands like `/egm:egm-review`. *Why it's trending:* it is a clean, cheap answer to context rot — the failure mode where an agent that has been in a conversation too long can no longer see what it assumed — and it is the rare agent-workflow pack with a stated epistemic mechanism rather than a prompt library. *Caveats:* the README says plainly this is **not an officially supported Google product**; it **requires** an agent that can spin up fresh subagent contexts and **aborts the review if the agent cannot**; reviews explicitly do not replace tests or human judgment; some agent versions have **known command-completion bugs** (issues #196, #220); and bootstrap/drift reporting is designed for software codebases only.

**2. [anthropics/claude-quickstarts](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-quickstarts)** — *Deployable starter projects for the Claude API.* MIT, **20.9k stars, +1,731 this week**, 3.5k forks, **118 commits**, **129 open issues and 115 open PRs**. What makes it worth a slot now rather than as evergreen docs is how much of it is **Managed Agents** scaffolding: a **Chat SDK** with a persistent session per conversation that swaps adapters to run on Slack, Teams, Discord, Telegram or WhatsApp; a **Daily Brief** agent that summarises Slack and GitHub PR activity each weekday and deploys with `ant apply`; a **Knowledge Wiki** that distils a document corpus (the sample is an M&A data room from SEC EDGAR) into a versioned wiki **with provenance on every fact**; **Linear** and **Slack** bots; an **MCP server** exposing the Sessions API as nine tools; **Sentry triage** and **Sentry Autofix**, which reproduces an error with a failing test and pushes a branch while a host opens the PR so a person reviews every fix — with a **per-session spend cap**. Alongside those sit the older computer- and browser-use demos (`computer_toolset_20260801`, `browser_toolset_20260801`) and an autonomous coding agent that persists progress through git. *Why it's trending:* the Autofix and Daily Brief patterns are the first official examples of **scheduled and webhook-driven** agents with human gates and budget caps built in — the institutional plumbing third-party projects have been improvising. *Caveats:* the toolset quickstarts **demonstrate the interface only and are not production code**; **Computer Use Best Practices runs directly on macOS** and the README advises a VM; Managed Agents projects each need their own services, credentials and vaults; and **115 open PRs against 118 commits on `main`** is a striking ratio — contributions are arriving far faster than they land.

**3. [tradermonty/claude-trading-skills](https://github.com/tradermonty/claude-trading-skills)** — *Claude Skills for equity investors and traders.* MIT, **4.3k stars, +69 today**, **880 forks**, **996 commits**, 65 open issues, 20 open PRs. A skill pack built around **process rather than prediction**: workflows for a daily market-regime check, a weekly core-portfolio review, swing-opportunity screening, **trade-memory logging** and a monthly performance review, with a navigator skill that recommends a workflow from a stated goal. Skills cover market regime, core portfolio, dividend income, swing opportunities, trade planning, trade memory, strategy research and satellite strategies (some marked beta). Ships as **`.skill` archives for the Claude web app** plus source folders for Claude Code, with a Hermes-based companion agent. **Python 3.11–3.13**; a **starter path of five skills runs with no paid APIs** using public CSVs, chart screenshots and local files, while the fuller setup can use **FMP** (free tier, 250 req/day), **FINVIZ Elite** ($39.50/mo) and **Alpaca** paper trading. *Why it's trending:* the **880 forks against 4.3k stars** is the tell — an unusually high fork ratio for a skill pack, meaning people are actually adapting it rather than bookmarking it. And its design premise runs against the grain: the README states the goal is explicitly **not** to have AI make buy or sell decisions. *Caveats, which the repo states clearly:* education and research only, **not financial, tax or legal advice**, **"not a signal service or a promise of profitability,"** provided **AS IS without warranty**, not designed for fully automated trading or scalping, with human decision gates central and the user responsible for sizing, compliance and broker use.

**4. [Likely7/Veyra-NRVideo](https://github.com/Likely7/Veyra-NRVideo)** — *Real-time GPU video enhancement for Windows.* GPL-3.0 (the bundled Chiaki streaming component is AGPL-3.0 with an OpenSSL exception), **1.7k stars, +256 today**, 76 forks, **1,336 commits**, 44 open issues, 18 open PRs; the README cites **79k+ portable-package downloads and 103k+ across all release assets** (download events, not unique users). Version 2.1.5 applies **AI super-resolution (including dual SR), layered noise reduction, HDR handling, anti-aliasing and frame generation** to local files, images, capture cards, screen capture, **PS5 via Remote Play**, PC streaming via Sunshine, and experimental Xbox — with a live preview, an editable export queue (trimming, MP4/MKV, multiple audio and subtitle tracks), and both a **List mode** for ordered effects and a **Node mode** for building a processing graph. UI in Simplified/Traditional Chinese, English and Japanese. *Why it's trending:* it is the week's reminder that consumer-facing AI is not all agents — this is inference tuned to a frame budget on hardware people already own, and it took the day's third-largest daily gain from a small base. *Caveats, and the repo is refreshingly explicit about them:* the repository description's **"Bring PS5 GTA6 to 120FPS with DLSS 5"** has **no benchmark behind it** and the README concedes "community runtimes are not vendor certification or a complete official DLSS 5 integration"; **"submitted FPS is not physical display FPS or end-to-end latency"**; Xbox support is unofficial and experimental; **Windows 11 x64 + DirectX 12** required, with AMD noise reduction needing **HIP 7** drivers and most testing done on an **RTX 5070**; there are **unresolved reports on RTX 30/40 noise reduction and frame generation, FSR 4 ML hardware, and NR-related TDR and VRAM growth**; Node mode supports neither offline export nor List mode's global NR protection region; and upgrading by dropping an old `veyra.ini` or `runtime_local` folder onto a new package is called out as a mistake.
