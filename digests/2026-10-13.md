## Notable Papers & Research

### EvoBelief: Falsifiable World Models for Self-Improving LLM Agents
**Nine authors, Tulane University and Amazon** ([arXiv:2610.12789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12789), submitted Oct 8; [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12789))

The clearest result in the window, and it inverts the usual self-improvement recipe. Most agent self-improvement works on the *policy* — how the agent acts. EvoBelief leaves the language model frozen and evolves the agent's **beliefs about environment dynamics** during deployment, represented as explicitly falsifiable hypotheses. The loop: a surprising outcome or a decision-relevant unknown spawns a hypothesis; the hypothesis must survive later observations and controlled experiments before it is allowed to guide a prediction or plan, and only within its supported scope; contradicting evidence narrows or retires the rule. Every rule stays tied to the evidence behind it and the decisions it touches.

The headline number is the kind that usually means the baselines were badly matched, so read it carefully: on DiscoveryWorld's Reactor Lab, **83.3% of Normal-tier and 58.3% of Challenge-tier instances completed, against 0% for every evaluated baseline**. Across four benchmarks spanning static dynamics, hidden drift and scientific discovery, the authors report both improved task performance and rule reuse across tasks. Caveats: all figures are self-reported, and we could only reach the abstract and introduction — the methods, ablations and the ALFWorld comparison are unverified.

Set it against the **Learn2Play Bench** finding in this digest on October 12 — that keeping complete records of actions and feedback beats summarizing them into rules — and the two papers are arguing opposite sides of the same question. Learn2Play says summarized rules lose information; EvoBelief says rules are fine if they are falsifiable and scoped. Whoever is right, agent memory layers that summarize into unfalsifiable rules lose under both readings.

### Yann LeCun in Seoul: AI takeover is "nothing more than science fiction," and alignment-by-training is "realistically impossible"
([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202610121551017/), Oct 12; [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/113258/lecun-says-ai-running-out-of-control-is-science-fiction-world-models-could-be-alternative), [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/tech-science/20261012/ai-pioneer-sees-koreas-role-in-developing-world-model), [Korea Daily](https://en.koreadaily.com/ai-doomsday-yann-lecun-dismisses-threat/))

The only tracked leader with genuinely new remarks in the window, delivered at an "Industrial AI" press briefing at the Westin Josun Seoul the day before the fifth IIAC in Tokyo. (Outlets name the event inconsistently — "Global AI Leaders Seoul Dialogue," "Industrial AI Sovereignty and Global Co-Development.")

Four claims worth separating:
1. **On doom:** the scenario of someone discovering human-level intelligence and robots suddenly seizing control is *"closer to science fiction"*; AI taking over the world is *"nothing more than science fiction."*
2. **On why current safety methods fail** — the technically interesting line: *"It is realistically impossible to train for every possible question and tune the system to give only non-dangerous answers."*
3. **On the alternative:** objective-driven AI built on world models, where the system predicts the consequences of its actions and acts under pre-set goals and safety constraints.
4. **On LLMs:** they *"merely replay human knowledge and do not understand the real world."* He also said base models will ultimately have to be open source, and that physical AI will affect Korea significantly.

Why it matters more than the usual LeCun broadside: claim 2 is not a dismissal of safety, it is a **mechanism argument against RLHF-style safety as a sufficient strategy** — and the architecture he proposes as the fix, constraints evaluated against a predictive model of consequences, is structurally close to what **Nadella asked for on October 10** (controls outside the model that the model cannot bypass) and what the **Praxa** paper above appears to formalize. Three very different parties arriving at "the safety property should live in the architecture, not the weights" inside one week is the week's real convergence, and it is being obscured by the fact that LeCun opens by calling the risk fictional. Standing note: his October 1 Fortune remarks (no concerns about extinction, Amodei "deluded," no need for new regulation) remain the position of record on regulation, and **AMI Labs has still shipped nothing** — he continues to decline to describe its first product.

**1. [storytold/effectcraft](https://github.com/storytold/effectcraft)** — **~5.7–5.9k stars, ~2.9–3.0k forks**, Rust
An open-source motion-graphics and compositing tool written from scratch in pure Rust — a clean-room After Effects. **Why it's here:** it is the only fresh entrant on the board with a verifiable creation date, **October 1, 2026**, which makes ~5.9k stars in eleven days real velocity rather than accumulated goodwill, and it is already at **v0.6.0** with ~243 open issues and ~150 PRs. Note the forks-to-stars ratio: roughly **one fork per two stars**, which is extraordinary and suggests people are actually building on it (one third-party fork already advertises OpenFX plug-in support for RSMB, Twixtor and Sapphire). It is the fourth repo from the same org to surface in this digest in a week — after `artcraft`, `photocraft` and `lightcraft` — which is the signal worth tracking: **a single team is systematically cloning the Adobe suite in Rust**, and the AI angle across all four is that the primary remote-control surface is an agent interface rather than the feature list.

**2. [ginwind/VLA-JEPA](https://github.com/ginwind/VLA-JEPA)** — star count not reliably sourced, Apache-2.0
A vision-language-action model augmented with a **latent world model**, published at **ECCV 2026**, updated within the last two weeks. **Why it's here:** it is the third world-action-model codebase to surface in this digest in two days, after Stanford's **OpenWAM** and **OpenMOSS/EasyWAM** — and unlike those two it is not a framework but an architecture claim, that a JEPA-style latent predictor is the right thing to bolt onto a VLA. Listed without a star figure rather than with an unsourced one. Directly relevant to LeCun's Seoul argument above, since JEPA is his own line of work and this is someone else's implementation of it inside a robot policy.

**3. [morluto/rea](https://github.com/morluto/rea)** — **100k–169k stars** (sources vary wildly), **~25k forks**
*A deliberate repeat — featured here October 7, 11 and 12 — because what changed today is not the star count.* An MCP server and CLI that connects coding agents (Claude Code among them) to reverse-engineering tooling such as Ghidra, to analyze apps and binaries without source. **Why it's back:** the project has crossed from "fast-growing repo" into **an unresolved legal and safety story**, and anyone who starred it this week should read the caveats. Its own SECURITY.md states the **local provider bridge is not a sandbox** — opening an untrusted binary runs parsing code with your user permissions, so untrusted targets belong in an isolated environment. On the legal side, commentators flag anti-decompilation clauses in user agreements, trade-secret exposure, and a structural **clean-room problem**: because the same agent performs both analysis and reconstruction, it cannot meet the isolation that clean-room reimplementation legally requires — which is precisely the standard the `storytold` repos above claim to meet. The README restricts use to lawful work and places authorization on the user; the project provides no licenses and does not authorize bypassing activation or DRM. Separately, the maintainers have stated they have **issued no token** and that coins trading on the name are unaffiliated. Maturity note: 100+ contributors and 170+ open issues and PRs; expect rough edges outside the showcased workflows.
