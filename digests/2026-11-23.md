### The Lean certification: 17 million lines, and a formal objection

The headline artifact is not a paper. On **November 20** Alpöge released his proof as a **Lean library** — reported at **~17.37 million lines across 175,187 Lean 4 files** in a 14-part archive — and on **November 21** the **Lean FRO** and the **Mathlib Initiative** published a joint statement ([leanprover-community blog](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/); [GitHub discussion #146](https://github.com/leanprover-community/blog/discussions/146); [@leanprover](https://x.com/leanprover/status/2124159121645801562)) stating: *"We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct."* The certified statement is **Mathlib's own `RiemannHypothesis`**, vetted by Lean and number-theory experts — which is the specific gap flagged as pending two days ago. **MIT's Andrew Sutherland** independently compiled the library, and the exported proof term was replayed through several independent checkers including Lean's official kernel.

**Three caveats that the Lean community itself raises**, visible in the discussion thread: the certification covers **the code, not a human-readable argument**; the independent checkers **share Mathlib's definitions**, so their agreement does not test those definitions; and at least one commenter argued the end-to-end comparator run was **not finished** at the time of the statement, so the result does not yet "strictly meet the community standard." The **Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the Riemann hypothesis as unsolved** — its $1M prize requires broad acceptance plus two years of scrutiny.

### The vacuity objection

**Jenny Lorraine Nielsen**, *"The Invalidity of Alpöge's Proposed Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis Vacuously"* ([PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/NIEIOT-2)) argues that while the top-level theorem typechecks against Mathlib's definition, the **key geometric witnesses are defined in ways that hold trivially**, breaking the chain to the conclusion — so what was proven reduces to a tautology. **The Lean community disputes this**, and the preprint is **not peer-reviewed**. Worth knowing why the objection is not frivolous: a **2025 paper on formalizing zeta and L-functions in Lean** ([arXiv:2503.00959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00959)) documents a near-miss of exactly this shape, where a junk value at *s* = 1 could have made a formalized conjecture trivially false. Treat this as **live and unresolved**.

### Claude Fable 5.2 on black-hole physics — now the week's most-read Anthropic research

Anthropic's **November 17** post ([anthropic.com/research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics)) climbed to **575 points / 274 comments on Hacker News inside this window**, so it belongs here even though the post predates it. Two results, both now **public preprints listing Claude as an author**: a **Hawking–Page transition** calculation left open since **2003** (completed at *weak* coupling; the intermediate-coupling case remains open), and a **modular-bootstrap** bound setting an exact ceiling of **c/Λ** with **Λ ≈ 9.1266** on the lightest black hole in 3D quantum gravity (limited to the simplest non-spinning case, and it does not reach the BTZ threshold). Researcher **Hongbin Chen** reportedly started the project in August by asking Claude to list open problems in the field. The scoping matters: these are real but bounded results, not the field-wide claims the headlines imply.

### Reserved slot: Apple's FastFrames — an open-weights video model fast enough to walk around in *(a lab not covered elsewhere in this digest)*

The most interesting launch this digest has not yet covered is **Apple's FastFrames**, now sitting at **#11 on Hugging Face's trending list** with weights live under the `apple` org. Two sizes — **FastFrames-L (14B)** and **FastFrames-S (1.3B)** — produced by distilling **Wan2.1-T2V-14B** into a **two-step causal (autoregressive) generator** at **832×480**. Reported latency is roughly **36 ms per frame** for the small model and **45 ms on an 8×B200 node** for the 14B, against **~825 ms** for the same model without the optimizations; most of the win came from replacing heavy VAE decoders and fixing KV-cache updates — "the parts everyone leaves alone." Paper: [arXiv:2611.23226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23226).

**Why it earns the slot:** this is the world-model thesis cashed out as latency. At ~50 ms a frame you can hold a key down and walk through the generated world; at 825 ms your keypresses queue. That is the difference between a video generator and an interactive environment, and it comes from engineering nobody was optimizing. **Three caveats:** the release is dated **November 16–18** (sources conflict, and no official Apple announcement page surfaced), so it is days old rather than hours; it requires **Linux + NVIDIA CUDA 12.8** and **does not run on Apple Silicon** — community MLX ports are unofficial; and the **license is reported inconsistently** (Apple AMLR research license vs. Apple Sample Code License), so check the model card before commercial use.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Jakub Pachocki (OpenAI): make perplexity the industry's speed limit

OpenAI's chief scientist published **"Perplexity and Intelligence"** on **November 22** ([openai.com](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/); **504 points / 348 comments on HN**), and it is the most consequential governance proposal of the window. The argument: **log-perplexity** — cross-entropy loss on a held-out set, which OpenAI says it has tracked since **GPT-3.5** — should become a **shared yardstick across labs**, and labs should commit **not to cross a threshold** until monitoring capability reaches a stated milestone, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitoring. Pachocki flags **~0.68–0.70** as the band where he expects transformative capability to emerge, and notes that at around **0.76** models already began producing groundbreaking results on open math problems. **Why it matters:** it is the first concrete proposal to gate frontier scaling on a *number* rather than on a capability evaluation — and the first time a major lab has published its own position on that number. **Why to be skeptical:** there is **no enforcement mechanism, no first limit, no verification procedure and no shared dataset**; the scores are on **OpenAI's own internal codebase**, so no other lab's figure would mean the same thing. A speed limit nobody else can read off their own speedometer is a press release, not a regime — but it is the kind of press release that moves a field.

### Demis Hassabis: "in the foothills of the singularity"

On a Stanford podcast, reported **November 21** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/21/ai-singularity-stripe-elon-musk-sam-altman-ai-agents-superintelligence-future-of-business)), the Google DeepMind chair said humanity is now **"in the foothills of the singularity."** **Why it matters:** Hassabis is the most consistently hedged of the frontier-lab principals, and his earlier framing this month was a warning — that removing the human reviewer from recursive self-improvement is "a very dangerous way" to get there ([Inc./Masters of Scale](https://www.inc.com/masters-of-scale/deepminds-demis-hassabis-warns-an-ai-oil-spill-is-coming-unless-the-industry-changes-course/91433965)). ### The mathematicians, split three ways on what the Lean proof is worth

The reaction to Alpöge's library has sorted into three distinct positions, and all three are defensible:

- **Historic, if it holds.** Clay Institute president **Martin Bridson** called it of **"historic significance"**; **James Maynard** (Fields Medal, Oxford) told *Nature* it could rank among the field's top results once understood. **Steven Strogatz** (Cornell) described the pairing as **"a very skilled AI jockey riding a very fast AI horse"** — crediting the human direction, not just the model.
- **Correct but worthless as mathematics.** Maynard also said, bluntly, that the news **"does very little for me because I never doubted the Riemann hypothesis was true"** ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-may-have-solved-math-s-greatest-mystery-can-humans-understand-answer)). The value of RH was never the truth value; it was the understanding the proof would bring.
- **Released irresponsibly.** Stanford's **Brian Conrad** called the release **"frankly completely outrageous"** on the grounds that **Alpöge does not fully understand his own proof**, and **Andrew Granville** argued he should have prepared a comprehensible write-up first. The force of this: the AI-generated expanded explanation reportedly runs to **over 250,000 pages** and has not been released, so there may be no write-up to prepare.

**Why it matters:** this is the first time the question "does a theorem count as proven if no human can read the proof?" has had actual stakes attached, and the discipline is answering it in public, in real time, without a procedure.

## Other Notable AI News

### OpenRouter cuts three inference providers after months of prompt theft

On **November 22**, **OpenRouter removed three inference providers** from its platform and **stripped 125 endpoints of Zero Data Retention eligibility**, notifying potentially affected customers. The trigger was **Dragos research led by HD Moore**, published November 19: roughly **two million prompts** seeded with unique canary credentials and URLs between **July and November 2026**, each secret used in exactly one request so any later use could be traced. Findings: **at least three providers compromised**, earliest around **mid-July**, **none aware until notified**; one adversary had compromised **two providers at once**, using the stolen keys together from a single rented server. Worse for the trust model, **Zero Data Retention flags were ignored in two of the breaches**, and an unnamed Chinese AI lab fetched URLs from about **two-thirds** of the ZDR-flagged prompts sent to its models. Marketplace resellers needed no exploit at all — list a model cheaply, enable request logging, read everything.

**OpenRouter says its own systems were not breached.** Neither OpenRouter nor Dragos has named the providers; **Novita, Phala and Together AI** have each posted their own incident notices, and **nobody has confirmed those are the same three**. Separately, **Together AI disclosed a distinct incident** in which **its own AI coding agent pushed a credential to a public GitHub repository**, letting an attacker download prompt samples from June and July ([Together AI](https://www.together.ai/blog/security-event-prompt-leakage-for-requests-in-june-and-july-of-2026)). **Why it matters:** if you route through an aggregator, "Zero Data Retention" was a setting, not a guarantee — and the practical advice in every write-up is the same: **rotate any key that has ever been in a prompt.** *(One aggregator names three specific providers as the removed ones; that attribution is unverified and not repeated here.)*

### US governors propose a federal/state split on AI regulation

On **November 22** the **National Governors Association**, through its bipartisan **Governors Council to Secure American AI Dominance**, published AI principles aimed at the incoming Congress ([NGA](https://www.nga.org/news/press-releases/governors-call-for-ai-action/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-22/governors-call-for-federal-ai-rules-without-sidelining-states)). The split: **Washington** takes risks that cross borders during development — including **independent safety reviews of advanced models** and **mandatory reporting of serious security breaches and safety incidents** — while **states keep authority** over consumer protection, anti-discrimination and the labour-market effects of AI. Both levels are asked to avoid rules that favour incumbents or raise barriers for smaller entrants. **Why it matters:** it is a direct bipartisan counter-offer to the March White House framework, which asked Congress to **preempt** state AI laws imposing "undue burdens." Governors are conceding the frontier-safety layer to the federal government in exchange for keeping the use layer — a trade neither the preemption camp nor the state-autonomy camp has previously offered. **No congressional sponsor is named**, and this is a proposed framework, not legislation. *(Sources disagree on which governor chairs the council — Wes Moore or Spencer Cox; unresolved here.)*

It runs a **local proxy on port 8045** exposing **OpenAI-, Anthropic- and Gemini-compatible endpoints**, translating between them, **rotating accounts and retrying on 429 or 401**, plus a model router, Imagen 3 image generation, and a native CLI switcher (`at`). Integration guides cover Claude Code CLI, OpenCode, Kilo Code and the OpenAI Python SDK; ships as .deb/.rpm/.AppImage, .dmg/Homebrew, Windows NSIS/MSI/portable, a Docker image (256MB minimum) and **Android Termux ARM64 headless binaries**. **Why it's here:** 33.7k stars is a top-tier total this digest has never covered, and it is the clearest artifact yet of a category nobody announces — **tooling that converts consumer AI subscriptions into API capacity**. The README says so outright: it works by turning **Google and Anthropic web sessions into API interfaces**. **Caveats, and they are substantial:** that mechanism is **squarely against the terms of service of the providers involved**, so treat this as something to understand rather than deploy; the licence is **non-commercial**; **1.7k open issues against 4 open PRs** is a maintenance signal, not a health one; the README carries **sponsor links and unsubstantiated promotional claims** (e.g. a "95%+ KV-Cache hit rate" for a third party); and there are per-platform rough edges — macOS "damaged" warnings needing an `xattr` fix, black windows on some Linux compositors, Kilo Code 404s in OpenAI mode.

**3. [runkids/skillshare](https://github.com/runkids/skillshare)** — *one source of truth for skills, agents, rules, MCP connections, hooks and plugins, synced across every AI CLI.* **MIT**, **3.3k stars, +141 this week**, **387 forks**, **3,293 commits**, **23 open issues**, **15 open PRs**, **v0.30.11**, a single dependency-free Go binary. You keep one directory (`~/.config/skillshare/`, meant to live in Git) and sync out to **Claude Code, Codex, Pi and OpenCode**, with **per-tool filtering** of what each target receives, per-project skills committed alongside code, native hook management *without executing the hooks*, a `skillshare ui` web dashboard, and installation from GitHub, GitLab or any Git host. **Why it's here, and why it matters more than its star count:** it ships a **security audit of skills, auto-scanning on install and update** — which makes it the first project on this board to treat an agent skill as a supply-chain artifact by default rather than as a config file. Two days ago this digest flagged agent-skill supply-chain risk as a real category with no vetted tooling; **3,293 commits and a v0.30.x release line is the most credible answer yet.** **Caveats:** Windows file symlinks require Developer Mode or fall back to copying; a tool that chokes on symlinks has to be switched to copy mode; and the README does not document Git as a prerequisite even though installing from repositories needs it.

**4. [croffasia/itsaplan](https://github.com/croffasia/itsaplan)** — *a self-hosted issue tracker where AI agents are project members.* **AGPL-3.0** (with `packages/runner` under **Apache-2.0**), **2.5k stars, +24 today**, **338 forks**, **684 commits**, **51 open issues**, **26 open PRs**. Kanban, table, timeline and calendar views, cycles, docs and notes boards — positioned against Linear and Plane — but the distinguishing move is that **agents can be assigned issues and triggered by @mention, assignment or schedule**. Internal agents run on your server with your model key; external agents run on your own machine through `@itsaplan/runner` **under your existing coding-CLI account** (Claude Code, Codex, Copilot CLI), which neatly sidesteps paying twice for inference. Stack: Bun, Turborepo, Elysia, Next.js, Drizzle/Postgres, S3-compatible storage; deploys via Docker, Coolify, Kubernetes/Helm or Railway. **Why it's here:** every other agent-coordination project on this board wraps the *agent*; this one wraps the **work item**, which is where human teams actually hand things off. **Caveats:** it is **open-core, not open source end to end** — audit log, unlimited workspaces, SAML/OIDC and SCIM, agent workflows and AI budgets are **Pro at $240/yr or $24/mo**, and without a licence the instance runs as Community; the "up to 91% cheaper" claim is the project's own marketing; the page shows **16 security and quality alerts**; some integrations depend on external providers behind a **referral link**; and the localhost setup is explicitly for trying it, not production.

**Also worth a look:** **[tianma-if/edgeever](https://github.com/tianma-if/edgeever)** (**AGPL-3.0**, **3.5k stars, +26 today**, 1.8k forks, 3,425 commits, 56 open issues) — an Evernote/Obsidian alternative running on Cloudflare Workers/D1/R2 or self-hosted Docker, whose relevance here is **Remote MCP support so agents can read and write your notes within granted permissions**: the note app as agent-addressable memory. Its **"100% free forever"** claim rests entirely on Cloudflare's free quotas (R2 requires a payment method regardless), its public demo **resets daily**, and its comparison tables are self-authored.
