### The Exponential Time Hypothesis, disputed twice, verified zero times *(dated Oct 16–28; not previously carried here)*
**OpenAI** says an unreleased internal model produced a **subexponential-time algorithm for counting satisfying assignments of 3-SAT**, which it argues refutes the **Exponential Time Hypothesis** — a 1999 conjecture underpinning a large body of fine-grained complexity results. The natural-language proof is dated **October 16** and was subsequently formalized in **Lean**; **Anthropic** posted a competing 3-SAT paper dated **October 26** ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-cracks-27-year-old-pillar-computer-science-openai-and-anthropic-claim), Oct 28).

**Why it matters, and why to hold it loosely.** This is the clearest test yet of whether a lab can land a result the field will accept on the lab's own terms. The evidence is unusually good — **the Lean files are public** — and the caveats are unusually sharp: **no one outside either company has confirmed either proof**; the three computer scientists OpenAI named as checkers said their involvement **"should not be taken as certification of the claim"**; and the **machine-verified time bound is reportedly weaker than the papers' headline claim**, so Lean is checking something narrower than what is being announced. Anthropic's own paper concedes its approach is **unlikely to beat brute force on any instance under ~10 million variables**, i.e. no practical consequence, and no deployed cryptography is affected. **P vs NP remains open.** Anthropic mathematician **Levent Alpöge** had earlier argued OpenAI's solutions leaned on pre-existing work. Treat as a live claim under review, not a result.

## Model & Product Launches

### DeepSeek open-sources a proactive coding agent — and calls it a prototype
**`deepseek-ai/dsh-superagent`**, public **October 31**, **MIT**, **9.2k stars / 935 forks** as of this morning ([GitHub](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/dsh-superagent), [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/deepseek-super-agent-open-source-coding-agent)). A bundle for **DeepSeek Harness** that watches **one GitHub repository** — and optionally **Feishu**, ByteDance's workplace chat — opens a work item when an authorized person asks, completes it **in its own agent session and working directory**, and replies in the originating conversation, typically with a pull request. A web panel surfaces only items needing human action. Per Bloomberg's reporting, it can also **watch developer group chats for coding problems and offer solutions unprompted**.

**Why it is the launch of the window.** Every shipping coding agent today is *reactive*: it waits in a terminal or a PR. This is the first open release from a frontier lab that makes **proactivity the design center**, and the README's stated philosophy is the interesting part — *minimize demands on human attention, maximize value*, acting only when asked or when a team rule permits, notifying only when action is needed. DeepSeek states the risk itself: an agent acting for a whole team can also **waste a whole team's attention**.

**What the README constrains, plainly.** It is a **research prototype for reference only** and **not an official DeepSeek product**; it may contain bugs with inconsistent engineering practice; it **should be reviewed and adapted before any real use**; the **published code differs from DeepSeek's internal version** *and* from the implementation described in the accompanying article, with some of that article's designs being **future directions, not implemented**. Setup is involved enough that the README suggests **pointing another coding agent at `SETUP.md`**. Scope is **one repo at a time**. The authorization, review and trust questions — who counts as authorized, what a "team rule" may permit, what happens when it is wrong in a group chat — are unresolved by the release.

### OpenAI will ask Washington for mandatory speed limits — and ask everyone to stop saying "data center"
**Axios reported Nov 1** that OpenAI plans a post-midterms policy and PR push with two prongs: state legislation giving communities a say in how AI infrastructure gets built **plus mandatory "speed limits" on the most powerful AI systems**, and a vocabulary change — its facilities should be called **"AI compute campuses"** that "economically transform host communities" rather than data centers ([implicator.ai summary](https://www.implicator.ai/openai-plans-post-midterms-policy-pr-blitz-pushing-mandatory/)). An OpenAI spokesperson told Business Insider: *"Compute campuses better describes the scale and role of the infrastructure we're building for AI."* The stated motive is the **bipartisan, nationwide backlash against data centers**; the cited exemplar is **PORTS-Pike in Ohio**, pitched as a technology campus.

**Why it matters.** A frontier lab asking for *mandatory* constraints on itself is the notable half, and it is consistent with Altman's September line that the leading labs "may be close to announcing a pact to slow AI development." But the two prongs are in tension in a way worth naming: the regulatory ask is about model capability, the renaming is about physical siting, and only one of those is what towns are actually fighting about. **Calibrate:** Axios is the originating source and TechCrunch's version is more tentative; the spokesperson's wording ("compute campuses") differs from the headline phrasing ("AI compute campuses"); and one outlet's claim of a **Nov 3** rename date is not corroborated elsewhere. This is a **proposed** vocabulary change, not a completed corporate renaming.

### Karpathy: the recommender "may work for the median person, but it definitely does not work for me"
Following up on **HackerTube**, carried yesterday: his own post is the clearest statement of the argument. He describes YouTube's homepage as an **"enshittified wall of slop"** and the algorithm as something that *"may work for the median person, but it definitely does not work for me"* ([X, Nov 1](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2116957135661945283)). His own framing of the build: *"Weekend hack: HackerTube. I was annoyed with YouTube's recommendations so I had Claude filter down 43M comments over 20 years of HN to 6,454 videos that keep being recommended. Significantly better viewing experience, ~2 hours with Opus 5.5."*

**Why it matters.** The claim is not that the recommender is badly built — it is that **population-optimal ranking is individually wrong by construction**, and that an LLM pass over a corpus of human recommendations is now a cheap enough substitute that one person can do it in an evening. The site has **no login and keeps viewing state in the browser**, and **the dataset is public** so others can rebuild it with their own filter. **One correction to the secondary coverage:** at least one outlet misidentifies Karpathy as an OpenAI co-founder, and the "~2 hours" figure describes this project only.

Covered in full under Model & Product Launches above. **Why it's trending:** a frontier lab shipping a *proactive* agent — one that can act on a rule rather than a prompt — is a genuine first in open source, and the star curve (reported ~4k within a day of release, 9.2k by this morning) is the steepest in the window. **What the README constrains:** research prototype, **not an official DeepSeek product**; **public code differs from the internal version** and from the write-up that describes it; may contain bugs with inconsistent engineering practice; should be reviewed and adapted before real use; one repo at a time; setup involved enough that the suggested path is to point another coding agent at `SETUP.md`. **[anomalyco/hex](https://github.com/anomalyco/hex)** — *"Local voice dictation. Hold a key, speak, and it pastes."* MIT, **~3.5k stars**, 201 forks, **+1,179 today** — the highest stars-per-day-to-total ratio on the board, roughly a third of its lifetime stars in one day. Push-to-talk dictation for Mac, Windows and Linux that transcribes **on device** and pastes into whatever app has focus, with Stable and Dev release channels, a "Labs" section for experimental features, and an unusually wide install matrix (DMG, Windows installers, Linux binaries, .deb, .rpm, Homebrew, AUR, install script, Nix). **Why it's trending:** dictation is the one place local inference clearly beats the cloud — latency is the product, and nothing leaves the machine — and this is the category's velocity story this week while **cjpais/Handy**, the established alternative at 36.7k stars, gained 339. **What the README states:** the **first run downloads a speech model**, so it needs network once; the macOS build is **Apple silicon only**, with no Intel build listed; Labs features may be unstable; and the project **does not accept pull requests** — contributors are asked to open an issue instead. The README makes **no claims about accuracy, language coverage or hardware requirements**, which is the gap to check before adopting it.

**[beenuar/AiSOC](https://github.com/beenuar/AiSOC)** — *"Open-source, self-hostable AI security operations center."* MIT, **~6.4k stars**, 653 forks, **+446 today**, badged v21.0.0. Ingests security telemetry, runs detection rules, groups alerts into incidents, and has agents investigate them — calling typed tools and logging prompts, tool calls, citations and cost to an **"Investigation Ledger"** that exports as signed evidence bundles. **2,538 executable rules** of a 7,023-rule library plus 76 sliding-window rules; ~**90 click-to-configure connectors** plus CloudTrail, GCP Pub/Sub, Azure Event Hubs and syslog/CEF collectors; a migration tool for Splunk SPL, Sentinel KQL and Elastic EQL that **refuses to translate rules it cannot carry faithfully**; CISA KEV ingest with no API key; SAML/OIDC, SCIM and TOTP MFA; an MCP server for Claude, Cursor and Continue; Docker Compose profiles with a bundled **~2GB llama3.2:3b running on CPU**, so the whole stack needs **no API keys**. **Why it's trending:** it is the rare agent product whose auditability is the feature — the ledger and signed bundles exist because SOC work gets subpoenaed. **The README's own caveats are the most honest in this category:** it is **not a drop-in SIEM replacement**, with no long-term retention or compliance search; it cannot see telemetry you have not connected; **response actions require a human approver and are not autonomous by default**, with playbooks running in preview; the bundled model is small, giving usable triage on **44 of 50** alerts before its output was constrained to JSON; **no hosted LLM provider has been tested** because the project has no funded key for one; the benchmark numbers are **"substrate self-consistency measures, not live agent accuracy"**; demo incidents are not real; **two executable rules still cannot fire**; npm distribution is **Beta** and the three packages still need a first-upload token; and development is **funded by Cyble**, which the README says gates no features.

**[FlashML-org/FreeToken](https://github.com/FlashML-org/FreeToken)** — *"Edge-native MoE serving engine: datacenter-scale model serving on the desktop."* Apache-2.0, **~15.5k stars**, 1.6k forks, **+242 today**, 284 open issues. Runs large open-weight MoE models on consumer hardware via bandwidth-adaptive **CPU–GPU co-execution**, double-buffered prefill streaming, a **global LRU expert cache**, a custom **FTW** weight format, and **runtime VRAM reallocation between expert cache and KV memory without restarting**. Its most distinctive feature is **semantic anchor checkpoints** for recurrent state and KV caches, meant to avoid recomputing context after agentic edits such as tool calls. Ships Anthropic- and OpenAI-compatible APIs so Claude Code and Codex point at it directly; examples cover **DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and GLM-5.2** in MXFP4, NVFP4, FP8 and BF16. **Why it's trending:** it is the local-inference answer to this week's open-weights wave — Mistral Large 4 out, Kimi K3.1 Pro promised Nov 4 — and the anchor-checkpoint idea targets the specific cost that makes local agent loops unusable. **What to check yourself:** the README has **no limitations section and states no caveats**, while claiming "blistering interactive speeds" on "290B+ frontier MoE models" **with no benchmark detail on the page**; hardware support is described only for **NVIDIA RTX 30/40/50**; the desktop app is **Windows and Linux only**; and the docs include pages on *"Repairing old FTW checkpoints"* and *"Diagnosing a checkpoint that fails to load,"* which suggests the custom format's compatibility is a real operational cost — an inference from the doc index, not a stated limitation.
