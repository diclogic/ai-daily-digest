## Notable Papers

**MOSS: Self-Evolution through Source-Level Rewriting in Autonomous Agent Systems**
*Qianshu Cai, Yonggang Zhang, Xianzhang Jia et al.* — Proposes a system where autonomous agents rewrite their own source code (not just prompts or skill files) to self-improve after deployment. A single unsupervised evolution cycle lifted mean grader scores from 0.25 to 0.61 across four tasks.
[arXiv:2605.22794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22794)

**AutoResearchClaw: Multi-Agent Autonomous Research Pipeline**
*UNC-Chapel Hill et al.* — A multi-agent system designed to mimic iterative, self-correcting human scientific inquiry — automating literature review, hypothesis generation, experimentation, and revision in a closed loop.
[devflokers.com](https://www.devflokers.com/blog/ai-news-may-2026-models-papers-open-source)

**AI Can Learn Scientific Taste (RLCF)**
*arXiv* — Introduces Reinforcement Learning from Community Feedback, training a "Scientific Judge" on 700K matched pairs of high- vs. low-citation papers to train a policy model that proposes research ideas with higher potential impact.
[arXiv (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14473)

**Fast Byte Latent Transformer**
*Meta & Stanford* — Proposes BLT variants that cut byte-level inference memory bandwidth by over 50% (up to 77% with BLT-S at 3B parameters) without tokenization and with no loss in task performance.
[MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/05/11/meta-and-stanford-researchers-propose-fast-byte-latent-transformer-that-reduces-inference-memory-bandwidth-by-over-50-without-tokenization/)
