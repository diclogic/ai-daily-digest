## Notable Papers & Research

### Agent-Centric Interactive World Proxies (Shanghai AI Lab)

A paper from Shanghai AI Laboratory (August 3) introduces "agent-centric interactive world proxies," shifting world modeling from physical state transitions to agent-usable information transitions — execution outcomes, retrieved experiences, and verification signals. The approach broadens world modeling to provide versatile feedback for continually improving agents, a meaningful step toward agents that learn from interaction rather than just observation.

([arXiv — cs.AI current listings](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/current))

### Graph Engineering for Multi-Agent Systems Gains Traction

The "graph engineering" paradigm — wiring specialized agents into graphs of nodes, edges, and shared state — continued to gain momentum this week, with the term trending on X and multiple implementations surfacing on GitHub. Anthropic engineers are leading adoption in production, composing five patterns (prompt chaining, routing, parallelization, orchestrator-workers, evaluator-optimizer) into agent graphs. Andrej Karpathy's earlier 12-page guide on the topic remains widely cited, though its exact provenance was contested.

([AI Builder Club](https://www.aibuilderclub.com/blog/graph-engineering-with-claude-code) · [Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/graph-engineering-ai-agents/))

## Model & Product Launches

### Google AI Leadership Overhaul: Hassabis Steps Up, Jeff Dean Exits

In the most consequential AI leadership shakeup of the year, Google announced two seismic changes on August 5:

**Demis Hassabis** steps down as CEO of Google DeepMind, moving to chair of DeepMind and the new role of chief scientist at Alphabet. He will continue leading Isomorphic Labs (AI drug discovery). Semafor reported Hassabis had been drifting away from day-to-day responsibilities for over a year, increasingly shifting operational duties to **Koray Kavukcuoglu**, who now takes over as SVP of Google DeepMind. Hassabis said his new role allows him to "focus on the big picture and help influence what is to come."

**Jeff Dean**, Google's chief scientist for 27 years, is leaving to co-found **Discovery Loop**, a public benefit corporation aiming to automate the scientific method. His co-founders: **Sanjay Ghemawat** (Google Senior Fellow), **Oriol Vinyals** (VP of Research at DeepMind, Gemini co-technical lead), and **Quoc Le** (co-founder of Google Brain). The startup will build AI systems that propose experiments, run them, evaluate results, and iterate — at a scale humans can't match alone. Seed funding from Radical Ventures and Khosla Ventures, with Alphabet as a founding investor providing compute for at least the first year. They're starting by automating ML research, then targeting medicine, materials, and clean energy.

### Yann LeCun & Oriol Vinyals Launch 224 Ventures ($100M+ AI Fund)

Yann LeCun (who left Meta in November 2025) and Oriol Vinyals (who departed DeepMind as part of the August 5 reshuffle) are launching **224 Ventures**, a VC firm investing exclusively in early-stage AI-native companies. ### Anthropic Signs $10B Compute Deal with Startup Volta

Anthropic locked in a six-year, $10B cloud-compute deal with Volta, a newly founded AI infrastructure startup backed by Andreessen Horowitz, Altimeter, and NVIDIA. Volta will partner with Bitdeer to build a 133MW facility in Norway running NVIDIA's Vera Rubin chips. Volta emerged from stealth with ~$300M raised at a $2.4B valuation. ### AI Swallows 86% of US Venture Funding in H1 2026

PitchBook data shows US venture capital hit $412.7B in H1 2026, with AI companies capturing $355.9B — 86% of every venture dollar. OpenAI and Anthropic alone accounted for $217B (43% of all startup funding). Global startup investment hit a record $510B in H1 2026, per Crunchbase. The concentration raises questions about whether non-AI startups can compete for capital.

([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/09/pitchbook-us-venture-funding-hits-412-7b-first-half-ai-deals-dominate/) · [Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/))

### SK hynix & SanDisk Publish First OCP Spec for High Bandwidth Flash

At Flash Memory Summit 2026, SK hynix and SanDisk published the first OCP technical specification for High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) — a new NAND-based memory tier between HBM and SSDs, supporting up to 512GB capacity and 0.4–3.0 TB/s bandwidth. The spec targets AI inference workloads where HBM is too expensive and SSDs too slow.
