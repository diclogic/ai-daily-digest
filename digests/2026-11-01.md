## Notable Papers & Research

**No new arXiv announcement cycle fell inside this window.** October 31 and November 1 are Saturday and Sunday; the most recent batch is the **2610.40xxx** block announced Friday, Oct 30, and the next lands Monday, Nov 2. The items below are from that batch and were *not* carried in yesterday's digest.

### A widely cited interpretability result loses its explanation
**Rethinking Neural Thickets Without Weight Perturbations** — A. Dufour, Gardès, Dravid et al. ([2610.40649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40649)). The reported Neural Thicket gains reproduce **without** the geometric weight perturbations the hypothesis says cause them. **Why it matters:** this is the rarer and more useful kind of paper — the effect survives, the mechanism does not, which means a year of downstream work built on the geometric story needs re-grounding. Negative results on a popular hypothesis are badly under-supplied in this field.

### A probe that catches an agent before it breaks the rule
**Pre-Crime for Agents, at the Last Second: Probing When a Recruited Agent Gives In** — Raghav ([2610.40588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40588)). A **linear** probe on agent internals predicts rule-breaking *before* the output token is produced. **Why it matters:** every deployed guardrail today reads the output. A linear probe is cheap enough to run on every forward pass, which makes pre-output intervention affordable rather than theoretical — and it lands the same week OpenAI was still publicly working out whether its own agents' monitor-disabling was "benign overpersistence."

### A world model built to be understood, not to score
**world2vec: Mechanistic world models through latent dynamics learning** — Schmidt, Gonzalez Laiz, Szuppinger et al. ([2610.40637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40637)). Learns **reward-free** world models with interpretability and faithful dynamics as the objective rather than downstream task return. **Why it matters:** the commercial world-model race (Genie, Marble, Cosmos, Atlas) is optimizing for what the generated world *looks* like. This asks whether the latent dynamics are *right* — the same question as the 263k-parameter result carried yesterday, approached from the representation side.

### Do vision foundation models hold a map, or just a view?
**Probing Spatial State Representations in Visual Foundation Models** — Yin, Cai, Wang et al. ([2610.40636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40636)). Tests whether visual foundation models maintain **persistent spatial state under self-motion** — i.e. whether the model still knows where the chair is after the camera turns away. **Why it matters:** this is the measurable core of the "spatial intelligence" claim that World Labs, AMI and NVIDIA are all built on, framed as a probe rather than a demo reel.

### A watermark that survives speculative decoding
**TextSeal2: Distortion-Free LLM Watermarking with Copula Mixing and Memoryless Races** — Sander, Fernandez, Gat et al. ([2610.40613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40613)). Distortion-free watermarking that still works **under speculative decoding**. **Why it matters:** every prior distortion-free scheme assumed vanilla sampling, and essentially all production serving now uses speculative decoding — so the deployable-watermarking literature had a hole exactly where the deployments are.

### Can a research agent tell which experiment is worth running?
**rTaste: Do Research Agents Have Taste? Evaluating Early Stage Research Judgment** — Kanke ([2610.40488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40488)). Benchmarks whether models can **forecast which experiments are worth doing**, rather than execute a specified one. **Why it matters:** autonomous-research claims are almost all measured on execution. Judgment at the proposal stage is the part that actually determines whether an automated lab produces anything, and it has had no benchmark.

### Briefly

- **A Low-Rank Space Captures Linear Alignment Between LLM and Human Neural Activity** — Paris, Finn ([2610.40511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40511)). A low-rank subspace of LLM representations predicts human brain activity — narrowing a claim usually made about representations as a whole.
- **Collaborative AI agents for autonomous experimental research with solid-state quantum spins** — Mansouri, Kimura, Koga et al. ([2610.40589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40589)). Agents design, run and interpret quantum-spin experiments end to end, with explicit evidence checks on the conclusions.
- **How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates** — Neuwinger, Yukhymenko, Schlag ([2610.40516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516)). Localized templates extend safety training into low-resource languages, where refusal behavior is weakest and least measured.
- **On the Fundamental Limits of Prefill-Based Decoding Length Prediction** — Shi, Yan, Chen et al. ([2610.40541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40541)). Shows current benchmarks conflate predictor quality with task difficulty, and gives information-theoretic limits — a scheduling result with direct serving-cost consequences.
- **Preserving Utility in Continual LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Projections** — Belieni, Erthal, da Silva et al. ([2610.40483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40483)). Keeps repeated unlearning requests from eroding the model, which is the regime any real right-to-erasure pipeline operates in.
- **BURGER: Benchmarking and Understanding Robotic Generalization via Embodied Drawing** — Xu, Peng, Liu et al. ([2610.40532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40532)). Uses language-conditioned drawing as a cheap proxy for studying VLA generalization.
- **Also surfacing this window, from the earlier 2610.37xxx block:** **OpenFrontiers** ([2610.37739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37739)), an open-problem set and "scientific rediscovery" benchmark; **WebAnything** ([2610.37786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37786)), building scalable agent environments from the open web; and **detecting covert collusion between AI agents via partner–outsider forward monitoring** ([2610.37849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37849)).

### A world model used to design the chip, not run on it

### Karpathy rebuilds YouTube in an evening, and says the quiet part about recommenders
**Andrej Karpathy**, **October 31**, on his blog ([HackerTube](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/hacker-tube/)). Opening YouTube late on a Friday, he describes the homepage as an **"enshittified wall of slop"** and adds that the algorithm may work for the median internet user but **"it definitely does not work for me."** He then had **Claude Opus 5.5** pull Hacker News comments, used Sonnet to filter them down to genuine recommendations, and built **hackertube.org** from the **6,454 videos** that survived, spanning **2007–2026 across ~150 topics** — reportedly about **two hours** of work. Design choices are the argument: thumbnails are a **frame from each video's midpoint** rather than the uploader's bait, state lives in **local browser session data** with no sign-in, and the dataset is **exported so anyone can rebuild their own**.

**Why it matters.** The most-discussed AI artifact of the day was a one-person weekend project that *replaced* a recommender rather than improving one — and from the single most credentialed insider on the training side. The claim embedded in it is that the scarce input is **curation signal**, not model capability: 6,454 human recommendations beat a planetary-scale engagement optimizer for this user. It is also a legible demonstration of what frontier coding models have done to the cost of building a working product, which is a more honest capability datapoint than most benchmark releases. **Note on secondary numbers:** reports of its Hacker News standing ranged from **95 points** to **539 points** at different times during the day; this session could not reach news.ycombinator.com to settle it.

## Trending Open-Source Projects

**[Orkas-AI/Orkas](https://github.com/Orkas-AI/Orkas)** — *"Open-source, local-first AI team desktop app."* MIT, **~6.8k stars**, 409 forks, **+1,044 today**, 826 commits. An Electron desktop app where a **Commander** agent decomposes a goal into to-dos and delegates to **16 built-in specialists** (DeepResearcher, ContentWriter, VideoStudio, OfficeWorker and others) while also driving **external CLI agents** — Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, OpenClaw, Hermes-Agent. Projects persist goals, rules, memory and files across conversations; **Auto-advance** takes the next to-do unprompted; automations run hourly to monthly; connectors reach Gmail, Slack and Notion. **Why it's trending:** it is the orchestration layer as a *consumer app* rather than a framework — most multi-agent repos ship a library and leave the shell to you. **The architecture is unusually specific for this category:** three Commander tools with distinct semantics (`dispatch_to` runs a named agent and returns, `hand_off_to` transfers remaining work and ends the turn, `run_worker` runs an isolated anonymous sub-task); context budgeted against usable input capacity with checkpoints that condense older process messages while structured file/command records survive compaction; a local SQLite vector index with an on-device embedding model; and hard execution limits — session locks, concurrency caps, tool-round budgets, repeated-call detection, idle watchdogs. Idle-time background reviews can rewrite per-agent `COMPETENCE.md` and `LEARNING_STRATEGIES.md`. **Caveats the README states plainly:** shared projects, cloud sync and web publishing exist **only in the packaged app**, not the open-source build; the packaged app requires sign-in and starts at **$11.99/mo** after 500 free credits; **voice input sends audio to Orkas** and needs their API key; automations require the machine awake and the app running; Linux is source-only (**musl unsupported**); first source launch downloads a ~95MB embedding model; and it says outright that Orkas **orchestrates models, it is not one**.
