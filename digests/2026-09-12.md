## Model & Product Launches

### Sakana AI — Fugu Max v1.0 and Fugu Ultra v2.0 *(reserved slot: lab not covered elsewhere)*

The most significant launch in the window, and from a lab this digest does not otherwise track. Released **September 10–11**, the two models replace Sakana's previous Fugu line — and the interesting thing about them is what they are *not*. Neither is a single trained model. Both are **learned orchestration systems**: a language model trained to route a task across a fixed pool of open-weight and specialized models, and to recursively call instances of itself. Fugu Ultra v2 notably **does not rely on any proprietary frontier model in its pool**.

**Fugu Max** is the cost tier: **$2 / 1M input, $6 / 1M output** — 40–60% below the output price of comparable frontier models. Sakana reports it takes best overall score on six benchmarks (Terminal Bench 2.1, GPQAD, AA-LCR, GDP.pdf, AutomationBench, SWEFish) and expands the cost-performance Pareto frontier on seven of ten. It integrates an unusually wide model pool, including NVIDIA's Nemotron family through a collaboration with NVIDIA.

**Fugu Ultra v2** is the quality tier: **$5 / $30 per 1M** (with a $10 / $45 premium tier above 272K context), aimed at multi-step reasoning, autonomous research and full-stack software engineering. Sakana reports best-or-joint-best on five of eight benchmarks, including **48.3 on Chartography against Claude Opus 5's 27.3**, and 74.3 on DeepSWE. Both support configurable reasoning effort, function calling, structured outputs, image and PDF input, and built-in search, and both ship API-compatible on OpenRouter and other gateways.

Why it matters: read against OpenAI's Agents API two days earlier, this is the second move in a week to make orchestration a product someone else buys rather than a thing every agent startup rebuilds. Sakana's version is the more aggressive one — OpenAI sells you *its* harness around *its* models; Sakana sells a router that treats frontier models as interchangeable and, in Ultra v2's case, declines to use them at all. Benchmark figures here are the vendor's own and have not been independently reproduced.

### Claude Mythos — Watchlist Update: **ENISA gets access**

**The first substantive Mythos development in over a week.** On **September 10**, Anthropic granted **ENISA**, the EU's cybersecurity agency, access to **Mythos 5** — the first EU entity to receive it. EU Commission spokesperson **Thomas Regnier** confirmed: *"ENISA has been granted access to Mythos 5 and is testing it now."* Testing began the same day.

The delay is the story. Anthropic signalled in **April**, alongside the launch of **Project Glasswing**, that the bloc would get access; it took **more than three months** of negotiation, complicated by **US export controls on advanced AI technology**, to actually land. Glasswing began with roughly 40 organizations (Amazon, Apple, Microsoft, Google, the Linux Foundation, JP Morgan Chase, NVIDIA) and expanded in **June** to ~150 organizations across 15+ countries, adding power, water, healthcare, communications and hardware operators.

No new model: **Mythos 5.1** (September 1) remains current and restricted to vetted cyberdefence and life-sciences organizations, with **Fable 5.1** generally available. What changed is *who* can run it — and the fact that a sovereign European agency had to negotiate for months through export-control friction to test a commercially available American model is the more durable signal here.

### Google — Gemini ships as a native Windows app

Announced **September 10**: a dedicated **Gemini desktop app for Windows 10 and 11**, invoked with **Alt + Space** from anywhere, free with a **$19.99 Pro tier**. It connects to Google Workspace — open, edit and act on Gmail, Drive and Docs files without switching windows — and Google's pitch is that it is "lightweight and quiet" and "runs without slowing down your PC."

The competitive read matters more than the feature list. Microsoft has had **system-level** AI in Windows since Copilot's 2023 debut, wired into the taskbar and the Windows key. Gemini arrives as a *downloaded app* with a keyboard shortcut and a tray icon — a real capability gap, and an unusually direct move onto Microsoft's desktop.

([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-app-now-on-windows/) · [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/google-releases-a-gemini-app-for-windows/) · [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/gemini-windows-app-os-integration-gap-2026/) · [gagadget](https://gagadget.com/en/725538-google-gemini-lands-on-windows-as-a-native-desktop-app/))

### OpenAI — ChatGPT for Financial Services, and a Data agent in ChatGPT Work

Two enterprise launches on **September 10** that yesterday's digest did not cover, both aimed squarely at work currently done by junior analysts.

**ChatGPT for Financial Services** is a tailored build of ChatGPT Work running on **GPT-6 Astra**, shaped with **Morgan Stanley and Evercore as design partners**. It ships with premium data built in — **Daloopa, PitchBook, LSEG News** — so firms don't wire up connectors first, and targets earnings analysis, valuation, LBO modeling, buyer screening and pitchbook prep, with role-based access, encryption and audit-log export.

The **Data agent** in ChatGPT Work connects to approved warehouses (**BigQuery, Snowflake, Databricks, Redshift, ClickHouse, MongoDB, Datadog**), investigates what changed in a business metric, and builds interactive dashboards it can push into **Power BI, Tableau, Omni, Oracle BI, Sigma and ThoughtSpot** — then share findings via Slack or email and take approved actions in connected tools.

Taken together with the Agents API, OpenAI shipped the harness, the analyst and the vertical in a single week.

## Key Opinions & Statements

### Frontier researchers on the Dwarkesh Podcast — "drop-in remote workers" in one to three years

Published within the last day: **John Schulman** (chief scientist at Thinking Machines, OpenAI co-founder, led the RLHF work behind ChatGPT), **Beren Millidge** (CTO, Zyphra) and **Charlie O'Neill** (head of model training, Baseten) debating how close recursive self-improvement actually is.

The rapid-fire timeline the panel converged on: **drop-in AI remote workers one to three years out**, **10× researcher uplift at roughly two years**, and **beating top human experts somewhere between three and ten years**.

The more interesting half is the bear case, which is **entirely technical rather than political**: Moravec's paradox, the **absence of continual learning** in current systems, and a persistent **sim-to-real gap** that blocks generalization. None of the three cited regulation, compute access or public backlash as the thing most likely to stall the loop.

Why it matters: this is the same question Altman's slowdown remarks and the Hawley probe circle from the outside, answered from the inside by people who train the models — and the answer is that the binding constraint is architecture, not governance. Note these are researchers at "openish" labs, not the frontier labs themselves; treat the timelines as informed estimates, not institutional positions.

([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/john-beren-charlie) · [24/7 Wall St. summary](https://247wallst.com/cards/frontier-researchers-including-thinking-machines-chief-scie-podcast-episode-01m28tfkxddqesqm15bewjake6))

### Oracle's AI backlog hits $664B — and the funding question gets sharper

Oracle's **Q1 FY2027** results, reported **September 10** and digested by markets through **September 11**: **remaining performance obligations of $664 billion** after **$30B+** in new AI cloud contracts closed in the quarter. **OCI revenue up 121% to $7.4B**, total revenue **$19.3B (+30%)**, **GPU utilization at 97.9%**, and **300,000+ GPUs** delivered to AI cloud customers — nearly triple the prior quarter. Shares rose ~7%, pulling **CoreWeave and Nebius up ~4%** with them.

Two details matter more than the headline. First, the **$664B backlog is roughly ten years of Oracle's current annual revenue** — a number that only converts if GPU capacity lands on schedule. Second, and more interesting: much of the RPO growth came from contracts where **customers either prepaid for GPUs or supplied the hardware themselves**, meaning Oracle books the backlog without fronting the cash, even against projected peak capex of **$90–95B**. That structure is the market's answer to the "who funds the buildout" question — increasingly, the customer does.

([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/11/oracle-surges-7-as-ai-cloud-backlog-hits-664b-coreweave-and-nebius-climb-4/) · [24/7 Wall St. — bear case](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/11/oracle-weakens-bear-case-with-broader-ai-customer-base-and-664-billion-backlog/) · [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/327307/20260911/oracle-cloud-revenue-doubles-ai-demand-pushes-gpu-utilization-979.htm) · [ERP Today](https://erp.today/oracle-q4-2026-earnings-ai-cloud-backlog-funding/))

### China's labs stay silent on Anthropic's distillation allegations

Two days after Anthropic's threat report attributed **151 million Claude exchanges** to Alibaba and named Moonshot, DeepSeek, Xiaomi and Zhipu, **none of the accused companies has responded on the record** — not to CNBC, Business Insider, or SCMP. The only official Chinese response came from the **Commerce Ministry on September 9**, and it answered a separate US advisory rather than Anthropic: the accusations are "groundless," and the US is pursuing a "monopoly of the AI industry."

Worth keeping straight for the record: Anthropic's figures are its own attributions, the underlying evidence has not been published, and an absence of denial is not a confirmation. This is the third distinct distillation accusation Anthropic has made against Chinese labs in 2026 (February, June, September), each larger than the last, and none yet tested anywhere but in a company report.
